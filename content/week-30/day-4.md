## Revelation

One of the most extraordinary gifts God has given to human beings is free will. The capacity to choose — to say yes or no, to turn toward or turn away, to obey or to refuse. It is the gift that makes love possible, because love cannot be coerced. A programmed response is not love. God could have made human beings who always obeyed, always worshipped, always did exactly what He wanted — but those creatures would not have been lovers. They would have been robots. And God did not want robots. He wanted children. He wanted the specific, irreplaceable thing that only happens when a being with the genuine capacity to refuse chooses instead to say yes.

This is why God will not make you do something. He will not override your will, even when what you are about to choose is not what He wants for you. He will speak. He will warn. He will convict through the Holy Spirit and counsel through His Word and bring circumstances to bear on the direction you are heading. But He will abide by your personal will. The sovereignty of God and the free will of the human person coexist in a tension that no theological system has ever fully resolved — and both are real.

But here is the thing that the modern world has distorted into something dangerous: free will is a gift, not a justification. The capacity to choose does not make every choice equally valid. The freedom to say yes or no does not mean that every yes and every no has equivalent consequences. God allows you to experience the fruit of your decisions — and the fruit of choosing sin is not a neutral experience. It is destructive — to you personally, to the people around you, to the generations who come after you. The freedom was given to enable love, not to legitimise whatever the desire of the moment happens to be.

Jesus warned that in the last days, because of the increase of wickedness, the love of most will grow cold (Matthew 24:12). This is not a prediction about the world outside the church — it is a word about the community of faith. The love growing cold is the love of those who began in relationship with God and drifted — not through dramatic apostasy but through the gradual, incremental, decision-by-decision abuse of the free will that was given them. A little compromise here. A little tolerance of what used to convict them there. A slow accommodation to the surrounding culture's definition of what is acceptable — until the thing they once called sin they now call preference, and the standard they once upheld they now call legalism, and the love that once burned with the heat of genuine encounter with God has settled into the comfortable warmth of a fire that has been burning on insufficient fuel for too long.

Stop listening to voices — even voices from pulpits — that tell you it is acceptable to live in ways that the Scripture clearly names as sin. Stop embracing the cultural logic that says as long as it is not hurting anyone, it is fine. The standard is not whether it hurts anyone visible. The standard is whether it honours the God to whom you belong and who paid the price that bought you.

Joshua put the choice before Israel with the clarity that free will requires: choose this day whom you will serve. Not eventually. Not when the culture makes it easier. This day. The gift of free will means that the choice is yours — and it means that you are responsible for it.

If we love Him, we will obey Him. That is not a condition for His love — His love is not conditioned on your obedience. But it is the natural, inevitable expression of yours. Use the gift wisely. Choose the obedience that the love produces. And stay close enough to Jesus and His Word that when the world tells you to use your freedom to do whatever you want, you know with clarity that what you want — genuinely, deeply, in the part of you that is most truly yourself — is to honour the One who gave you the freedom in the first place.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the gift of free will with the seriousness it deserves — not as the license to do whatever I want, but as the capacity to choose what I actually want when I am most fully alive to who You are and who I am in You. I confess the specific places where I have been using the freedom as justification rather than as the gift it was always meant to be. Give me the Joshua clarity today: choose this day whom you will serve. I choose You. Not because I have to — but because You are worth choosing, and because the fruit of choosing You is the only fruit worth having.

## Daily Practices

- Read Matthew 24:1–14 in full — the complete context of the verse about love growing cold. Note that verse 12 comes in the middle of Jesus's description of the last days — and that the growing cold of love is presented as a consequence of the increase of wickedness, not an independent phenomenon. Ask: in what specific ways has the surrounding culture been incrementally reducing the temperature of my love for God and for His standards?
- Read Deuteronomy 30:15–20 — I have set before you life and death, blessings and curses. Now choose life. Note that the choice of life is not merely the choice of eternal salvation but the daily choice of the way of obedience. Ask: what is the specific choice between life and death that I am facing today — and what does choosing life look like in that specific situation?
- Journal: "Where have I been using my free will to accommodate what God has called sin — rationalising it, minimising it, giving it a more acceptable name? What would it look like to use my free will today in the direction it was designed for: to choose obedience, to choose love, to choose the life that God set before me?" Write the honest audit.
- Read John 14:15–21 — if you love me, keep my commands. And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate to help you and be with you forever. Note the connection: the commandment-keeping is not the condition for the Spirit but the expression of the love that the Spirit produces. Ask: how does the presence of the Holy Spirit within me change my capacity to keep the commands that love requires?
