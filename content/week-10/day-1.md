## Revelation

There is a garden at the foot of the Mount of Olives called Gethsemane, which means oil press. It is a fitting name for what happened there the night before the crucifixion — because what took place in that garden was the pressing of a human soul under the full weight of what God was asking of it. Jesus knelt on the ground and prayed the most honest prayer in all of Scripture: Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me. He did not perform acceptance. He did not manufacture serenity He did not feel. He asked, with His whole heart, for a different way. And then He said the words that changed the course of all of history: yet not my will, but yours be done.

This is submission. And it is nothing like what the word has come to mean in the popular imagination — which is passivity, resignation, the hollow compliance of someone who has given up caring about the outcome. Biblical submission is none of those things. It is the act of a person who cares deeply, who has felt the full weight of the cost, who has genuinely asked for another way — and who then, from a position of trust rather than defeat, places the outcome in the hands of a Father they believe is more trustworthy than their own preferences. Christ submitted not because He had no power to refuse. He submitted because He trusted the One who asked. That is the only submission that means anything.

Philippians 2:6–8 gives us the full architecture of this moment. Christ was in very nature God — He had equality with the Father as an ontological reality, not an aspiration. And He did not consider that equality something to be grasped or leveraged. He emptied Himself. He took the form of a servant. He became obedient to death — even death on a cross. The downward movement of the incarnation and the cross was not a defeat. It was the most deliberate, powerful, freely chosen act in the history of the universe. And James 4:7 draws the application for your own life: submit yourselves to God. The same posture that Christ inhabited in Gethsemane is the posture He is inviting you into. Not as a crushing of your will but as the alignment of it with a will that is infinitely wiser, infinitely more loving, and infinitely more capable of producing what you actually need.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You this morning in the posture of Gethsemane. Not with a performance of acceptance I do not feel, but with the honesty of a person who has a will of their own and is choosing, deliberately and trustingly, to place it under Yours. There are things I want. There are outcomes I have prayed for. There are cups I would very much like to have removed. I bring all of it to You now — honestly, without pretending the cost is small.

And then I say what Christ said: yet not my will, but Yours be done. Not because I do not care about the outcome. Because I trust You more than I trust my own assessment of what the outcome should be. You see what I cannot see. You know what I do not know. You are working toward a good that my current circumstances cannot yet reveal.

Teach me the submission of Gethsemane. Not the passivity of someone who has stopped caring, but the trust of someone who has placed their deepest concerns in the hands of a Father who can be trusted with them completely.

## Daily Practices

- Read Luke 22:39–46 in full — the complete Gethsemane account. Note that Jesus prayed the same prayer three times. Submission was not achieved in a single moment of spiritual heroism. It was arrived at through repeated, honest, costly engagement with the will of God. Let that normalise your own wrestling.
- Journal: "What is the cup in my own life right now that I am asking God to remove? Have I genuinely brought it to Him honestly, or have I been performing acceptance while privately holding onto my own preferred outcome?" Write the honest version.
- Read Philippians 2:1–11 in full. Paul presents Christ's submission not as a theological curiosity but as a model for how believers should relate to one another. Ask: where in my relationships is God calling me to the same downward, self-emptying movement Christ demonstrated?
- Spend 10 minutes praying the prayer of Gethsemane over the specific situation you named above. Say what you want — honestly. Then say: yet not my will, but Yours be done. Let it be a genuine act of trust rather than a formula.
