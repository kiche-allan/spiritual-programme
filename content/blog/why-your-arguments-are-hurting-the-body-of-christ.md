When someone walks away from fellowship citing weariness with religiosity, the reaction within our walls often exposes the condition of our own hearts more than it exposes theirs. The departure is met not with quiet intercession but with whisper campaigns, corridor verdicts, and the bitter labelling of the departed as fallen. In our zeal to defend the standard;  particularly within a fellowship deeply committed to repentance, holiness, and the fear of the Lord; we risk trading the fragrance of Christ for the poison of contention.

Murmuring and gossip do not preserve righteousness. They actively tear down the altar.

## We Have Forgotten Who the Real Enemy Is

Paul warned the Galatians directly:

*"If you bite and devour each other, watch out or you will be destroyed by each other."*
— Galatians 5:15, NIV

When believers spend their energy analysing another person's spiritual standing, picking apart motives, and defending their position with harsh rhetoric, they have forgotten who the real adversary is. The enemy does not need to attack the church from outside when he can get the church to attack itself from within. Division within the body grieves the Holy Spirit. It turns a place meant for healing and repentance into a courtroom of human pride.

The Scripture does not give us the corridor conversation as a legitimate response to a departing brother or sister. It gives us Matthew 18:15; go to them first, alone, directly. And Galatians 6:1; restore gently, watching yourself, carrying the burden. These are not optional. They are the only Spirit-sanctioned responses to the situation. Everything outside of them is flesh.

## The Spiritually Deep Have No Appetite for This

A believer truly rooted in secret-place prayer and Scripture simply lacks the appetite for these arguments. This is not coincidence. It is the direct result of what sustained time in God's presence produces.

**True spiritual depth produces silence over slander.** The believer who spends genuine time in fellowship with God understands that words carry spiritual weight. Gossip starves in an atmosphere of prayer; because the person who has been in God's presence cannot easily leave it to spend the next hour in the corridor.

**Maturity protects the unity of the body.** Ephesians 4:3 commands us to make every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace. The person walking closely with the Lord recognises that attacking another part of the body harms the whole; including the part doing the attacking.

**Intercession replaces accusation.** The carnal instinct attacks. The spiritual instinct prays. When someone stumbles or leaves disillusioned, the biblical mandate is restoration and sorrow; never vindication, never gossip.

Ephesians 4:30 is unambiguous: do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God. The verse sits immediately after the instruction about unwholesome talk. The connection is deliberate. The corridor verdict, the WhatsApp discussion, the prayer meeting that spends more time assessing the departed than addressing the throne — all of it grieves the Person of the Spirit who was building what the gossip is dismantling.

## A Mirror, Not a Verdict

The person who left citing religiosity may be wrong about some things. But the gossip that followed their departure is not the defence of the standard. It is the evidence that the standard has not yet been fully applied to the one area where it is most needed — the tongue.

Søren Kierkegaard observed that the hardest thing in Christendom is not defending religious doctrine but actually living it without hypocrisy. When our righteousness becomes weaponised against people rather than aimed at personal transformation, it ceases to be holiness and becomes noise. The community that responds to a cry of religiosity with a corridor verdict has not demonstrated that the cry was wrong. It has confirmed that the cry had something to point at.

## What One Awakened Person Can Do

It only takes a consecrated few to alter the spiritual climate:

> *One man awake, awakens another,*
> *The second awakens his next-door brother.*
> *The three awake can rouse a town,*
> *By turning the whole place upside down.*
> *The many awake can cause such a fuss,*
> *It finally awakens the rest of us.*

Awakening does not begin with heated debate or self-righteous post-mortems over who left and why. It begins when the remaining body wakes up to its primary calling — personal repentance, earnest prayer, and a radical love that seeks to restore rather than destroy. The awakened believer is not the one with the loudest opinion about who has fallen. They are the one who went to that person first, alone, quietly, and said: I miss you. Tell me what happened.

## Speak the Truth in Love

Ephesians 4:15 is often reduced to a permission slip for bluntness: speak the truth. But the verse does not end there. Speak the truth in love, Paul says, so that the body grows up into Christ.

The measure of a Christian argument is not only whether it is factually defensible. It is also whether it helps the body grow toward its Head. Does it produce greater faithfulness, repentance, clarity, and care? Or does it harden people into camps, make reconciliation harder, and leave ordinary believers anxious about speaking at all?

## Before You Reply

Before the next difficult conversation, take a moment to examine what is happening underneath your certainty. Ask God whether you want the other person restored or merely defeated. Ask whether you have listened long enough to describe their view fairly. Ask whether your words would sound different if you remembered that Christ loves them with the same costly love He has shown you.
The body of Christ is already under enough pressure from a world that does not understand its hope. It does not need us to make one another bleed in order to prove our convictions. The cross has settled the deepest question about how God handles people who are wrong: He moves toward them in costly love. That is how we are called to move toward one another.
