## Revelation

The grace established on Monday produces something specific and stable: identity. Not an identity earned through achievement, appearance, or the approval of others, but an identity that is settled in Christ before any of that enters the picture. 2 Corinthians 5:17 states it as an accomplished fact, not an aspiration: if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come. Not is coming eventually, pending sufficient spiritual progress. Has come. The old has gone, the new is here. This is the present-tense reality of every believer, regardless of how much that reality has been internalised or how loudly the old, false identities continue to clamour for attention.

Most people construct their sense of identity from external sources — what they produce, what others say about them, how their life compares to the lives around them. This is an unstable foundation, because all three of these sources change constantly. Production has good seasons and bad ones. Other people's opinions shift. Comparison always finds someone doing better in some category. An identity built on any of these will be perpetually anxious, because the foundation itself is never settled. Ephesians 2:10 offers something entirely different: we are God's handiwork — the Greek word is poiema, the root of our word poem, suggesting something crafted with intention and artistry — created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do.

Notice the order here, which mirrors the order established on Monday: identity comes first — God's handiwork, made in Christ — and purpose follows from identity, not the other way around. You are not valuable because of the good works you produce. You produce good works, prepared in advance, because you are already valuable — already a new creation, already God's handiwork. This reordering changes everything about how purpose is pursued. It is no longer a search for significance through achievement. It is the outworking of a significance that has already been settled.

Colossians 3:12 names the identity directly: God's chosen people, holy and dearly loved. Chosen. Holy. Dearly loved. These three words describe a settled status, not a performance review. And the call that follows — to clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience — is the natural clothing of someone who already knows who they are, not the effort of someone still trying to prove it.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the truth that my identity is not something I am still constructing through performance, comparison, or the approval of others. I am Your handiwork — made new in Christ, chosen, holy, dearly loved. This was settled before I did anything to deserve it, and it remains settled regardless of how this particular day unfolds.

Let my pursuit of purpose today flow from this identity rather than substitute for it. I do not need to achieve in order to become valuable. I am already valuable, and the good works prepared for me today are simply the natural outworking of who I already am in You.

Where I have been seeking identity in what I produce or how I compare to others, redirect my gaze back to what is already true: I am in Christ. The old has gone. The new is here.

## Daily Practices

- Read 2 Corinthians 5:16–21 in full. Note that the new creation language is immediately followed by the language of reconciliation and ambassadorship in verses 18–20. Identity in Christ is not private — it carries a purpose that flows outward.
- Journal: "Where have I been constructing my sense of identity from sources other than Christ — performance, comparison, or others' approval? What would change if I genuinely believed 2 Corinthians 5:17 is already true of me?"
- Read Ephesians 2:1–10 again, this time focusing specifically on verse 10. Note the phrase prepared in advance. Ask: what good works might God have already prepared for today, that I have not yet recognised or stepped into?
- Speak Colossians 3:12 over yourself today as a declaration: I am chosen, holy, and dearly loved. Let this be the lens through which you interpret today's events, rather than letting today's events determine how you feel about your identity.
