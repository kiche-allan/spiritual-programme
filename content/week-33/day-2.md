## Revelation

What do you do when the rains fail?

Habakkuk lived in a nation on the edge. He'd just heard that a brutal army was coming, and he said his heart pounded, his lips quivered and his legs gave way beneath him. There's nothing neat about his fear. He didn't pretend things were fine.

Then he describes the worst case for a farming people. The fig tree doesn't blossom. The vines give no grapes. The olive crop fails, the fields produce no food, the pens are empty of sheep and the stalls empty of cattle. Think of a farmer standing in a shamba where the maize has dried up for the second season running. Think of a trader whose stock has been lost, or a family where the salary has stopped. No harvest. No income. No plan B.

And then comes my favourite word in the passage: "yet." Though all of that is true, yet I will rejoice in the Lord. He doesn't say, "I feel happy." He says, "I will." Joy here is a decision made with open eyes. It isn't built on the harvest. It's built on the God of the harvest.

Job did the same. He lost his children, his livestock and his health in a single season, and he said, "The Lord gave and the Lord has taken away; may the name of the Lord be praised." Neither man was in denial. They were anchored.

This is the difference between gratitude that depends on circumstances and gratitude that depends on God. If I only thank Him when the barns are full, I'm thanking the barns. Habakkuk's joy survived an empty barn because it never lived there.

And look at where he ends up: "God, the Lord, is my strength; he makes my feet like the deer's; he makes me tread on my high places." A deer can leap across rocky ground without slipping. God doesn't always remove the mountain, but He gives you feet to climb it.

So what is your empty fig tree right now? Name it honestly before the Lord. Then say your own "yet": "Though ____, yet I will rejoice in the Lord." Say it out loud. Say it again tomorrow.

## Morning Prayer

Lord, You see the empty places in my life: the income that has not come, the answer that has not arrived, the season that has not turned. I will not pretend they don't hurt. But I choose to say yet. Yet I will rejoice in You, the God of my salvation. Be my strength, and give me feet to climb.

## Daily Practices

- Read all of Habakkuk 3, especially verses 16–19. Notice that the fear and the joy sit side by side.
- Journal: "What is my empty fig tree right now?" Finish the sentence: "Though ______, yet I will rejoice in the Lord because ______."
- Say your "yet" sentence aloud at the start and the end of today.
