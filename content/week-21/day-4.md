## Revelation

2 Corinthians 5:17 contains the most radical identity claim available to a human being — and it is a claim that has been so thoroughly domesticated by the church's tendency to reduce it to moral transformation that its actual content has been largely lost. Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come. The old has gone, the new is here. This is not a statement about behaviour modification. This is not a description of someone who has stopped some bad habits and developed some good ones. This is an ontological declaration — a statement about what has genuinely happened at the level of being to the person who is in Christ.

The word new in the Greek is kainos — which means new in kind, not merely new in time. A new version of the old thing would be neos — a younger or more recent version of the same category. Kainos describes something that is new in its essential nature — a different category from what preceded it. Paul is not saying you are a reformed version of what you were. He is saying you are a different kind of thing from what you were. The new creation that has come in Christ is not a renovation. It is a new creation in the same sense that Genesis 1 was a new creation — something that did not exist before is now existing, brought into being by an act of the same God who spoke light into the void.

The old has gone. Not is going, not is fading, not is gradually being replaced by the new. Has gone — the aorist tense in Greek indicating a completed action. The old has already passed. At the moment of genuine conversion, the person who was under the dominion of sin and death — who was, as Ephesians 2 says, dead in trespasses and sins, following the course of this world, living according to the passions of the flesh — that person died. Not reformed. Died. And what is alive is not that person improved. It is a new creation that the death made possible.

Galatians 2:20 gives us the personal, first-person testimony of what this new creation feels like from the inside — or rather, what the old felt like and what the new feels like in contrast. I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. The crucifixion is in the perfect tense — a past event with ongoing consequences. The person who was crucified is not living anymore. What is living — what is occupying the space where the old life used to be — is Christ Himself. Not an improved version of Paul. Not Paul with better values and stronger willpower. Christ, living in the person who has been emptied of the old life that previously occupied the space.

Colossians 3:3–4 adds the dimension that most profoundly changes how the new creation identity is held in the practical circumstances of daily life: your life is now hidden with Christ in God. The true life of the new creation is not visible on the surface — not in the circumstances, not in the feelings, not in the day-to-day experiences that can so easily be read as the evidence of a life that has not been transformed. The true life is hidden. It is secure in a location that no circumstance can reach — with Christ, in God — and it will only be fully revealed when Christ appears. Then you also will appear with him in glory. The full manifestation of what the new creation actually is has not yet occurred. It is held in reserve, hidden in the safest possible location, waiting for the revelation that the return of Christ will bring.

Living from the new creation identity means learning to hold the gap between what is currently visible and what is ontologically true. The feelings that insist you are the same person you always were. The patterns that resurface from the old life despite the death that was supposed to have occurred. The circumstances that do not yet reflect the new creation reality. None of these are the evidence that the new creation has not happened. They are the evidence that the full manifestation of what has happened is still hidden — still being worked out, still being expressed through a body and a mind and a set of habits that have not yet fully caught up with the ontological transformation that the new creation represents. The new creation is a fact. The experience of the new creation is a process. And the gap between the fact and the experience is where the faith of the Christian life is most consistently exercised.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the most radical thing the gospel says about me — that I am a new creation. Not a reformed version of what I was. Not an improved edition. A new creation, in the kainos sense — a different kind of thing from what existed before, brought into being by the same creative act that spoke light into the void at the beginning. The old has gone. The new is here. Let me live today from the reality of what I actually am rather than from the echo of what I used to be.

Where the old patterns resurface, let me hold the gap between what is currently visible and what is ontologically true. My life is hidden with Christ in God. The full manifestation of what I am has not yet appeared. But it is secure. It is real. And it will be revealed in glory when the time comes.

I no longer live. Christ lives in me. Let the day reflect that.

## Daily Practices

- Read 2 Corinthians 5:14–21 in full — the full context of the new creation declaration. Note that Paul moves immediately from the new creation to the ministry of reconciliation in verses 18–20 — the new creation is not only a personal identity. It carries a mission. The person who has become a new creation in Christ has been given the ministry of reconciliation. Ask: how does the new creation identity produce the ministry of reconciliation? What is the connection between what you have become and what you are called to do?
- Read Colossians 3:1–17 in full — Paul's practical application of the new creation identity to the ordinary circumstances of daily life. Note the structure: since you have been raised with Christ, set your minds on things above, put to death what belongs to your earthly nature, put on the new self. Ask: what does it mean to put to death what belongs to the earthly nature, when that nature has already been crucified with Christ? What is the relationship between the completed event and the ongoing practice?
- Journal: "What is the specific area of my life where the gap between the new creation identity and the current visible reality is most pronounced — where the old patterns are most persistent, where the new feels most theoretical? What would it look like to live from the ontological fact of what I am rather than the experiential evidence of what the day has produced?"
- Read Romans 6:1–14 — the theology of co-crucifixion and co-resurrection with Christ. Note verse 11: count yourselves dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus. The counting is an act of faith — the deliberate reckoning of what is spiritually true as the basis for how you live practically. Practise the counting today: in the specific area of old-pattern temptation, count yourself dead to it and alive to God in Christ Jesus. Let the counting precede the feeling.
