## Revelation

The week began with hell — the eternal consequence of rejecting the rescue God offers. It is right that we arrive at salvation today, because salvation is the direct answer to everything the first two days described. But salvation needs to be understood in its full dimensions — not merely as the avoidance of hell, not merely as the forgiveness of past sins, not merely as a transaction that secures a future destination. Salvation is the comprehensive rescue of the whole person — past forgiven, present transformed, future secured — and it is designed to produce a specific kind of life in the here and now.

The theologians have given us three words that capture the full arc of salvation: justification, sanctification, and glorification. Justification is what happened the moment you believed — God declared you righteous on the basis of Christ's righteousness credited to your account. Not because you became righteous at that moment, but because the One who was perfectly righteous took your sin and gave you His standing before the Father. Romans 5:1 — therefore, since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. The war is over. The verdict has been rendered. You are not awaiting judgment — the judgment has already been made, in your favour, at the cross.

Sanctification is the ongoing work — the Spirit's continuous transformation of your desires, your character, your instincts, and your choices into increasing conformity with the image of Christ. This is not a second blessing or an advanced spiritual state available only to certain believers. It is the normal Christian life. It is what happens when the person who has been justified yields daily to the Spirit who was given to produce holiness from the inside out. It is not performance. It is not the accumulation of religious activity. It is the natural, Spirit-driven fruit of a life that is genuinely rooted in Christ — the same way an apple tree does not perform apples but produces them naturally from the life within it.

Glorification is the final act — the resurrection of the body, the perfection of the soul, the full and final completion of everything that justification began and sanctification continued. 1 John 3:2 — when He appears, we shall be like Him, for we shall see Him as He is. And the prospect of glorification is not merely a future hope — it is a present motivation. All who have this hope in Him purify themselves, just as He is pure. The call to holiness is not the condition of salvation. It is the response to it — the natural, joyful, Spirit-empowered expression of a person who has understood what they have been rescued into.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive salvation today in its full dimensions — not merely as a ticket out of hell, not merely as the forgiveness of my past, but as the comprehensive rescue of my whole person. I was justified at the moment of faith — declared righteous, at peace with You, no longer standing under judgment. I am being sanctified now — the Spirit is actively at work in me, transforming my desires and my character into the image of Christ. And I will be glorified — fully and finally complete, perfectly like Jesus, on the other side of this age.

Let the full architecture of what You have done for me produce the full response of a life genuinely transformed by it. Not a performance of holiness — the natural fruit of a person who has been rooted in Christ and is being continuously renewed by the Spirit. Not religious activity — genuine, daily, Spirit-empowered conformity to the image of the One who saved me.

I receive the grace that teaches. The grace that appeared in Christ Jesus does not merely forgive — it instructs. It teaches me to say no to ungodliness and yes to godliness. Let it teach me today.

## Daily Practices

- Read Romans 5:1–11 in full — Paul's description of what justification produces. Note the cascade: peace with God, access to grace, rejoicing in hope, character produced through suffering, hope that does not disappoint, love poured out by the Spirit. This is what salvation looks like when it is lived fully rather than merely possessed theoretically.
- Read Titus 2:11–14 and sit with the phrase the grace that teaches. Grace is not merely the forgiveness of sin. It is an active instructor — teaching you what to say no to and what to say yes to. Ask: is the grace I have received functioning as an instructor in my daily life, or have I received it without letting it teach me?
- Journal: "What is the difference between my understanding of salvation when I first believed and my understanding of it today? What dimensions of it have I discovered that I did not know at the beginning? What dimensions do I still need to understand more fully?"
- Read 1 Peter 1:13–25 — Peter's sustained argument for holiness as the response to salvation. Note that his appeal is not to duty or law but to what has been received: you were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ. The holiness flows from the redemption. The call to be holy is grounded entirely in what God has already done.
