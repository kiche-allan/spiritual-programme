## Revelation

How do you walk into a home in our culture?

You don't barge in and start making demands. You knock, you say "hodi," you greet the people properly, you ask how the home is, and only then do you say why you came. Skip the greetings and you'll be remembered for it.

Psalm 100 says the same about entering God's house: "Enter his gates with thanksgiving, and his courts with praise!" Thanksgiving is the greeting at the gate. Praise is how you walk through the courtyard. The psalm gives you the order: gates first, courts next, and then you stand before Him.

Too many of our prayers walk in with a shopping list. "Lord, I need this. Lord, fix that. Lord, where is my breakthrough?" Asking isn't wrong. Jesus told us to ask, and Paul says to make our requests known to God. But notice that Philippians 4:6 says to do it "with thanksgiving." The request goes in on the back of a thank-you.

Why does thanksgiving open the door? Because it changes who we're looking at. Complaint fixes my eyes on my problem. Thanksgiving lifts my eyes to the Giver. By the time I've listed what He has already done, I'm remembering who I'm talking to. My problems haven't shrunk, but God has become bigger in my sight.

The writer of Hebrews calls it "a sacrifice of praise—the fruit of lips that openly profess his name." A sacrifice costs something. Some days it's easy to thank God, and some days it costs everything you have. Those are the days it counts the most.

You don't need a church building or a worship team. You can enter His gates on a bus, in a kitchen, at a desk, or standing outside a hospital ward. The gate is open because Jesus opened it.

So before you pray for anything today, spend the first two minutes only thanking Him. Say it out loud. Name specific things: yesterday's meal, a person who helped you, a door that opened. Then ask. See how different the conversation feels.

## Morning Prayer

Lord, I come to Your gates today with thanksgiving. Before I ask for anything, I thank You for who You are and for what You have already done. Thank You for Jesus, who opened the way in. Lift my eyes from my problems to Your face, and let my lips offer You a sacrifice of praise.

## Amen

In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read Psalm 100 aloud. Mark every instruction: enter, give thanks, bless, serve, know.
- Journal: "When I pray, do I start with thanks or with requests?" Write out a prayer that begins with five specific thanks.
- Spend the first two minutes of every prayer today on thanksgiving alone, before you ask for anything.
