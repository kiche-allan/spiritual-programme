## Revelation

There is a specific temptation that comes in the middle of the story — the temptation to read the current chapter as the final one. To look at the present difficulty, the present limitation, the present distance between where you are and where you believed God was taking you, and conclude that what you are experiencing right now is as good as it gets. That the best is behind you. That the peak has already been passed. That the most significant moments of your life are already in the rear-view mirror.

This is the temptation that the enemy deploys most effectively in the middle season — the season between the promise and the fulfilment, between the calling and the arrival, between the word God spoke and the day it becomes visible reality. And it is effective precisely because it uses the evidence of the present moment against the promise of the future one.

Jeremiah 29:11 was spoken to people in exile — people who had lost the temple, lost the city, lost the land, lost almost everything that had represented the blessing of God in their visible experience. The word did not come to them in their prosperity. It came to them in Babylon. And the word was not a comfort that explained the exile or minimised its pain. It was a declaration about what was coming — plans to prosper you, plans to give you hope and a future. The best of what God had planned for Israel was not behind them in the Jerusalem they had lost. It was ahead of them in the return they had not yet seen.

1 Corinthians 2:9 gives us the most expansive statement of the forward orientation that faith requires: what no eye has seen, what no ear has heard, what no human mind has conceived — the things God has prepared for those who love Him. The category of what God has prepared is specifically the category of the inconceivable — the things that do not fit within the frame of reference that current experience provides. The best that is yet to come is not the best that you can currently imagine. It is beyond the imagination. It exceeds the frame.

This is the most important practical implication of the best is yet to come — it is not optimism about a future version of the life you currently have. It is the specific, revealed conviction that what God has prepared exceeds the categories of what you have experienced or imagined. The person who has experienced the greatest blessing of their life to date has not yet seen the ceiling of what God has prepared. The person who has experienced the deepest loss has not seen the end of what God can restore and exceed. The best is not a bigger version of the past. It is something that no eye has yet seen.

Live forward. The exile is not the ending. Babylon is not the destination. The plans God has for you include a future that the present chapter cannot contain.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I choose today to live forward — to orient my faith toward what You have prepared rather than what I have already experienced or lost. I receive Jeremiah 29:11 not as a comfort for the exile but as a declaration about the destination — plans to prosper me, plans to give me hope and a future. Let that future be more real to me today than the present difficulty that is trying to convince me the best is behind me.

What no eye has seen, what no ear has heard — that is what You have prepared. I cannot imagine it fully. But I can trust the One who prepared it. And I choose today to trust rather than to conclude that the current chapter is the final one.

The best is yet to come. Let me live like I believe it.

## Daily Practices

- Read Jeremiah 29:1–14 in full — the complete context of the famous verse. Note that it was written to exiles, that it included the instruction to build houses and plant gardens in Babylon, and that the future was seventy years away. Ask: what does it mean to live forward in faith when the fulfilment is further away than I would choose?
- Read Romans 8:18–25 — the present sufferings are not worth comparing with the glory that will be revealed. Note Paul's use of the word eagerly — the creation waits in eager expectation. Ask: what would it look like to wait for what God has prepared with eager expectation rather than resigned endurance?
- Journal: "What is the specific area of my life where I have been most tempted to conclude that the best is behind me — that I have already passed the peak? What does 1 Corinthians 2:9 say specifically about that area — what has no eye seen that God has prepared there?" Write the honest forward-looking exploration.
- Read Hebrews 11:13–16 — the great faith figures who died without receiving the promises, who were looking forward to a better country. They chose the forward orientation even when the promise was not fulfilled in their lifetime. Ask: how does the faith of these men and women reframe what it means to live forward when the fulfilment is not yet visible?
