## Revelation

When it comes to temptation and sin, there is a distinction that the Scripture insists on and that the church often fails to make clearly enough: being tempted is not sin. The temptation is the invitation. The sin is the response to the invitation. Jesus was tempted in every way just as we are — yet was without sin (Hebrews 4:15). The temptation arrived. He said no. And the no was not the product of the absence of genuine desire — it was the product of a will that was more committed to the Father than to the satisfaction of the desire.

Part of being human in a fallen world is facing temptation. It will come — in forms you anticipated and in forms that surprised you, at moments when your guard was up and at moments when you did not know your guard was down. The question is not whether the temptation arrives. The question is what you do when it does.

Romans 6:11 gives us the specific posture from which the no is possible: count yourselves dead to sin but alive to God in Christ Jesus. The word count — logizomai in the Greek — is an accounting term. It means to reckon, to calculate, to enter into the ledger as settled fact. Paul is not asking you to feel dead to sin — feelings are unreliable narrators of spiritual reality. He is asking you to reckon it as true, to treat it as the settled fact of your identity in Christ regardless of what the feelings are reporting, and then to act from that reckoning. Dead people do not respond to the invitations of the living. If you are dead to sin — if you have reckoned it as true that the power of sin over you was broken at the cross — then the invitation of sin does not have the same claim on you that it once did. You do not have to answer it.

But the reckoning must be followed by the refusing. Verse 13 — do not offer any part of yourself to sin as an instrument of wickedness. The language of offering is deliberate: sin does not take you by force. It waits for you to offer something — your time, your attention, your imagination, your presence in a specific place or a specific conversation or in front of a specific screen. The no is the refusal to offer what sin is waiting for you to hand over.

As Josh Billings observed, half the trouble of life can be traced to saying yes too quickly and not saying no soon enough. The no that needs to be said is almost always obvious in the moment before the temptation becomes acute — before the door is fully open, before the conversation has gone too far, before the situation has developed in the direction that makes the no cost more than it would have cost five minutes earlier. Say no early. Say it clearly. And say it in the power of the One who has already broken the reign of sin over your life.

The capacity to say no is not a matter of willpower alone — it is a matter of identity. The person who has genuinely reckoned themselves dead to sin and alive to God has a different relationship to temptation than the person who is fighting each battle from scratch on the basis of willpower. They are fighting from a position that has already been secured. Sin shall no longer be your master — not because you have finally become strong enough to defeat it, but because the One who has already defeated it lives in you.

Say no to the desire. Say yes to the God who is stronger than the desire. And say no early enough that the battle is won before it becomes the kind of battle that is hard to win.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with the specific temptation that I know is waiting for me — the one I can already see on the horizon of this day, the invitation that will arrive in a form I recognise. I reckon myself dead to it. Not because I feel nothing, but because You have declared it true in Christ and I choose to act from the declaration rather than the feeling. Give me the wisdom to say no early — before the conversation goes too far, before the situation develops past the point where the no costs more than it should. I am not fighting for a victory that is still uncertain. I am enforcing a victory that is already secured. Sin is not my master. You are. Let me live today from that settled fact.

## Daily Practices

- Read Romans 6:1–14 in full — the complete passage on dying to sin and living to God. Note that Paul grounds the whole argument in baptism — the public declaration of the death of the old self and the resurrection of the new. Ask: am I living from my baptismal identity — dead to sin, alive to God — or am I fighting each temptation as if the outcome is still uncertain?
- Read 1 Corinthians 10:12–13 — God is faithful; he will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear. But when you are tempted, he will also provide a way out. Note that the way out is provided — it exists before the temptation arrives. Ask: what is the specific way out that God has provided for the specific temptation I am currently most vulnerable to — and am I using it?
- Journal: "What is the specific invitation of sin I have been saying yes to — or have been leaving open long enough that the yes becomes easier? What is the specific no I need to say today, and what is the earliest possible moment to say it — before the situation develops past the point where it costs the most?" Write the honest answer and the specific plan.
- Read James 4:7–8 — submit yourselves to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you. Draw near to God and he will draw near to you. Note the sequence: submission to God precedes and enables the resistance of the devil. Ask: in the area where I am most vulnerable to temptation, how close am I currently walking to God — and is the vulnerability related to the distance?
