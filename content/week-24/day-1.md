## Revelation

Psalm 27:13 is one of the most honest verses in the entire Psalter — because of the two words that most translations include and that most readings pass over: I remain confident. The word remain implies resistance — the resistance of a person who is holding a position that is being pressed against, who is maintaining a conviction in the face of something that is pushing against it. David is not writing from a place of settled peace and comfortable confirmation. He is writing from a place of pressure — the specific, sustained pressure of a wait that has gone on long enough to make the remaining confident a deliberate act of will rather than a natural response to what the circumstances are producing.

I will see the goodness of the Lord in the land of the living. The land of the living is not heaven. It is not the resurrection. It is not the afterlife where everything that was wrong will be made right. It is here — the land in which the living are currently living, the present tense existence of a person who is still in the body, still in the wait, still in the circumstances that have not yet resolved. The confidence is not that he will eventually see the goodness of God in eternity — that would be less remarkable. The confidence is that he will see it here, now, before everything has been resolved, in the specific land he is currently inhabiting with its specific difficulties and its specific unanswered questions.

This confidence is what makes verse 14 possible: wait for the Lord; be strong and take heart and wait for the Lord. The double command to wait — repeated for emphasis, the same instruction given twice because the waiting is hard enough to require hearing it twice — is grounded in the confidence of verse 13. The waiting is not passive resignation. It is the active, strong, take-heart posture of a person who is remaining confident of something that has not yet appeared — holding the conviction that the goodness is coming, that the land of the living will be the location of the seeing, that the wait has an end and the end is the goodness of God.

Psalm 31:19 adds the dimension of the goodness that makes the waiting possible: how abundant is your goodness, which you have stored up for those who fear you, which you bestow in the sight of all, on those who take refuge in you. The goodness is stored up — not depleted, not rationed, not running low because the wait has been long. Stored up — the accumulation of a goodness that has been building in preparation for the moment of its bestowing, growing larger rather than smaller as the waiting continues, ready to be given in the sight of all on those who have been taking refuge in the One who stored it. The wait does not diminish the goodness that is coming. It accumulates it.

Nahum 1:7 gives us the most practically accessible statement of the goodness of God available in the prophetic literature: the Lord is good, a refuge in times of trouble. He cares for those who trust in Him. The goodness is not an abstraction to be contemplated from a theological distance. It is a refuge — a specific place to go when the trouble arrives, a shelter that is made of what God is rather than what the circumstances are, available to the person who is in the trouble and needs somewhere to be that is not the full exposed weight of the trouble itself. And He cares — the Hebrew verb yada, the word of intimate knowing — for those who trust in Him. The caring is personal. It is the specific attentiveness of a God who knows the person in the trouble by name, who has been present in every moment of the wait, who has not been indifferent to the cost of the remaining confident when the confidence has been hard.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I remain confident today — not because the evidence is overwhelming, not because the circumstances are confirming what I believe, but because Your character is the ground on which the confidence rests and Your character does not change with the circumstances. I will see Your goodness in the land of the living. Here. Before everything is resolved. In this specific land I am inhabiting with its specific difficulties and its specific unanswered questions.

I receive today the stored-up goodness of Psalm 31:19 — the goodness that has been accumulating, not depleting, through the length of the wait. And I take refuge in the goodness of Nahum 1:7 — the refuge that is made of who You are rather than what my situation is.

Wait for the Lord. Be strong and take heart. And wait for the Lord. I am doing all three today. Not because it is easy. Because You are good.

## Daily Practices

- Read Psalm 27 in full — all fourteen verses. Note the movement of the psalm: the confident declarations of verses 1–6, the urgent prayer of verses 7–12, and the resolve of verses 13–14. The confident declarations and the urgent prayer exist in the same psalm — the confidence and the need are not in contradiction. Ask: what does it mean to hold both the confident declaration of God's goodness and the urgent prayer for His intervention simultaneously?
- Read Lamentations 3:19–33 — the passage that moves from the bitterness of affliction to the declaration of God's faithfulness. Note verse 21: yet this I call to mind and therefore I have hope. The confidence is not produced by the circumstances. It is produced by the deliberate, intentional calling to mind of what is true about God regardless of what the circumstances are producing. Ask: what do I need to deliberately call to mind today about the goodness of God in order to remain confident of it?
- Journal: "I will see the goodness of the Lord in the land of the living. What would it mean for me to receive this as a present-tense confidence rather than a future-tense hope — to be genuinely confident that the goodness will be seen here, in this land, in this season, before everything has resolved? What is making the remaining confident most difficult right now, and what is the specific truth about God's character that most directly answers that difficulty?" Write the honest engagement.
- Read Romans 8:28–32 — and conclude with verse 32: He who did not spare His own Son, but gave Him up for us all — how will He not also, along with Him, graciously give us all things? The cross is the proof of the goodness that cannot be disproved by any subsequent circumstance. Ask: how does the cross function as the evidence of God's goodness that the current difficulty cannot override?
