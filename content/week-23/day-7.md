## Revelation

2 Corinthians 1:20 contains one of the most important and most overlooked theological statements in the entire Pauline corpus: for no matter how many promises God has made, they are yes in Christ. And so through Him the amen is spoken by us to the glory of God. The promises of God are not yes and no — conditional, tentative, subject to revision based on human performance. They are yes — in Christ, permanently, unilaterally, unconditionally. And the amen is the human response to the divine yes — the agreement, the acceptance, the wholehearted affirmation of the person who has received the yes and is now saying yes back.

The Great Amen is not the word spoken at the end of a prayer. It is the life that says yes to everything God has declared — with the whole of the life rather than merely with the confessional statement. The Beatitudes are true. Amen — therefore I will inhabit the posture of the poor in spirit rather than performing the confidence the world requires. The secret place is real. Amen — therefore I will close the door and pray rather than managing my religious appearance for a public audience. The idols must be put to death. Amen — therefore I will do the honest inventory of what is functioning as god in my interior life and bring it to the judgment of the first commandment. The resurrection body is coming. Amen — therefore I will treat my current body with the respect due to the seed of something glorious and hold my physical limitations with the perspective of someone who knows what they are the seed of. The disciplines are the training. Amen — therefore I will gymnazo rather than merely aspire. The church is the body. Amen — therefore I will belong rather than merely attend.

Romans 12:1–2 gives us the most comprehensive single description of what the Great Amen looks like in practice: offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God — this is your true and proper worship. Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. The living sacrifice is the opposite of the easy life — it is the total, whole-bodied, continuously offered presentation of the entire person to God as the appropriate response to the mercy that has been received. Not occasional moments of spiritual intensity. A living sacrifice — the sustained, daily, renewed presentation of what is alive on the altar of what God is doing. The not conforming is the active resistance of the gravitational pull of the world's value system — the system that blesses the proud in spirit, the comfortable, the powerful, the self-serving, the idolised. And the being transformed by the renewing of the mind is the ongoing, Spirit-worked change of the interior framework that determines what the life expresses.

Joshua 24:15 gives us the archetypal declaration of the Great Amen in the Old Testament — spoken by Joshua at the end of his life, at the covenant renewal at Shechem, to a people who had been presented with the choice between the gods their ancestors had served and the God who had brought them out of Egypt and through the wilderness and into the Promised Land. Choose this day whom you will serve. And then Joshua's personal declaration: but as for me and my household, we will serve the Lord. The as for me is the individual, personal, non-delegated decision of a man who has decided that the amen to God's yes is not a collective cultural choice that he makes by default because of the community he lives in. It is his personal, deliberate, renewed, specific declaration — made on the day of choice, in the presence of the whole assembly, with the full awareness of what the choice will cost and what the alternatives are.

The week has offered seven truths — the upside-down kingdom, the secret place, the idols to be dethroned, the resurrection body coming, the disciplines to be trained in, the church to be inhabited. The Great Amen is the declaration that says yes to all of them — not in the moment of spiritual intensity but in the ordinary day that follows the declaration. The amen is spoken, as 2 Corinthians 1:20 says, to the glory of God. The life that lives the amen is the life that glorifies the God whose promises were yes before the amen was spoken. And the amen is most glorifying when it is spoken in the ordinary Tuesday — the day when no one is watching and nothing dramatic is happening and the choice between conforming and being transformed is made in the small, unglamorous, unwitnessed decisions that constitute the actual texture of a life.

Choose this day. As for me and my household. We will serve the Lord. Amen.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week with the Great Amen — the wholehearted, whole-bodied, whole-life yes to everything You have declared in Christ. Not the amen of a confessional statement that leaves the life unchanged. The amen of a living sacrifice — offered daily, renewed continuously, expressing in every ordinary decision the yes that Your promises deserve and that Your mercy has purchased.

I say amen today to the upside-down kingdom — let me inhabit the posture of the poor in spirit. I say amen to the secret place — let me close the door and pray. I say amen to the dethroning of the idols — let me bring what has been functioning as god to the judgment of the first commandment. I say amen to the resurrection body — let me hold my current limitations with the perspective of the seed. I say amen to the training — let me gymnazo rather than merely aspire. I say amen to the body — let me belong rather than merely attend.

As for me and my household — we will serve the Lord. Amen. And amen.

## Amen

In Jesus' name — and with the full amen of the whole life — Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read Joshua 24:1–27 in full — the covenant renewal at Shechem, the review of God's faithfulness across the entire history of Israel, and the challenge to choose this day whom you will serve. Note the specific moment when the people respond: we will serve the Lord our God and obey him. And Joshua's response: you are witnesses against yourselves that you have chosen to serve the Lord. The choice is witnessed, recorded, and holds. Ask: what would it mean for you to make the choose this day declaration today — witnessed, recorded, holding — about the specific areas of your life that the week has addressed?
- Review the entire week — the Beatitudes, the secret place, idolatry, the resurrection body, the disciplines, the church, and the Great Amen — and write one sentence for each day that begins with Amen: "Amen to the upside-down kingdom — therefore I will..." Seven amen sentences. Let them be specific rather than general, practical rather than aspirational, and honest rather than impressive.
- Journal: "Which of the seven themes of this week produced the most resistance in me — the one I found hardest to say amen to, the one that most directly challenges the comfortable version of the Christian life I have been maintaining? What does the resistance tell me about where the amen is most needed?" Write the honest exploration.
- Read 2 Corinthians 1:18–22 in full — the full context of the yes and amen passage. Note verse 22: He has set his seal of ownership on us and put his Spirit in our hearts as a deposit, guaranteeing what is to come. The amen is possible because the Spirit has been given as the deposit of everything that has been promised. The yes of God's promises and the amen of the human response are both sustained by the same Spirit. Ask: how does knowing that the Spirit has been given as the guarantee of the promises change how you say amen to them today?
