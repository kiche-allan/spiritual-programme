## Revelation

How do you feel about belonging to someone?

The very idea runs against the grain of everything the modern world prizes. Independence. Autonomy. The right to define yourself on your own terms and to make your own decisions without reference to any authority outside yourself. The culture tells you that freedom means belonging to no one — that the highest form of human existence is the self-directed life, answerable to no one, shaped by no one, owned by no one.

And then Paul writes: you are not your own. You were bought at a price.

When Paul wrote to the Thessalonians about living to please God, he used a picture from the Greek military — a command being passed down through the ranks, each soldier expected to operate as one, following the same orders, moving in the same direction, with no room for independent agendas or personal preference when those agendas conflicted with the mission. The Greek word he used was parangelia — a military order transmitted through the chain of command. The life of the believer, Paul is saying, has the structure of a military engagement: there is a Commander, there are orders, and the life that honours God is the life that has come to terms with both.

The word Paul used for himself was doulos — bondservant, slave. A doulos in the first-century world had no independent legal standing, no personal agenda, no rights that existed independently of the master. Everything the doulos had came from and returned to the master. And Paul used this word not as a lament but as a title — the opening of his letter to the Romans is Paul, a doulos of Christ Jesus (Romans 1:1). It is the first thing he tells you about himself. Before apostle, before missionary, before theologian — slave of Christ Jesus.

The instinctive reaction is resistance. We prize our independence. We want to call our own shots, live by our own terms, chart our own course. The idea of belonging to someone — of having your identity and your direction defined by another — feels like a reduction of the self, a diminishment of what it means to be human.

But here is the discovery that every genuine doulos makes sooner or later: belonging to Jesus is the most freeing thing in the world.

When you belong to Him you are finally free from the exhausting, endless performance of trying to earn everyone else's approval. You are free from the tyranny of other people's opinions — because the only opinion that ultimately governs your life is His. You are free from the paralysis of having to figure out your own purpose — because the One who bought you has already established it. You are free from the anxiety of self-construction — because you are not building yourself. You are being built by the One who knows what He is making.

And then there is the specific, extraordinary reality that 1 Corinthians 6:19 makes explicit: your body — the specific physical body you inhabit right now — is the temple of the Holy Spirit. Not a symbol of it. Not a metaphor for it. The actual dwelling place of the third person of the Trinity. He actually resides in you. He reveals sin with a specificity that no external standard could achieve. He gives direction at the moments when the path is not clear. He empowers obedience in the exact places where your own will is insufficient.

You were bought at a price — the specific, historical, bodily price of the cross, the blood of the Son of God poured out for your redemption. The One who paid that price loves you more than anyone else who has ever claimed to own anything. And whatever He wants, wherever He leads, you can say yes with the confidence of someone who has read the character of the One who is asking.

You belong to Him now. And honestly — that changes everything.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with the honest confession that I have been living as if I belong to myself — making decisions based on my own preferences, building my life around my own agenda, treating Your ownership of me as a theological fact that does not have much practical bearing on my Monday. Forgive me. I was bought at a price I could not have paid, by a love I could not have earned, for a purpose I could not have designed. You own me — and You are the best possible owner. Let me live today as what I am: Your temple. Let the Holy Spirit who resides in me reveal, direct, and empower. Not my will. Yours.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Corinthians 6:12–20 in full — the complete passage on the body as temple. Note that Paul's argument is not primarily about purity for its own sake but about the specific identity of the body as the dwelling place of God. Ask: how does the awareness that my body is the temple of the Holy Spirit change the specific choices I am making about what I do with it today?
- Read Romans 6:15–23 — you are slaves to the one you obey. Note that Paul's point is not that slavery is good but that no one is actually free in the absolute sense — everyone is serving something. The question is not whether you belong to someone but who you belong to. Ask: what am I practically serving with my time, my attention, and my decision-making today — and does the answer match the theology I say I believe?
- Journal: "What is the specific area of my life where I have been living as if I belong to myself rather than to God — where my decisions are being made on the basis of my preference rather than His will? What would genuine surrender of that area look like today, practically?" Write the honest answer.
- Read Galatians 2:20 — I have been crucified with Christ and I no longer live, but Christ lives in me. Note the grammar: I no longer live. The old self that insisted on its own ownership has been crucified. Ask: which parts of my life have I not yet brought to the cross — and what is the specific resistance to surrender that I keep running into in those areas?
