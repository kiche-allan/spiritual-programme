## Revelation

Proverbs 4:23 is one of the most practical and most urgently relevant instructions in the entire wisdom literature for the person in the season of singleness — and it is also one of the most consistently misapplied. It is usually quoted in the context of romantic relationships and sexual purity, which is a legitimate application. But the scope of the instruction is broader than the romantic. Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it. The heart is the source — the spring from which the whole of the life flows. And the guarding is not primarily about what enters the heart through the romantic avenue. It is about the state of the spring itself — the condition of the interior life from which every relationship, every decision, every word, and every act of service flows.

In the context of the waiting season, the heart requires guarding from several specific threats that the season uniquely produces. The first is the situationship — the undefined, uncommitted, emotionally intimate relationship that provides some of the emotional experiences of a committed relationship without the accountability, the clarity, or the covenant protection of one. The situationship is the most common form of heart damage in the waiting season — because it feels like progress, feels like connection, feels like the beginning of something — while consistently taking from the emotional reserves without making the covenant commitment that would make the taking legitimate. The heart that has been in several situationships arrives at the genuine relationship depleted in ways that the person carrying it may not fully understand.

The second threat is the comparison — the social media feed that curates the engagements and the weddings and the babies of every person in the sphere, producing in the person who watches it the specific grief of the person who is always the witness and never the protagonist. The comparison does not only produce grief. It produces the specific distortion of the timeline — the sense that the story is behind where it should be, that the delay is an anomaly, that everyone else is arriving at the destination in the order they were supposed to and something has gone wrong with the route. The heart that is saturated with comparison loses the capacity to inhabit the present season because it is perpetually measuring the present season against a curated version of other people's stories.

The third threat is the premature emotional investment — the giving of the heart to a person who has not made the commitment that the giving of the heart deserves. Song of Songs 2:7 charges the daughters of Jerusalem not to arouse or awaken love until it so desires — the specific warning against the premature awakening of emotional and romantic attachment before the conditions that can sustain it are in place. The heart that has been prematurely awakened and then disappointed does not simply return to its previous state. It learns, from the disappointment, to protect itself in ways that will later make the genuine opening of the heart more difficult.

2 Timothy 2:22 gives us the positive counterpart to the guarding: flee the evil desires of youth and pursue righteousness, faith, love and peace along with those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart. The guarding is not only the defensive work of not doing certain things. It is the offensive work of pursuing the specific qualities — righteousness, faith, love, peace — in the company of specific people — those who call on the Lord out of a pure heart. The community in which the pursuit happens matters. The person who is trying to guard their heart in isolation is fighting the battle without the support that the community provides. The person who is pursuing righteousness and faith and love and peace in the company of people who are doing the same has both the accountability and the encouragement that the guarding requires.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the instruction that is above all else — guard your heart, for everything flows from it. I bring You the specific threats to my heart in this season: the situationships that have taken without committing, the comparisons that have distorted the timeline, the premature awakenings that have produced disappointments I am still carrying. I ask You to guard what I cannot guard alone — the spring from which everything else in my life flows. Keep it clean. Keep it whole. Keep it soft toward You and appropriately boundaried toward the things that would damage what You are building.

And let me pursue — righteousness, faith, love, peace — in the company of the people who are calling on You out of a pure heart. Let the guarding be both the defence and the pursuit.

## Daily Practices

- Read Proverbs 4:20–27 in full — the complete passage on the guarding of the heart. Note every specific instruction: attend to my words, do not let them out of your sight, guard your heart, put away perverse speech, keep your eyes straight ahead, give careful thought to your paths. Ask: which of these specific instructions is most directly relevant to where the heart is most vulnerable in my current season?
- Read 1 Corinthians 13:4–7 — the description of love that gives you the standard for what the real thing looks like. Ask: does the relationship I am currently most emotionally invested in — defined or undefined — match the description of love in 1 Corinthians 13? Does it produce patience and kindness and the not-keeping-record-of-wrongs? Or does it produce anxiety and jealousy and the constant recounting of grievances?
- Journal: "What is the specific way my heart has been most unguarded in this season — the specific situationship, comparison, or premature emotional investment that has taken from the spring without restoring it? What would it look like to bring that specifically to God and ask for the restoration of what has been depleted and the guarding of what remains?" Write the honest audit.
- Identify one specific practical boundary you will establish this week for the guarding of your heart — one specific action, one specific limit, one specific pursuit that will move you in the direction of righteousness and faith and love and peace. Write it down. Tell one person who will hold you to it. Let the guarding be both a prayer and a practice.
