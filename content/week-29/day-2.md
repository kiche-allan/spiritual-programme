## Revelation

Have you noticed how many people operate under the assumption that God's primary concern for their life is their happiness? The idea has become so pervasive that it barely registers as the theological error it actually is. As long as you are happy, that is all that counts. God just wants you to be happy. If it makes you happy it cannot be wrong. You hear it from well-meaning friends, from popular culture, and increasingly from corners of the church that should know better. And there is a word for it — not a kind word, but an honest one. Hogwash.

God is not in the business of making you happy. He is in the business of making you holy. And the sooner we settle that in our theology, the sooner the confusion about what God is doing in our lives begins to clear.

Now, do not misunderstand this. God loves you with a love that is wider and deeper and more personal than anything human language can fully describe. And there is genuine, profound, durable joy in walking with Him — not the brittle, circumstance-dependent happiness that the world chases, but the specific joy of a person who has found what they were actually made for. But that joy comes as the byproduct of pursuing holiness, not as the reward for chasing happiness on your own terms. It is the difference between the happiness of the person who eats whatever they want and the deeper, more stable wellbeing of the person who has learned to eat what actually nourishes them. One feels better in the moment. The other produces a life.

We are living in a moment when the church faces a specific pressure that every generation faces in a different form — the pressure to applaud what God has called His people to repent of. There used to be altars flooded with people who were genuinely broken over their failures and desperate for the forgiveness that only God can give. People who understood that the gap between who they were and who God had called them to be was not something to be celebrated but something to be taken seriously — something that required the specific, costly work of repentance rather than the comfortable reassurance that God is fine with wherever you are because He just wants you to be happy.

The call of God on your life is not the call to a comfortable, feeling-good, perpetually affirmed existence. It is the call to something far more demanding and far more glorious — the specific, daily, costly pursuit of a life that actually looks like the One who called you. Be holy, because I am holy. Not be happy, because I am happy. Holy — the specific quality of being set apart, of being different in the ways that matter, of reflecting in your ordinary life the character of the extraordinary God whose name you carry.

If God is nudging you today about something in your life that does not please Him — do not run from it. Do not reach for the theological comfort of the God who just wants you to be happy. Own it. Repent. Turn away while there is still time and the turning is still possible. He is not trying to steal your joy. He is trying to give you something far better and far more durable than the temporary happiness you have been settling for.

When you pursue holiness, you discover that what you were actually looking for was waiting for you there all along. Real satisfaction — the kind that does not evaporate when the circumstances change — was always on the other side of the obedience you have been avoiding.

Trust Him with your life. He knows what He is doing with it.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I repent today of the theology that has made Your primary concern my comfort rather than my character. I have wanted You to be the God who approves of wherever I am because You love me — and You do love me, but Your love is too serious and too committed to my actual good to leave me comfortable in what is making me less than who You called me to be. Make me holy today. Not just happy — holy. Show me the specific area where You are nudging me, and give me the courage to respond with repentance rather than rationalisation. I trust that what You have for me on the other side of the holiness is better than what I am currently settling for.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Thessalonians 4:1–8 in full — the complete passage on holy living. Note that Paul frames holiness not as religious performance but as the specific way of living that pleases God and reflects the calling that was placed on you. Ask: in what specific area of my life am I currently pursuing happiness at the expense of holiness?
- Read Romans 12:1–2 — offer your bodies as living sacrifices, holy and pleasing to God. Note the phrase do not conform to the pattern of this world. Ask: in what specific way is the pattern of this world currently shaping my choices more than the renewing of my mind?
- Journal: "What is the specific thing in my life that I have been calling my happiness that God has been calling my compromise? What would genuine repentance — not remorse, not regret, but the actual turning away — look like today?" Write the honest answer.
- Read Hebrews 12:10–11 — God disciplines us for our good, that we may share in His holiness. No discipline seems pleasant at the time but produces a harvest of righteousness and peace. Ask: what is the specific discipline I have been resisting that God has been using to produce the holiness I say I want?
