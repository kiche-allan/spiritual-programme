## Revelation

How many of you have ever used a word of profanity? I'm not going to make you raise your hand. Well, maybe I will. How many of you have ever used one in church? Gotcha.

Profanity keeps getting darker in our culture. Words that were never said out loud are now acceptable everywhere. You can't turn on the television or scroll social media without hearing them. We've become so desensitized to sin that we don't even flinch anymore, and that includes our own mouths.

When Isaiah saw the Lord high and lifted up, he didn't say, "What a beautiful experience." He cried out, "Woe is me! For I am lost; for I am a man of unclean lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people of unclean lips." There's your first cussing preacher right there. Notice that the moment he sees God clearly, he sees his own mouth clearly.

But look at what God did next. He didn't leave Isaiah undone. He sent a seraph with a burning coal from the altar, touched his mouth, and took his guilt away. Right after that, Isaiah heard God ask, "Whom shall I send?" and he answered, "Here I am! Send me."

God cleanses the mouth He intends to use.

Here's what excites me. If you belong to Christ, the Holy Spirit lives inside you, and wherever you go, He goes. When you walk into your office, your job site, or your classroom, His presence walks in with you. His job is to bring conviction of sin.

I believe one reason we haven't seen real revival, like the great awakenings, is that believers haven't taken their presence in the workplace seriously. When coworkers notice you don't talk the way everyone else talks, they're noticing Him. That difference draws people to Jesus.

It doesn't take a crowd. All it takes is one man or one woman totally committed to Christ.

So if your words need cleansing today, don't hide it. Take it to the altar. He'll touch your lips, take your guilt away, and send you back into your workplace carrying His presence.

## Morning Prayer

Holy Father, I confess that my mouth has not always honoured You. I have grown used to words that grieve You, and I have stopped flinching. Touch my lips with the coal from Your altar. Take my guilt away. Then send me back into my workplace, my home, and my classroom carrying Your presence. Here I am. Send me.

## Amen

In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read Isaiah 6:1–8 slowly. Notice the order: seeing God, seeing yourself, being cleansed, being sent.
- Journal: "Which words do I use that I would not use if Jesus were standing next to me? What do I need to bring to the altar?" Confess them by name.
- Today, pay attention to the way you speak at work or school. Let the difference be quiet and consistent, and ask God to use it.
