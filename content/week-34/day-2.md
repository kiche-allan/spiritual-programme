## Revelation

Is there someone you've hurt who you're hoping will just forget about it?

Let me tell you something I've noticed. Many of us excuse our behaviour. We speak to our spouses, our children and the people who work for us with a harsh tone, and then we justify it. We tell ourselves we were only trying to motivate them, or that what we said was correct. Sometimes it was correct. But it wasn't what we said. It was how we said it that cut so deep.

Some of you run a business or supervise people. You may get the job done by pushing hard, but your staff go home carrying what you said to them. Some of you have a son or daughter who is grown now, and there's a coldness between you that began years ago with words you've never taken back. Some of us haven't properly spoken to a brother or sister since a quarrel over land, or an inheritance, or a family meeting where things were said that couldn't be unsaid.

Jesus says that if you are about to worship and you remember that someone has something against you, stop. Leave your gift right there at the altar. Go and make it right first, and then come back and worship.

That's how serious God is about relationships. He would rather have you reconciled with your brother than receive your offering. You can sing beautifully, give faithfully and serve every Sunday, and still carry a wall into church that God can see.

Paul puts the standard this way: "If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all." Notice the words "so far as it depends on you." You can't control how they respond. You can control whether you have done your part. And Proverbs reminds us how much tone matters: "A soft answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."

Let me pray this for us. Lord, help us to seek restoration with the spouses we have hurt, the children we have embittered and the employees we have wounded by the tone of our voice. We mean it, and we need it.

So who came to your mind just now? Don't wait for them to bring it up. Call them, or better yet, go and see them this week. Say it plainly: "I was wrong in how I spoke to you. Will you forgive me?" Then come back to worship with nothing standing between you.

## Morning Prayer

Lord, You see the people I have hurt with my words and my tone, and some of them I have tried hard to forget. Give me the humility to go to them. Help me to say plainly that I was wrong, and to ask for forgiveness without excuses. Let me come back to worship with nothing standing between us.

## Amen

In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read Matthew 5:21–26. Notice that Jesus places reconciliation before the offering, not after it.
- Journal: "Who has something against me? Whom have I hurt by my tone, my silence or my words?" Write the name and the sentence you will say: "I was wrong in how I spoke to you. Will you forgive me?"
- Contact that person this week. Call or visit, and don't send a message if you can speak to them face to face. Say it, and don't defend yourself.
