## Revelation

Have the false alarms made you stop believing the real thing is coming?

A few months ago, my mother was in the ICU after emergency surgery. If you have never sat beside an ICU bed, here is something you learn quickly: the monitors never stop. They beep, they flash, they light up in red. Heart rate. Blood pressure. Breathing. Oxygen. Numbers on a screen that made my heart sink every time I looked at them. I became a student of numbers I never wanted to understand.

And many of those alarms, I'm told, turn out to be nothing. A sensor slips off a finger. A patient shifts in the bed. A blank reading can mean a probe has come loose, or it can mean something is terribly wrong, and from where I stood I couldn't always tell which. Hospitals even have a name for what happens to people who hear alarms all day. They call it alarm fatigue. After a while, the beeping becomes background noise.

But a nurse can't afford to tune it out, because one day the alarm is real. There was a day when the oxygen reading on my mother's monitor fell sharply within minutes, and that alarm was not false.

Peter saw the same thing coming for the return of Christ. He wrote that scoffers would say, "Where is the promise of his coming? For ever since the fathers fell asleep, all things are continuing as they were from the beginning of creation." Two thousand years of waiting. People who set dates and got them wrong. Headlines about the end that came to nothing. It's easy to hear an alarm so often that you stop hearing it at all.

But Peter's answer is gentle. "The Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise as some count slowness, but is patient toward you, not wishing that any should perish, but that all should reach repentance." The delay is not God being late. It's mercy. Every extra day is one more day for someone to come home. And then comes the warning: "the day of the Lord will come like a thief."

That mercy won't last forever, and nobody has been told the date.

I'm thankful that my mother is home today. But I haven't forgotten what that room taught me. Some alarms you cannot afford to ignore, and the real one often sounds just after you've stopped listening.

So don't let the delay make you sleepy. Use the time. Repent while there is still time. Make peace, say yes to Jesus, and live awake. If the real alarm sounded today, would you be ready?

## Morning Prayer

Lord, thank You for Your patience. Thank You that every extra day is mercy and not slowness. Forgive me for the times I have heard Your warnings so often that I stopped listening. Keep me awake and repentant, and let me live today as if the alarm may sound before tonight.

## Amen

In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read 2 Peter 3:3–13. Notice what Peter says the delay is for, and what he says we should do while we wait.
- Journal: "Have I grown used to hearing about Christ's return, so that it no longer moves me?" Write down one thing you have been putting off that you would do if you knew He was coming this week.
- Tell one person today that God's patience is an invitation, and not a delay. If you have a name on your heart, pray for them by name.
