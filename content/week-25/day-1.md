## Revelation

The fear of the Lord is not a season. It is not the emotional intensity of a powerful encounter that fades as the memory of the encounter fades. It is not the goosebumps of the worship service or the weight of the conviction that arrives at the altar and lifts by Tuesday morning. The fear of the Lord, as the wisdom literature consistently describes it, is a way of life — the sustained, deepening, daily orientation of a person who has genuinely reckoned with who God is and has allowed that reckoning to govern the whole of their existence.

Psalm 34:11 gives us one of the most personal invitations in the entire Psalter: come, my children, listen to me — I will teach you the fear of the Lord. David is not describing something that arrived fully formed in a single encounter. He is offering to teach it — which means it is teachable, which means it is learned, which means it develops over time through sustained engagement rather than arriving complete in a single moment. The fear of the Lord is the curriculum of a lifetime, not the content of a single lesson.

The distinction that most needs to be made is the distinction between the fear that is a one-time experience and the fear that has become the atmosphere. Most believers have had the experience — the moment in worship or in prayer or in crisis when the reality of God's holiness pressed so close that everything else became small and the appropriate response was awe. These moments are real and they are important. But they are not yet the fear of the Lord as the wisdom writers describe it. They are the introduction to it. The fear of the Lord as a way of life is what happens when the person who has had those moments allows them to change the ongoing orientation of their daily existence — when the awe that arrived in the encounter does not evaporate with the encounter but becomes the lens through which every subsequent moment is interpreted.

What does this look like practically? It looks like the person who makes decisions differently because they are aware of being seen. Not performing for an audience — genuinely aware of the presence of the One whose opinion is the only one that ultimately matters, and allowing that awareness to govern the choice when no one else is watching. It looks like the person whose tongue is disciplined not primarily by the fear of being caught but by the awareness that words are spoken before the God who will call every careless word to account. It looks like the person who handles money, relationships, and power with a specific kind of carefulness that flows not from anxiety but from the reverent awareness that everything they are managing belongs to Someone else and will be accounted for.

Proverbs 23:17 gives us the most practical challenge to sustaining the fear of the Lord across time: do not let your heart envy sinners, but always be zealous for the fear of the Lord. The always is the key word. The fear of the Lord competes, across the ordinary days, with the envy of what the person who does not fear the Lord appears to be enjoying. The person without the fear of the Lord seems to prosper without the constraints. They seem to gain without the cost. And the temptation across the long middle of the faithful life is to allow the envy to erode the awe — to lose the fear of the Lord not through a dramatic act of rebellion but through the gradual drift of a heart that has been watching the wrong thing for too long.

Isaiah 33:6 gives us the treasure that makes the sustained fear of the Lord worth every cost: the fear of the Lord is the key to a rich store of salvation and wisdom and knowledge. The fear of the Lord is not a restriction on life. It is the key to the fullest version of it — the access code to the storehouse of everything that the human being was made to inhabit. Salvation, wisdom, knowledge — not as distant theological categories but as the living, practical, daily experience of a person whose life is governed by the awe that the fear of the Lord produces.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the fear of the Lord not as a feeling to be recovered but as a posture to be inhabited — the sustained, daily, deepening orientation of a person who has genuinely reckoned with who You are and is allowing that reckoning to govern everything. Where the awe has faded into familiarity, restore it. Where the encounter has not yet become the atmosphere, deepen it. Let the fear of the Lord be the lens through which I interpret every decision, every word, every relationship, and every moment of this ordinary day.

Do not let my heart envy the person who lives without this. Let me be zealous for the fear of the Lord — today, and all day long, and again tomorrow.

## Daily Practices

- Read Psalm 34 in full — the complete psalm from which verse 11 is drawn. Note that David wrote it while pretending to be insane before Abimelech — one of the most undignified moments of his life. And from that place he offered to teach the fear of the Lord. Ask: what does it mean that the fear of the Lord is available and teachable even in the most undignified seasons?
- Read Proverbs 1:1–7 and Proverbs 9:10 — the two foundational statements of the fear of the Lord as the beginning of wisdom. Ask: in what specific area of my life do I most need the wisdom that only the fear of the Lord produces?
- Journal: "What is the difference between my experience of the fear of the Lord in a peak spiritual moment and my experience of it on an ordinary Tuesday? What would it look like for the awe of the encounter to become the atmosphere of the ordinary day?" Write the honest exploration.
- Read Isaiah 6:1–8 — Isaiah's encounter with the enthroned God. Note what the fear of the Lord produced in him: first the undoing, then the cleansing, then the commissioning. Ask: is the fear of the Lord in my life producing the same sequence — the honest recognition of who I am before God, the cleansing that follows, and the commissioning that the cleansing makes possible?
