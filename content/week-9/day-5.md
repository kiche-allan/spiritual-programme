## Revelation

Every principle Myles Munroe taught about purpose is present in the story of Esther, which is why it closes the week rather than opening it. By Friday you have the full vocabulary. Now read the story with that vocabulary and watch how it illuminates every scene.

Purpose preceded her creation. Esther was not placed in the palace by accident. She was placed there before the crisis existed — before Haman was appointed, before the decree was signed, before Mordecai tore his clothes. The placement preceded the need it was designed to meet. God does not scramble to respond to crises. He positions His people inside the future He already sees. Esther's beauty, her character, her relationship with Mordecai, her access to the king — none of these were coincidences. They were the embedded equipment for a purpose that had not yet been revealed.

Purpose is discovered not invented. Esther did not choose her calling. It was revealed to her through a crisis she did not create and would not have chosen. Mordecai's question — who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this — was not a suggestion. It was a revelation. He was showing her what was already true about her position. This is often how purpose is disclosed. Not through a moment of solitary inspiration but through a crisis that requires you to look at your life from a different angle and see what has always been there.

Her passion pointed to her purpose. Esther could not be unmoved by the annihilation of her people. Whatever comfort the palace offered, it could not compete with the impossible grief of knowing that the people she came from were scheduled for destruction. That grief was not a distraction from her purpose. It was the signal of it. And her purpose served others at enormous personal cost. If I perish, I perish. This is the statement of a person who has understood that her purpose is not about her survival. It is about her people's survival. The willing surrender of self-preservation in service of others is the most complete expression of Munroe's principle. Success — even safety, even life — without purpose is empty. Esther chose purpose over safety and saved a nation.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week's stories with the one that brings them all together. Esther in the palace. Purpose that preceded her arrival. A calling she did not choose but could not refuse. A crisis that revealed what had always been true about her position. A decision that cost everything and saved everything simultaneously.

I ask You today the question Mordecai asked her: for such a time as this? What is the such a time of my own life? What crisis, what need, what problem in my world requires exactly what You placed in me before I was born? I have been asking what I want to do with my life. Perhaps the better question is: what did You design me to do in this specific moment of history?

I receive the courage of Esther's declaration. I do not need to see the full outcome before I step forward. I do not need a guarantee of survival before I walk through the door. If I perish, I perish — but I will go. Give me that kind of settled, costly, purposeful courage. Not performed bravery, but the quiet resolution of someone who knows who placed them here and why.

## Daily Practices

- Read Esther 3 and 4 in full — the context of the crisis and Mordecai and Esther's exchange. Note every detail of Esther's position: her access, her relationship with the king, her ethnic identity that was concealed, the timing of it all. Each detail is a piece of equipment for the purpose she was about to fulfil.
- Reflect on all five of Munroe's principles through the lens of your own life: Purpose preceded your creation — what evidence do you see of gifts and capacities that were present before you chose them? Your purpose was discovered not invented — what circumstances or crises have revealed something true about your calling? Your passions point to purpose — what are you unable to be neutral about? Your purpose serves others — who is your purpose for? Success without purpose is empty — where have you achieved things that still felt hollow?
- Journal: "If someone who loved me were to say to me what Mordecai said to Esther — 'who knows but that you have come to your position for such a time as this' — what would the such a time be? What is the crisis in my world that my specific life was designed to address?"
- Read Romans 8:28–30 as your closing declaration. All things working together for good, for those called according to His purpose. The story of your life — including the painful chapters — is being woven by a God who established your purpose before you arrived. Trust the weaver.
