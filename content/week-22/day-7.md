## Revelation

The Emmaus road account is the most personal resurrection appearance in any of the four Gospels — and the most instructive for the ordinary Christian life, precisely because it does not happen in a dramatic setting. It does not happen in the upper room with the doors locked. It does not happen in a garden with angels in white. It happens on a road. Two disciples walking away from Jerusalem in defeat, confused and grieving and rehearsing the events of the weekend that had ended all their hopes — and a stranger falls into step beside them and asks what they are talking about. They stand still, their faces downcast, and tell Him everything. The things about Jesus of Nazareth. A prophet, powerful in word and deed. We had hoped that he was the one to redeem Israel. But it has been three days.

The Emmaus road begins with the disciples walking away. Away from Jerusalem, away from the community of the other disciples, away from the empty tomb that the women had reported and that seemed too good to be true. The direction of their walking is the direction of their faith — retreating, withdrawing, putting distance between themselves and the place where everything had gone wrong. And Jesus — without announcing Himself, without correcting their confusion before joining them, without requiring that they recognise Him before He would walk with them — fell into step beside them. He met them in the direction they were going rather than waiting for them to turn around and come back. He was present before they knew He was present. He was walking with them before they knew who was walking with them.

Luke 24:27 tells us what Jesus did on the road: beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, He explained to them what was said in all the Scriptures concerning Himself. He opened the whole of Scripture — not a few proof texts, not the most convenient passages, but Moses and all the Prophets, the comprehensive sweep of the entire Old Testament — and showed them how all of it was pointing toward Himself, the One who had just been crucified and raised. And as He did it, something was happening in them that they did not yet have words for. Their hearts were burning within them. Not a feeling they identified in the moment — only in retrospect, when they looked back on the road and asked each other: were not our hearts burning while He talked with us? The presence of Jesus produced a burning before the recognition of Jesus produced the awareness of the burning.

The recognition came at the table. He reclined with them. He took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and began to give it to them. And their eyes were opened and they recognised Him — and He disappeared from their sight. The breaking of the bread was the moment. Not the theological explanation. Not the burning heart. The specific, physical, utterly ordinary act of a Person taking bread in His hands and breaking it the way He had always broken it — the way He had broken it at the feeding of the five thousand, the way He had broken it at the Last Supper — was the thing that made the eyes see what had been present all along. He was known to them in the breaking of the bread.

This is the pattern of the encounter with Jesus in the ordinary. He falls into step on roads you did not expect Him to be on. He joins conversations that are going in the wrong direction. He explains the Scriptures in ways that produce the burning before you have identified the burning. And then He is known in the most ordinary possible moment — the breaking of bread, the table, the specific physical act that carries the weight of what He accomplished. The disciples on the road had been in the presence of the risen Christ for the length of a journey from Jerusalem to Emmaus — approximately seven miles — without knowing it. The burning heart was the sign of a presence they had not yet identified. And the recognition, when it came, was at the table rather than on the theological summit.

This closes the week perfectly. We began with the garden of Eden where the walking with God was interrupted. We close with the road to Emmaus where the walking with God resumed — quietly, on an ordinary road, with two people who were going the wrong direction and who encountered the risen Christ before they knew they were encountering Him. The garden of the new Jerusalem is coming. But between the first garden and the last, the risen Christ is walking beside us on the roads we are currently on — explaining the Scriptures, burning our hearts, waiting to be known at the table — and the question the week ends with is the question the disciples asked each other when they finally understood what had happened: were not our hearts burning within us while He talked with us on the road?

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week on the Emmaus road — aware that the risen Christ has been walking beside me this week in ways I have not always recognised, explaining the Scriptures in ways that have been burning my heart without my always having identified the burning. Let me notice the burning today. Let me look back on this week and ask the question the disciples asked: where was He walking with me that I did not fully recognise? Where was the presence I was in before I knew whose presence it was?

And let me be known to Him at the table — in the breaking of bread, in the ordinary physical act that carries the weight of everything the cross accomplished. Let my eyes be opened in the moment of the breaking. Let me recognise Him there, in the most ordinary moment of the most ordinary day, and let the recognition send me back toward Jerusalem — back toward the community, back toward the direction I had been retreating from — with the news that the Lord is risen indeed.

He is risen. He is on the road. He is at the table. He is here.

## Daily Practices

- Read Luke 24:13–35 in full — the complete Emmaus road account. Read it slowly enough to notice every specific detail: the two disciples, the direction they were walking, the downcast faces, the stranger who fell into step, the explanation of the Scriptures, the invitation to stay, the table, the breaking of the bread, the recognition and the disappearance, the immediate return to Jerusalem. Ask: which detail speaks most directly to where you are in your own journey right now?
- Review the entire week — the garden and the glory, the blood of the covenant, the wilderness, righteousness, the power of the tongue, discipleship, and the table and the road — and write one sentence for each day capturing what Jesus was explaining on the road as He walked beside you through each theme. Seven sentences. This is the Scripture He opened to you this week.
- Journal: "Where has my heart been burning this week — the moments when I felt the presence of something more significant than I could immediately identify, the places where the Scriptures opened in a way that felt like a walking companion explaining them rather than a text being processed? Write what the burning was pointing to."
- Go to the table this week — to the Lord's Supper if it is available to you — and receive the broken bread with the full awareness of Emmaus. He is known in the breaking. Let your eyes be opened there. And then go back to Jerusalem — back to the community, back to the direction you may have been retreating from — with the testimony of what happened on the road and at the table.
