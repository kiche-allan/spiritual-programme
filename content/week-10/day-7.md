## Revelation

The last prayer in the Bible is two words. Come, Lord Jesus. Maranatha — an Aramaic phrase so ancient that it was preserved untranslated even in the Greek-speaking church, which tells you something about how deep in the bone of the earliest Christians this longing was. They did not say it as a formula or a liturgical reflex. They said it as people who had seen the risen Christ, who were living in the power of the Holy Spirit, who were suffering for their faith in an empire that despised them — and who were genuinely, daily, expectantly watching the horizon for the One they loved. Come. We are ready. Come.

The week you have walked through has been building to this moment. Submission in Gethsemane — the willingness to say not my will but Yours, which is itself a form of Come. Trust in the waiting — the decision to anchor to the character of God when the circumstances offer nothing, which is the daily posture of someone who believes the One they are waiting for is worth waiting for. Patience in the refusal — the grace to receive God's no as a better answer than your own request, which requires believing that the coming King's perspective is more trustworthy than your own assessment of what you need. Prayer as relationship — the ongoing, honest, daily conversation with a Father rather than a series of transactions with a vending machine. And holy living — the shape of a life so oriented toward the coming King that it becomes the most tangible evidence that he is expected.

1 John 3:2–3 ties all of it together in one verse: all who have this hope in him purify themselves, just as he is pure. The hope of Christ's return is not an escape from the responsibility of holy living. It is the most powerful motivation for it. The person who genuinely, daily, concretely expects Jesus to return lives differently. Not because they are performing religion for an audience but because the orientation of their entire life has shifted. They are not building their kingdom. They are watching for His. They are not accumulating for themselves. They are preparing for Him. And 2 Peter 3:11–12 asks the question that should settle everything else: since everything will be destroyed, what kind of people ought you to be? The answer the whole week has been building toward is: praying people, submitting people, trusting people, waiting people, holy people. People who can say with their whole life, not just their lips: Maranatha. Come, Lord Jesus.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week with the oldest prayer of the church on my lips and in my heart: Come, Lord Jesus. Come into the submission I am still learning. Come into the waiting I am still enduring. Come into the prayers I am still bringing. Come into the holy life I am still imperfectly pursuing. Come.

I live between the already and the not yet — and I want to live this space well. You have already come — in the incarnation, the cross, the resurrection, the Spirit. And You are coming again. In between, I have been given the extraordinary privilege of living in such a way that my life is oriented entirely toward both. Let everything I do this week be shaped by that orientation. Not a performance of religion but a genuine posture of expectancy.

Let the week that follows this one be evidence that these seven days were not information received but a life being transformed. I am Yours. I am watching. I am waiting. I am praying. I am submitting. I am trusting. Come, Lord Jesus. Come.

## Daily Practices

- Read Revelation 22:12–21 in full — the final words of Scripture. Count how many times the theme of coming appears. Note that the Spirit and the bride both say come. You are part of the chorus. Let the final words of the Bible be the first words of your week ahead.
- Review your journal from the whole week. Write one sentence that captures the single most important thing God said to you across all seven days. This is your testimony from this week. Carry it into the month ahead.
- Identify how all four themes connected in your own life this week. Where did you practise submission? Where did you trust God in the waiting? Where did you bring a specific prayer? Where did you choose holy living over the pattern of the world? Name each one specifically — not in the abstract.
- Share something from this week with someone close to you today. The themes of this week — submission, trust, prayer, and holy expectation — are not meant to be kept private. They are meant to be lived out loud.
