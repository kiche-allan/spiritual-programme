## Revelation

Is your life a straight line?

How many of us would honestly be able to say that we gave our lives to Christ and it has been a straight, clear, predictable ascent from that point forward — that the path has been visible from the start, that the destination has always been in sight, that the steps have followed one another with the logical clarity of a well-written plan? Very few. Because the Christian life is almost never a straight line. It is a road with turns in it — turns that you did not see coming, that you would not have chosen, that made no sense at the time and sometimes still make no sense from the other side.

Proverbs 16:9 captures this with an honesty that is both unsettling and comforting: in their hearts humans plan their course, but the Lord establishes their steps. You plan. He establishes. And the establishing is not always — is almost never — identical to the planning. The steps He establishes often look different from the course you charted. They go through places you did not include in the itinerary. They stop at junctions you would have driven past. They take you down roads that seem to lead away from the destination before they loop back toward it from an angle you never would have approached from if you had taken the route you planned.

God takes you through valleys you did not expect. He leads you on detours that make no sense while you are in them. He allows delays that produce frustration. He closes doors you had already walked up to with your hand on the knob. He opens doors you never considered — in places you did not want to go, for reasons you cannot yet see.

And then, looking back, you can see why.

The delay positioned you. The detour taught you what the direct path would have bypassed. The closed door protected you from something that looked like an opportunity and was actually a trap. The valley cultivated in you the specific character that the destination was always going to require — and that only the valley could have produced. The winding road brought you precisely to where you needed to be, by the route that only the God who sees the end from the beginning could have designed.

Joseph's road to the palace ran through a pit and a prison. The direct route from the dream to the throne — the one Joseph might have drawn at seventeen — did not have either of those stops on it. But without the pit he would not have gone to Egypt. Without the prison he would not have met the cupbearer. Without the cupbearer he would not have stood before Pharaoh. Without Pharaoh he would not have become the second-in-command of the most powerful nation in the world at the precise moment when his family needed someone in exactly that position to survive a famine. The crooked line was the straight path — it just could only be seen as such from the other end.

Romans 8:28 is the theological foundation underneath the crooked lines: God works all things together for good for those who love Him and are called according to His purpose. Not some things. All things. The things you chose and the things that were chosen for you. The things that made sense and the things that never have. The open doors and the closed ones. The valley and the hilltop. Nothing is wasted. Every twist, every turn, every unexpected change is being worked together by the God whose ways are higher than your ways and whose thoughts are higher than your thoughts.

Stop demanding the straight path. Stop resenting the detour. Stop being angry at the closed door. Trust the God who knows the best route to get you where He is taking you. The destination is secure. The path might be crooked. But every step is ordered by the Lord.

And He always gets His children home.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I confess that I have been demanding a straight path when You have been giving me an ordered one — demanding to see the destination from the starting point when You have only promised to be a lamp for my feet and a light for my path. I repent of the anger and the frustration and the resentment toward the detours that I now understand are part of the route. Give me the trust of Joseph in the pit — the faith that knows the God who gave the dream is also the God who is working in the delay, in the valley, in the closed door, in the winding road that does not yet make sense. Nothing is wasted. Every step is ordered. Take me home Your way.

## Daily Practices

- Read Genesis 37–50 in overview, noting the specific turns in Joseph's road — the pit, the slavery, the false accusation, the prison, the forgotten cupbearer, and finally the palace. Note how each turn that looked like a setback was actually the specific setup for the next stage. Ask: which specific turn in my own road am I currently most frustrated by — and what might God be setting up through it?
- Read Isaiah 55:6–11 in full — His ways are higher than your ways. Note that the higher ways of God are compared to the rain that comes down from heaven and does not return without watering the earth and producing growth. Ask: in what specific area of my life is God's higher way currently producing growth that my own planned route would not have produced?
- Journal: "What is the specific detour in my life right now that I have been most angry or frustrated about — the delay, the closed door, the unexpected turn? What would it look like to look at that detour through the lens of Romans 8:28 today — to receive it as something that God is working together for good rather than something He has failed to prevent?" Write the honest reflection.
- Read Psalm 37:23–24 — the Lord makes firm the steps of the one who delights in him; though he may stumble, he will not fall, for the Lord upholds him with his hand. Note that the ordered steps include the stumble — even the stumble is held in the hand of the God who orders the steps. Ask: what is the stumble in my recent past that I have not yet received as being held in God's hand?
