## Revelation

Romans 13:11 begins with a phrase that reorients everything that follows: understanding the present time. The instruction that Paul is about to give — wake up, put aside the deeds of darkness, put on the armor of light — is grounded not in a general moral aspiration but in a specific temporal awareness. This is what time it is. And the time it is changes what the appropriate response to the time is. The person who does not know what hour it is behaves differently from the person who does — who knows that the night is nearly over, that the day is almost here, that every moment of the remaining darkness is a moment closer to the dawn rather than a moment deeper into the night.

The hour has already come to wake up from slumber. The image is the image of a person who has been asleep while the world around them was active — who has been in the drowsiness of spiritual inattentiveness while the eschatological clock has been running. Our salvation is nearer now than when we first believed. This is one of the most precise and most neglected statements in the Pauline corpus — the returning of Christ, the completion of the salvation that began at conversion, is measurably nearer than it was at the moment of first believing. Every day that has passed since the first day of faith is a day by which the return is closer. The person who has been a believer for twenty years is twenty years closer to the completion of the salvation than they were when they first believed. The urgency of the present moment is not a static background of general Christian obligation. It is a running clock — and the clock is closer to midnight than it has ever been.

The night is nearly over. The day is almost here. This does not mean that the darkness is not real — the night is still the night, the darkness is still dark, the deeds of darkness are still active in the world the believer inhabits. But the night is nearly over — which changes the relationship of the believer to the darkness. The person who knows that dawn is coming does not settle into the darkness the way the person who expects the night to last indefinitely settles into it. They stay alert. They remain awake. They do not make the comfortable accommodations of someone who has accepted that this is what the permanent condition looks like.

Put aside the deeds of darkness and put on the armor of light. The armor of light is not the armor of Ephesians 6 — though the imagery is related. It is the comprehensive clothing of the person who belongs to the day — who has identified with the coming light so thoroughly that their entire way of being in the world reflects the dawn rather than the darkness. The putting on is deliberate, active, chosen — the specific daily decision to clothe yourself in what you are, in what you belong to, in what the day that is coming will make undeniable.

Ephesians 5:14–16 adds the practical urgency: make the most of every opportunity, because the days are evil. The Greek for make the most is exagorazo — to buy up, to purchase in full, to redeem completely. Every opportunity — every conversation, every relationship, every circumstance of service, every moment in which the light can be expressed in a world that is still mostly dark — is to be bought up completely, redeemed fully, used with the specific intentionality of someone who knows the price of what is being purchased and will not let it slip away for lack of attention.

1 Thessalonians 5:4–5 gives us the identity that makes the wakefulness possible: you are children of the light and children of the day. We do not belong to the night or to the darkness. The armor of light is not something alien that the believer puts on from outside. It is the natural clothing of someone who has been transferred from the domain of darkness to the kingdom of the light — who is, at the deepest level of their identity, a child of the day living in the final hours of a night that is almost over.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I understand the present time today — I receive the eschatological awareness that Romans 13:11 commands. The hour has come to wake up. Our salvation is nearer now than when I first believed. The night is nearly over. And the appropriate response to this specific moment in the story is to put aside the deeds of darkness and put on the armor of light.

Let me be awake today — to the time, to the opportunities, to the deeds of darkness that I have been accommodating because the night has seemed long enough to settle into. I am a child of the light and a child of the day. Let me live in the clothing that belongs to who I am. And let me make the most of every opportunity that this specific day presents — buying it up completely, redeeming it fully, with the intentionality of someone who knows what time it is and how close the dawn is.

The day is almost here. Let me live today as though I believe it.

## Daily Practices

- Read Romans 13:8–14 in full — the complete passage including the love commandment that precedes the armor of light section. Note that Paul grounds the eschatological urgency in the love commandment: love is the fulfillment of the law, and the approaching day is the motivation for the love. Ask: how does the knowledge that the day is almost here change the quality and the urgency of the love you bring to the people around you today?
- Read Matthew 25:1–13 — the parable of the ten virgins. Note that all ten were waiting for the bridegroom — all ten expected his coming. The difference was preparedness. Five had oil and five did not. When the bridegroom arrived, those who were ready went in. Ask: what does it mean to be prepared for the coming of the bridegroom — not merely to expect it but to be ready for it? What is the oil of your current readiness?
- Journal: "What are the specific deeds of darkness I have been accommodating — not the dramatic sins but the gradual, comfortable, barely-noticed accommodations to the darkness that the knowledge of the approaching day should make impossible? What would it look like to put on the armor of light specifically in the area where the darkness has been most comfortable?" Write the honest audit.
- Read Revelation 22:12 — behold, I am coming soon! My reward is with me, and I will give to each person according to what they have done. Ask: if I knew that today was the day of the return — that the night was over and the day had arrived — what would I do differently? And if the answer is significantly different from how I am actually living, what does the difference tell me about how genuinely I believe the night is nearly over?
