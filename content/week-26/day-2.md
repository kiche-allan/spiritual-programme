## Revelation

The church has not always served single people well. The prosperity gospel version of singleness promises marriage to everyone who believes hard enough — as though the absence of a spouse is primarily a faith problem that more prayer and more confession will resolve. The traditional religious version makes single people feel like incomplete human beings who are in a holding pattern until the real life begins — the life of the couple, the family, the married household that the church has sometimes treated as the normative Christian unit. Both of these versions are pastorally harmful and theologically inadequate. And both need to be replaced by what Scripture actually says.

What Scripture actually says begins with Jesus Himself — who was single, who never married, who lived the most complete and most fully human life available and did so outside of the married state. If marriage were the completion of human personhood, the most fully human person who ever lived has an unexplained gap in His humanity. He does not. The life of Jesus demonstrates that a human being can be fully alive, fully purposeful, fully loving, and fully in relationship with God and other people without being married.

Paul's teaching in 1 Corinthians 7 is the most direct biblical treatment of singleness available — and it is not the teaching of a person who views singleness as a deficiency. Paul describes the single person as having an undivided devotion to the Lord that the married person, by the nature of the marriage covenant, cannot fully maintain. The unmarried woman is concerned about the Lord's affairs — her aim is to be devoted to the Lord in both body and spirit. This is not a consolation prize. It is a genuine description of a specific grace available in the season of singleness that is not equally available in the season of marriage. The single person has access to a quality of undivided attention toward God that the married person must share with the legitimate and beautiful demands of the covenant they have entered.

Isaiah 54:5 gives us the most extraordinary statement available to the single person who is experiencing the specific grief of not yet being chosen: your Maker is your husband. The Lord Almighty is His name. This is not a platitude. It is a theological declaration about the specific relational provision that God makes for the person who does not yet have a human covenant partner — the declaration that the One who made you is also the One who has committed Himself to you in the most complete and most faithful version of the covenant relationship available. The human husband is a partial and imperfect expression of the covenant love that God Himself provides in full. The single person is not without covenant love. They have it in its fullest and most reliable form.

Psalm 27:14 gives us the posture that makes the waiting season sustainable: wait for the Lord; be strong and take heart and wait for the Lord. The double command to wait — the same double emphasis we encountered in Psalm 27:13 — is the acknowledgment that the waiting is hard enough to require hearing twice. Be strong. Take heart. The waiting is not passive resignation. It is the active, strong, courageous holding of the conviction that the God who is being waited for is worth the waiting — and that what He is producing in the season of the wait is not less valuable than what would have been produced by an earlier arrival.

The season of singleness is not a problem to be solved. It is a chapter to be inhabited — with the specific grace that the chapter provides, the specific freedom that the undivided devotion makes possible, and the specific depth of relationship with God that the waiting season, properly received, is uniquely capable of producing. The person who has learned to be fully alive in the season of singleness is the person who will bring the most to the season of marriage if and when it comes. And the person who has spent the season of singleness waiting to begin living will arrive at the marriage having wasted the most formative chapter available to them.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the dignity of the season I am in — not as the lesser season before the real one begins, but as the chapter with its own specific grace, its own specific freedom, and its own specific invitation to the undivided devotion that 1 Corinthians 7 describes. Let me inhabit this season fully rather than spending it waiting for the next one. Let me receive the covenant love of Isaiah 54:5 — the Your Maker is your husband that is not a consolation but a genuine provision. And let me be strong and take heart in the waiting — not because the desire for marriage is wrong, but because the God I am waiting with is sufficient for the waiting and is doing something in it that the arrival would interrupt.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Corinthians 7:25–35 in full — Paul's complete teaching on the single life. Note that Paul says he wishes everyone were as he is — not as a universal prescription but as a genuine expression of the value he placed on the freedom that singleness provided for his ministry. Ask: what specific dimension of the freedom of singleness am I currently inhabiting — and what specific dimension am I failing to use?
- Read Isaiah 54:1–8 in full — the complete passage in which Your Maker is your husband appears. Note the full context: the desolate woman who will have more children than the married woman, the enlarging of the tent, the shame of youth forgotten, the widowhood remembered no more. Ask: what does the full promise of Isaiah 54 say to the specific grief of the person who has felt the shame of being unchosen?
- Journal: "What would it look like to fully inhabit the season of singleness — to treat it as the chapter with its own specific grace rather than the waiting room before the real chapter begins? What would change in how I spend my time, pursue my calling, invest in my relationships with God and others, and receive the specific freedom that this season provides?" Write the honest exploration.
- Read Matthew 19:10–12 — Jesus's teaching on the single life in response to the disciples' reaction to His teaching on marriage. Note that Jesus says not everyone can receive this teaching — only those to whom it has been given. Ask: what has God given me in this season that is specific to this season — and am I receiving it?
