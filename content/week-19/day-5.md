## Revelation

Hebrews 7:25 contains one of the most comforting statements in the entire New Testament — and one of the least meditated upon. He always lives to intercede for them. Always. Not when the prayer is impressive enough to warrant His attention. Not when the faith is strong enough to qualify for His advocacy. Always — as the continuous, unceasing, eternal activity of the risen Christ at the right hand of the Father. Right now, at this moment, as you read these words, the Son of God is praying for you. By name. With full knowledge of your situation. With the authority of the One who has already defeated every power that is working against you. With the intimacy of the One who has been where you are — who was tempted in every way as you are, who wept at the tomb of his friend, who sweated blood in the garden, who knows from the inside what it costs to be human.

The intercession of Christ is not a supplement to your prayer life. It is the foundation of it. When you pray, you are not praying alone. You are joining a prayer that has already been praying — that was praying before you woke up this morning, that was praying through the night when you were not aware of it, that will continue praying after you have moved on with your day. The prayer of Christ is not waiting for your prayer to get it started. Your prayer is participating in what His prayer has already established.

John 17 gives us the clearest window into what the intercession of Christ actually sounds like. It is sometimes called the High Priestly Prayer — the prayer Jesus prayed in the upper room the night before His crucifixion, which functions as the New Testament equivalent of the High Priest's prayer on the Day of Atonement. And in verses 20–21, Jesus prays for people who do not yet exist: my prayer is not for them alone. I pray also for those who will believe in me through their message. You are in that prayer. If you have believed in Christ through the message of the apostles — which is the message of the New Testament — you are one of the those who will believe that Jesus was praying for in the upper room two thousand years ago. Before you were born. Before you had heard the gospel. Before you had any capacity to respond. You were named in the prayer of the Son of God on the most important night in the history of the world.

Romans 8:34 places the intercession in its most practically relevant context — the context of condemnation. Who then is the one who condemns? No one. Christ Jesus who died — more than that, who was raised to life — is at the right hand of God and is also interceding for us. The intercession is the answer to every accusation. The enemy who accuses the brothers day and night before the throne of God — Revelation 12:10 — finds his accusations met by the intercession of the One whose blood has already answered every charge. The advocate is already present. The case has already been made. The verdict has already been rendered in your favour, not because you are without fault, but because the One who intercedes for you is without fault — and His righteousness is the righteousness that stands in your place.

This changes the nature of your trial. Every difficulty you face, every accusation the enemy brings, every moment when the weight of your own failure makes it hard to believe you have standing before God — in all of these, the risen Christ is at the right hand of the Father, interceding. Not a historical memory. Not a theological position. An active, present, continuous advocacy that does not sleep and does not tire and does not give up on the person it is praying for. He always lives to intercede for them. Always.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the most neglected comfort in the New Testament — that Your Son is at Your right hand, right now, interceding for me. By name. With full knowledge of everything I am carrying, everything I have failed, everything that is being brought against me. The advocate is already present. The prayer is already being prayed. My prayer today is joining something that has already been underway for longer than I have been alive.

Let this truth change how I face the day. Not alone. Not unrepresented. Not hoping that my faith will be strong enough or my prayer impressive enough to gain a hearing. Already heard — because the One who always lives to intercede has already interceded, and the Father who hears His Son has already received what He has brought.

Thank You that I was in the prayer of John 17 before I was born. Let me live today with the confidence of someone who knows they have an advocate.

## Daily Practices

- Read John 17 in full — the complete High Priestly Prayer. Read it slowly, noting every specific thing Jesus prays for: protection, unity, sanctification, the knowledge of the Father, the love of the Father. These are the things the risen Christ continues to intercede for. Ask: which of these specific intercessions do I most need to receive today?
- Read Hebrews 4:14–16 and Hebrews 7:23–28 together — the two passages that most fully develop the intercession of Christ in the book of Hebrews. Note that the intercession is grounded in the humanity of Christ — He sympathises with our weaknesses because He was tempted in every way as we are. Ask: how does the humanity of the Intercessor change how you feel about bringing your specific weaknesses to the throne of grace?
- Journal: "What is the accusation I am most tempted to believe about myself right now — the voice that says I am too far gone, too weak, too inconsistent for God to be actively interceding for me? What does Romans 8:34 say directly to that accusation?" Write the accusation and then write the answer.
- Pray today with the conscious awareness that you are joining a prayer already in progress. Begin your prayer not with your request but with the acknowledgment: Lord Jesus, You are already interceding for me. I join what You have already begun. Then bring what you need to bring, from within the security of that awareness.
