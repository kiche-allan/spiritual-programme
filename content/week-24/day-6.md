## Revelation

Jesus still has wounds. This is one of the most theologically significant facts in the entire New Testament — and one of the least contemplated. The resurrection body of the Son of God, which 1 Corinthians 15 describes as imperishable, glorious, and powerful, bears the specific marks of the specific death that produced it. The nail marks are in the hands. The wound is in the side. Thomas is invited to touch them — not as a concession to doubt, but as the evidence of a continuity between the crucified body and the risen body that the resurrection has not erased.

Why does Jesus still have wounds? If the resurrection is the triumphant reversal of everything that the cross did to His body, why has the reversal not included the removal of the marks? Every other dimension of the body's limitations has been transformed — it is no longer subject to death, no longer confined to a single location, no longer bound by the physical constraints that governed it before the resurrection. And yet the wounds remain. Deliberately, apparently, permanently. Thomas is invited to touch them on the evening of the resurrection. The same wounds are still present when Jesus appears to the disciples eight days later. And Revelation 5:6 describes the Lamb in the throne room of heaven as looking as if it had been slain — the wounds of the cross present in the eternal, glorified, exalted state of the Son of God at the right hand of the Father.

The wounds of the risen Christ are not the marks of a trauma that the resurrection was unable to fully address. They are the permanent testimony of what the love of God was willing to endure for the sake of the people He loved. They are the badge of the victory — not the shame of the defeat. The soldier who has fought and won carries the marks of the battle, and the marks are not evidence that the battle was lost. They are the proof that the battle was real, that it was costly, and that the person bearing them was willing to pay whatever it cost to win it. The wounds of Jesus are the permanent, visible, touchable testimony that God was willing to be wounded for the people He loves — that the love that drove the incarnation and the cross was not theoretical but physical, was not painless but costly, was not from a safe distance but from the inside of the suffering itself.

Isaiah 53:5 told us this before it happened: by His wounds we are healed. The wounds were not incidental to the healing. They were the mechanism of it. The specific, physical, real suffering of the body of the Son of God was the specific price that the healing required. And the healing that the wounds produce is comprehensive — not only the spiritual healing of forgiveness, but the physical healing available to the body that the wounds were inflicted on. By His wounds. The wounds are not past tense in the sense of having been healed and forgotten. They are present in the resurrection body. They are still available as the source of healing for every wound that the people He loves carry.

Zechariah 13:6 gives us one of the most haunting pre-figurements of the wounds of Christ in the prophetic literature: what are these wounds on your body? Then he will answer: those with which I was wounded in the house of my friends. The wounds came from the people He loved. The rejection, the betrayal, the false accusation, the crucifixion — all of it at the hands of those whose redemption the wounds were accomplishing. And He bears the marks of the wounding by His friends permanently, in the resurrection body, as the evidence that love that is wounded by the ones it loves does not stop loving. It rises. Still wounded. Still present. Still inviting the wounded to touch the wounds.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the most comforting truth available to a person who is currently wounded — that the God I am bringing my wounds to is the God who still bears His own. The wounds of Jesus in the resurrection body are the permanent testimony that You are not a God who speaks about suffering from a safe distance. You entered it. You bear the marks. And the wounds that are still in Your hands are the source of the healing that my wounds require.

Touch my wounds today with the wounds of Jesus. Not the distant theological proposition of a God who once suffered on my behalf. The specific, physical, permanent testimony of a God who still bears the marks — and who invites me to touch them as the evidence that what was real enough to wound was also real enough to raise, and that what was raised still bears the marks so that I would know the raising was genuine.

By His wounds I am healed. Let me receive that healing today — specifically, physically, in the specific place where the wound is deepest.

## Daily Practices

- Read John 20:19–29 in full — both resurrection appearances to the disciples. Note that in both appearances, Jesus shows them His wounds — not as an afterthought but as a central element of the encounter. Ask: why does the risen Jesus lead with the wounds in both appearances? What is He communicating by showing the wounds before saying anything else?
- Read Revelation 5:1–10 — the Lamb that looks as if it had been slain, standing at the centre of the throne. Note that the most central figure in the throne room of heaven is identified by His wounds — the Lamb as if slain. Ask: what does it mean that the central image of heavenly worship is the wounded Lamb? What does heaven's focus on the wounds say about how God values what the wounds represent?
- Journal: "What is the specific wound I am currently carrying — the physical, emotional, relational, or spiritual wound that has not yet healed? What does it mean to bring this specific wound to the One who still bears His own — to the God who is acquainted with wounds from the inside rather than the outside? What does the permanence of His wounds say to the person who is afraid that their own wounds will be permanent?" Write the honest engagement.
- Read Isaiah 53 in full — the complete servant song. Note every specific description of the wounding: pierced, crushed, punishment on Him, wounds, led like a lamb to the slaughter, cut off from the land of the living. Then read verse 5 again: by His wounds we are healed. Ask: is there a specific wound in my body, in my soul, or in my relationships for which I have not yet applied the healing that the wounds of Jesus have made available? Bring it to Him today by name.
