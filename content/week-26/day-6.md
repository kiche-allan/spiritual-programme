## Revelation

Hannah's prayer in 1 Samuel 1 is the most honest prayer of desire in the entire Old Testament — and it is the model for the person who is trying to bring a deep, sustained, sometimes anguished desire to a God who has not yet answered it. Hannah wanted a son. Not in the mild, would-be-nice sense. In the deep, defining, it-has-become-the-thing-I-carry sense. The desire for a child had shaped her grief and her identity and her daily experience of life in a way that Peninnah's provocations kept fresh and Elkanah's well-intentioned but inadequate question — am I not better to you than ten sons — could not address.

She went to the temple and prayed — and the prayer she prayed was not the polished, theologically composed prayer of someone who had processed the grief to the point of equanimity. It was the prayer of deep anguish. She wept bitterly. The Hebrew for deep anguish is mar nephesh — the bitterness of the soul, the most interior, most real level of the distress. She brought the actual state of her interior life to God without the management that religious presentation usually requires. This is the prayer that Eli mistook for drunkenness — the lips moving, no sound coming out, the body expressing what the voice could not articulate. And it is the prayer that God answered.

The model Hannah provides for the person bringing the desire for marriage to God is the model of the unmanaged prayer — the prayer that does not pretend the desire is smaller than it is, does not perform the spiritual composure that is not yet genuinely felt, does not protect God from the honest weight of what is being carried. Hannah did not pray: Lord, if it is Your will and if it is in Your plan, I would appreciate the possibility of considering the question of a child, understanding of course that You know best and that I am content regardless. She prayed: if You will only look on my misery and remember me and give me a son. Specific. Personal. Urgent. Unguarded. And she made a vow — she brought not only the asking but the offering, the commitment of what she would do with the answer if it came.

Psalm 37:4 gives us the principle that undergirds Hannah's prayer: take delight in the Lord and He will give you the desires of your heart. The delight is not the technique by which the desire is obtained — as though sufficiently enthusiastic worship is the mechanism that produces the answer. The delight is the relationship in which the desires of the heart are gradually formed into alignment with the desires of the One in whom the person delights. As you delight in the Lord, your desires are formed by the relationship — and the desires that have been formed by the relationship are the desires that He gives. Hannah's desire for a son was not a self-centred desire. It was a desire so thoroughly brought to God, so completely vowed back to Him before the answer came, that the son she was asking for was already, in the vow, the Lord's before he was hers.

Philippians 4:6 gives us the practical instruction for the person who is carrying the desire for marriage: do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. Present — the Greek word is gnorizo, to make known, to bring into the open, to declare specifically. The desire for marriage is not too personal or too specific to present to God. It is exactly the kind of request that the instruction covers — the specific, personal, deeply felt request that is brought with thanksgiving rather than anxiety, and that is released to the God who receives it rather than held tightly as the demand that must be met before peace is possible.

The difference between the desire that has become an idol and the desire that is properly held is the difference between the closed fist and the open hand. The closed fist grips the desire — you must give me this, my peace depends on this, I will not be whole until this arrives. The open hand presents the desire — here it is, Lord, I have not hidden it from You, I have not pretended it is smaller than it is, I have brought it specifically and honestly — and then waits with the open hand for whatever the hand of God places in it.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I bring You today the desire I have been carrying — specifically, honestly, with the deep anguish of Hannah's prayer rather than the managed composure of the prayer that protects You from the actual weight of what I feel. You already know the desire. I am not informing You of something You were unaware of. I am bringing it into the open — making it known, presenting it, releasing the closed fist and opening the hand.

Take delight in the Lord and He will give you the desires of your heart. Let me delight in You today — genuinely, not as the technique by which I obtain the answer, but as the relationship in which the desire is held, formed, and eventually received or redirected according to the wisdom that exceeds my understanding. The desire is in Your hands. I trust the hands.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Samuel 1:1–20 in full — the complete Hannah narrative from the provocation of Peninnah to the birth of Samuel. Note every specific detail: the weeping, the not eating, the deep anguish, the prayer, Eli's misreading, Hannah's response, the going away and eating and being sad no longer — before the answer came. Ask: what does Hannah's experience of the peace that arrived before the answer say about what the genuine presenting of the desire to God produces?
- Read Psalm 37:1–11 — the complete passage in which delight in the Lord and the giving of the heart's desires appears. Note the full context: do not fret, trust in the Lord, do good, dwell in the land, be still before the Lord, wait patiently. Ask: which of these instructions is most directly relevant to how I am currently holding the desire for marriage?
- Journal: "What is the honest prayer I have been afraid to pray — the specific, unmanaged, Hannah-style prayer that brings the actual weight of the desire rather than the managed version of it? Write it. Not the polished version. The actual one. And then vow it — what is the thing you are committing to do with the answer if it comes?" Write both the prayer and the vow.
- Read Romans 8:26–27 — the Spirit interceding for us with wordless groans. Ask: what does it mean that when the desire is too deep for words — when the prayer is the moving lips and no sound, the way Hannah prayed — the Spirit is interceding with the groans that you cannot articulate? Let the deep anguish, wherever it is today, be brought to the One who receives the groan as prayer.
