## Revelation

The most dangerous season in the spiritual life is not the crisis. In the crisis, you know you need God. The bills are overdue, the diagnosis has arrived, the relationship has broken, the job has gone — and you are on your knees because you have nowhere else to go. Crisis faith is real faith. But it is not mature faith. Mature faith is the faith that persists and deepens when everything is fine — when the bills are paid, when the body is healthy, when the relationships are peaceful, when the job is secure. This is the season that Israel kept failing in across the entire Old Testament. Not the wilderness season — they cried out to God consistently in the wilderness. The Promised Land season. The season of abundance, of fine houses, of multiplied silver and gold. The season when they forgot.

Deuteronomy 8 is Moses's most urgent warning to a people who are about to enter the most comfortable season of their national existence. He does not warn them about the enemies they will face. He warns them about the prosperity they will receive. Be careful that you do not forget the Lord your God. The word careful in Hebrew is the same word used for guarding or keeping watch — the active, alert, deliberate attention of someone who knows that a specific threat exists and is taking it seriously. The threat Moses identifies is not military or economic. It is spiritual and it is internal: your heart will become proud. Comfort produces pride. Satisfaction produces forgetfulness. The very blessings of God, received without the discipline of gratitude and continued dependence, become the mechanism of spiritual drift.

2 Peter 1:5–7 gives us the antidote — not a passive waiting for spiritual growth to happen but an active, effortful, intentional building. Make every effort to add to your faith. The word add is the Greek epichoregeo — to supply generously, to furnish abundantly, to keep adding layer upon layer. Peter gives us eight qualities that are to be progressively layered onto the foundation of faith: goodness, knowledge, self-control, perseverance, godliness, mutual affection, and love. This is not a checklist to be completed. It is a description of a life that is always growing — always adding, always deepening, always building on the foundation rather than merely sitting on it. The good season is the season to build. Not to coast, not to consolidate, not to enjoy what has been received and stop there. To build.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You this morning in whatever degree of comfort or stability my life currently holds — and I receive it as a gift from Your hand without letting it become a reason to forget the hand that gave it. Deuteronomy 8 has named something that I recognise in myself: the tendency to cry out in the difficult season and drift in the comfortable one. I do not want to be that person.

Let the good season be a building season. Let the absence of crisis produce not spiritual complacency but the unhurried, undistracted, deliberate deepening of my roots that only becomes possible when I am not in survival mode. Let me add to my faith today — not dramatically, not with great spiritual effort, but with the consistent, daily, faithful attentiveness of someone who knows that what is built in the calm will sustain them through the storm.

Keep me from the pride that comfort produces. Keep me from the forgetfulness that satisfaction enables. Let my heart remain dependent even when my circumstances do not require it.

## Daily Practices

- Read Deuteronomy 8 in full — Moses's complete warning about the danger of prosperity. Note every specific mechanism he identifies: satisfaction, fine houses, multiplied wealth, pride. Ask honestly: which of these is most present in my current season, and what is it doing to my spiritual attentiveness?
- Read the parable of the four soils in Matthew 13:1–23. Note that the soil that produces no fruit is not always the hard soil of outright rejection. Some of the most interesting commentary in the parable is about the thorny soil — the person who receives the word with joy but whose growth is choked by the worries of life and the deceitfulness of wealth. Ask: which soil best describes my current spiritual condition?
- Journal: "What spiritual disciplines have I allowed to erode in a comfortable season? Prayer, Scripture reading, community, generosity, service? Name them specifically and ask: what would it take to rebuild them now, while the season is still good and the urgency of crisis is not driving me?"
- Read 2 Peter 1:3–11 in full. Note that Peter says if you possess these qualities in increasing measure they will keep you from being ineffective and unproductive. The building is not optional for fruitfulness. Begin today — pick one quality from the list and ask God to develop it specifically in you this week.
