## Revelation

Myles Munroe said something that should permanently disrupt the way you think about your own existence: God never makes anything without a reason. Not a tree, not a star, not a grain of sand — and certainly not a human being. Before a single cell of you was arranged, before your name was chosen, before your parents had a conversation, God had already established the reason for your arrival. You were not born and then assigned a purpose. You were purposed, and then born. The sequence matters enormously. It means that everything you need to fulfil your purpose was already embedded in you before you drew your first breath.

Jeremiah 1:5 is perhaps the most radical statement of this truth in all of Scripture. God does not say to Jeremiah: I have been watching you and I think you might be useful. He says: before I formed you in the womb I knew you. The knowing preceded the forming. The calling preceded the birth. The appointment as a prophet to the nations was not a decision God made in response to Jeremiah's qualities — it was a decision made before Jeremiah had any qualities at all, before he existed in any form that could be evaluated. This is purpose that precedes creation. This is the kind of intentionality that cannot be accidental.

Psalm 139 gives us the language of craftsmanship. Knit together. Woven. Fearfully and wonderfully made. This is not the language of mass production or accident. This is the language of a craftsman who knows exactly what He is making and why, who attends to every detail because every detail serves the finished purpose. And Ephesians 2:10 gives us the destination of that craftsmanship: good works prepared in advance. Not invented on the fly, not discovered by accident, not stumbled upon in a moment of inspiration — prepared. In advance. Before you arrived. Your purpose was ready before you were. The question has never been whether you have one. The question is whether you will take the time to uncover what was placed in you before you were born.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with a question I have been carrying, sometimes consciously and sometimes as a quiet ache underneath everything else: why am I here? Not in a despairing sense — but in the genuine, searching sense of someone who wants to know the reason for their existence beyond the surface answers that the world offers.

I receive today the truth that You do not make anything without a reason. That the same intentionality with which You knit me together in my mother's womb is the intentionality with which You placed specific capacities, passions, and gifts inside me — not for my own enjoyment alone, but for a purpose that was established before I could contribute anything to it. I did not earn my purpose. I cannot lose it through failure. It was given before I arrived.

Open my eyes this week to what You placed in me before I was born. Not through striving, not through performance — through attentiveness. I want to uncover what is already there. I want to live from the centre of the reason You made me. Begin that work in me today.

## Daily Practices

- Read Jeremiah 1:1–10 in full — the complete account of Jeremiah's call. Note that his first response is inadequacy: "I do not know how to speak; I am too young." God's response is not to reassure him about his abilities but to remind him of his appointment. The purpose preceded the person's readiness.
- Journal: "What do I currently believe about why I exist? Is my answer borrowed from what others expect of me, or is it something I have genuinely wrestled with before God?" Write honestly, not aspirationally.
- Read Psalm 139:1–18 slowly as a meditation. Let every verse that describes God's knowledge of you land fully before moving to the next. You are being read about, not just reading.
- Spend 10 minutes in silence asking one question: "Lord, what did You put in me before I was born that I have not yet fully acknowledged?" Write down whatever surfaces without editing it.
