## Revelation

There is a version of the Christian life that is entirely governed by feeling — where the quality of Sunday is measured by whether tears came, where the reality of God's presence is confirmed by the emotional charge of worship, where the dry season is automatically interpreted as divine absence. It is an understandable version. Feeling is immediate, concrete, and convincing. When the presence of God feels near, faith is easy. When it does not, everything becomes harder.

But Hebrews 11:1 defines faith in a way that specifically excludes feeling as its foundation. Faith is confidence in what we hope for — not confidence in what we feel. It is assurance about what we do not see — not assurance about what we experience emotionally. The writer of Hebrews is not dismissing the reality of spiritual experience. He is establishing that the ground of faith is not experience but promise — not what happens in the emotional register of a worship service but what God has declared to be true regardless of what the emotional register is reporting.

The two disciples on the road to Emmaus are the most instructive biblical illustration of this principle. Luke 24:13–35 tells us that as they walked toward Emmaus, Jesus himself came up and walked along with them — but they were kept from recognising him. They felt confused, downcast, and disappointed. They described the events of the weekend to the stranger walking beside them with the unmistakable tone of people whose hope had collapsed. And yet He was right there. Walking at their pace. Asking them questions. Listening to their grief. Present in the most complete sense possible — and entirely unfelt.

The Emmaus road is the road that many believers find themselves on in the dry season — the season when the worship does not produce the charge, the sermon does not produce the tears, the prayer feels like it is going nowhere, and the silence of God feels louder than any word He has spoken. And the question that the Emmaus road answers is: was Jesus absent from those disciples because they could not feel His presence? The answer is clearly no. He was beside them the entire time. Their inability to perceive Him did not change the reality of His presence by a single step.

Joshua 1:9 is the promise that governs the Emmaus road experience: the LORD your God will be with you wherever you go. Not wherever you feel Him. Not wherever the worship is good and the sermon is moving and the emotional conditions are favourable. Wherever you go — including the road toward Emmaus, including the dry season, including the morning when it is less good morning Lord and more good Lord morning. The promise is not conditioned on perception. It is unconditional.

Spiritual maturity is precisely the capacity to trust the promise over the feeling — to say on the morning when nothing is felt: He is here, not because I feel Him, but because He said He would be. Feelings are real. They are not unreliable as feelings — they accurately report the emotional state of the person experiencing them. What they are unreliable as is indicators of God's presence. His presence is not confirmed by how you feel. It is confirmed by what He promised. And He promised never to leave you or forsake you.

Feel Him or not — He is there.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You this morning not on the basis of how I feel but on the basis of who You are and what You have promised. You said You would never leave me or forsake me. You said You would be with me wherever I go. I receive that promise today as the ground of my faith — not the emotional register of this morning, not the feeling that is or is not present, but Your word which does not change with my mood or my biological clock or the quality of last night's sleep.

On the days when I feel You clearly — thank You. On the days when I feel nothing — You are still here. Let me be the disciple who trusts the promise on both kinds of days with equal confidence. And open my eyes, as You opened the eyes of the Emmaus disciples, to recognise the presence that has been beside me the whole time.

## Daily Practices

- Read Luke 24:13–35 in full — the complete Emmaus road account. Note that Jesus was present before they recognised Him and that His presence did not depend on their perception of it. Ask: where in my current season has Jesus been walking beside me in ways I have not yet recognised?
- Read Hebrews 11:1–6 — the full opening of the faith chapter. Note that every person listed in this chapter acted on promise rather than feeling. Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham — none of them had the emotional confirmation before they acted. They had the word. Ask: what word has God spoken to me that I have been waiting to feel before I act on?
- Journal: "What is the specific dry season I am currently navigating — the area where I feel nothing and am tempted to interpret the absence of feeling as the absence of God? What changes when I receive Joshua 1:9 not as comfort but as fact — He is with me here, not because I feel it but because He said it?" Write the honest exploration.
- At some point today — in traffic, at your desk, in the ordinary middle of the afternoon — stop and say out loud or in your heart: He is here. Not as a feeling. As a statement of fact based on a promise. Notice what the deliberate, feeling-independent declaration does to your awareness of His presence.
