## Revelation

The Holy Spirit is the most neglected Person of the Trinity in most evangelical Christianity. The Father is understood — God above, Creator, Sovereign, the One to whom prayer is ultimately addressed. The Son is understood — Jesus, historical, incarnate, crucified, risen, the One through whom access to the Father is made possible. But the Spirit remains, for most believers, a vague and largely impersonal presence — acknowledged in the doctrinal statement, mentioned in the benediction, associated with emotional experiences in worship, but not genuinely known as the Person that Scripture consistently describes.

2 Corinthians 13:14 — the most trinitarian sentence in the Pauline corpus — describes the Spirit's relationship with believers in a specific and significant word: the fellowship of the Holy Spirit. The Greek word is koinonia — the deepest form of sharing and participation available in the Greek vocabulary. It is the word used for the fellowship of the early church in Acts 2:42, for the participation in the body and blood of Christ in 1 Corinthians 10:16, for the sharing in the sufferings of Christ in Philippians 3:10. Koinonia is not a casual association. It is the intimate, mutual, personally engaged participation of people who genuinely share something important. The fellowship of the Holy Spirit — the koinonia that the Spirit offers — is not the occasional emotional experience of His presence in a worship service. It is the ongoing, daily, personally engaged communion of a Person who lives in you and wants to be genuinely known by you.

Galatians 5:25 gives us the most practical instruction about how this communion is maintained: since we live by the Spirit, let us keep in step with the Spirit. The phrase keep in step is the Greek stoicheo — which means to walk in a line, to follow in someone's footsteps, to match your pace and direction to the one walking ahead. The image is of two people walking together who are genuinely aligned — not the back one dragging behind and the front one pulling, not the back one running past the front one, but the two walking together with the same pace and the same direction. This is what the communion of the Spirit looks like in practice. Not the occasional dramatic experience of His power. The daily, moment-by-moment alignment of the believer's pace and direction with the Person who is leading.

John 16:13 tells us what the Spirit is doing in the life of the person who is genuinely walking with Him: He will guide you into all the truth. He will not speak on His own — He will speak only what He hears from the Father. He will tell you what is yet to come. The Spirit is not silent. He is speaking — but His speaking requires a listener who has developed the sensitivity to hear Him. The communion of the Spirit is not primarily about dramatic manifestations, though the Spirit is capable of those. It is primarily about the developed sensitivity of a person who has spent enough time in the Spirit's company to recognise His voice in the ordinary moments — the prompting that feels like a nudge rather than a shout, the conviction that arrives quietly rather than dramatically, the guidance that is often perceived as a thought that has a different quality from the surrounding thoughts, the comfort that arrives from the inside rather than from the outside.

The practical development of this sensitivity requires the same things that the development of sensitivity to any person requires: time, attention, the willingness to be quiet enough to listen, and the responsiveness that acts on what is heard rather than waiting for louder confirmation. The person who wants to walk in koinonia with the Spirit must practise the specific disciplines that create the conditions for the Spirit's voice to be heard — the silence that contemplative prayer establishes, the attentiveness that the examen develops, the responsiveness that acts on the small prompting before demanding the large one. The Spirit is a Person. He responds to being treated like one.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come today to the Person I have most consistently neglected in my trinitarian faith — the Holy Spirit, who lives in me, who was sent to be my Paraclete, who guides into all truth and speaks what He hears from You and tells of what is to come. I receive today the fellowship — the koinonia — that 2 Corinthians 13:14 describes. Not access to a power. Communion with a Person.

Teach me to keep in step with Him the way Galatians 5:25 describes — matching my pace and direction to His, not dragging behind and not running past, but walking together in the genuine alignment of two people who are genuinely going the same direction. Develop in me the sensitivity to hear His voice in the ordinary moments — the nudge, the prompting, the conviction that arrives quietly rather than dramatically.

Holy Spirit, I acknowledge You today as a Person. I am listening. Guide me into the truth You have for today.

## Daily Practices

- Read John 14:15–31 and John 16:5–15 together — Jesus's complete teaching on the Spirit in the Upper Room Discourse. Make a list of every specific thing Jesus says the Spirit will do. Note that every item on the list describes the activity of a Person, not a force — the Spirit teaches, reminds, testifies, convicts, guides, speaks, tells. Ask: am I relating to the Spirit as a Person who does these things, or as an atmosphere that occasionally produces experiences?
- Read Acts 13:1–4 — the commissioning of Paul and Barnabas. Note the specific sequence: while the church was worshipping and fasting, the Holy Spirit said set apart for me Barnabas and Saul. The Spirit spoke. The church heard. The church responded. Ask: what is the difference between a church culture that is attentive enough to the Spirit's speaking to hear a specific instruction, and a church culture that processes its own decisions and then asks the Spirit to bless them?
- Journal: "What is my honest relationship with the Holy Spirit? Do I think of Him as a Person I know, a power I access occasionally, or a doctrine I affirm? When did I last consciously respond to something I perceived the Spirit prompting me toward?" Write the honest answer without self-condemnation.
- Practise the communion of the Spirit today in its most basic form: set aside fifteen minutes — not to pray in the conventional speaking sense, but to be genuinely quiet and attentive, asking the Spirit to speak and then waiting, listening, writing down whatever surfaces. This is not mysticism. This is the beginning of treating the Spirit as the Person He is.
