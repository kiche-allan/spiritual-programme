## Revelation

The grace received on Monday was never meant to remain a private possession. It was always meant to flow outward — and Saturday brings the week full circle by addressing the most concrete and often the most difficult expression of that outward flow: forgiveness. Ephesians 4:32 gives the standard with a clarity that leaves no room for negotiation: forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you. The measure of the forgiveness you are called to extend is not the size of the offence against you. It is the size of the forgiveness you yourself have already received — which, if Monday's reflection on grace was genuinely absorbed, is far larger than almost any offence another person could commit against you.

Peter's question in Matthew 18:21 reveals how most people instinctively think about forgiveness — as a finite resource with a reasonable limit. Up to seven times? Peter is being generous by the standards of his culture, and he likely expected to be commended for the offer. Jesus's response — not seven times, but seventy-seven times — is not a new, larger finite number to count toward. It is a deliberate rejection of the counting mentality altogether. The number is symbolic of completeness, of forgiveness without a ledger, of a posture that does not keep score because keeping score was never the point.

This is illustrated immediately afterward in the parable of the unforgiving servant — a man forgiven an impossibly large debt who then refuses to forgive a small debt owed to him. The disproportion in the parable is the entire point. Most grievances held against another person, measured honestly against what God has already forgiven in us, are the small debt next to the impossibly large one. This does not minimise real pain — forgiveness is not a denial that genuine harm occurred. It is the decision to release the claim for repayment, trusting God with the justice of the situation rather than insisting on extracting it yourself.

Colossians 3:13 gives the standard one more time, in case it was missed the first time: forgive as the Lord forgave you. Not partially. Not conditionally. Not only once the other person has sufficiently apologised or demonstrated change. As the Lord forgave you — which, per Monday's reflection, happened while you were still a sinner, before any qualifying had taken place. This is the hardest and most freeing instruction in the entire week, because it asks you to extend to others the exact same unearned, unconditional grace that you received and that made this whole week's foundation possible in the first place.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with the specific person or situation where forgiveness has been hardest for me to extend. I bring it honestly, without pretending the offence did not matter or that the pain was not real.

I ask You to help me forgive as You forgave me — not because the person has earned it, not because the debt has been repaid, but because You forgave me long before I had done anything to deserve it. Let the grace I received on Monday flow through me today toward the person I have been holding a grievance against. Help me release the claim I have been keeping, trusting You with the justice of the situation rather than insisting on extracting it myself.

Be kind and compassionate in me today, the way Ephesians 4:32 describes, toward the people who have wronged me — the same way You have already been kind and compassionate toward me.

## Daily Practices

- Read the parable of the unforgiving servant in Matthew 18:23–35 in full. Note the staggering disproportion between the debt the servant was forgiven and the debt he refused to forgive. Ask: measured against what I have already been forgiven by God, how does my current unforgiveness compare?
- Journal: "Who do I most need to forgive right now? What is the specific grievance I have been holding, and what has kept me from releasing it?" Write honestly rather than minimising it.
- Read Ephesians 4:31–32 together. Note that verse 31 lists what must be put away first — bitterness, rage, anger, brawling, slander, malice — before verse 32 describes what replaces it: kindness, compassion, forgiveness. Forgiveness often requires actively putting something else down first.
- Take one concrete step toward forgiveness today — even a small one. This might be a prayer specifically for the person's wellbeing, a decision to stop rehearsing the offence in your mind, or, where appropriate, direct reconciliation. Let grace move outward the way it moved toward you.
