## Revelation

There is a famous painting by Holman Hunt called The Light of the World. It shows Jesus standing at a vine-covered door, lantern in hand, knocking. It is a beautiful image of Revelation 3:20.

But most people miss the detail that the artist put there on purpose: there is no doorknob on the outside of the door.

The door can only be opened from the inside. Jesus will not kick it down. He will not force His way through. He will not manipulate or manoeuvre His way past your will. He knocks. He waits. And the choice to open belongs entirely to you.

This is both the wonder and the weight of the gospel — that the God of the universe stands at the door of a human life and waits to be invited in. The intimacy He is offering is real. He wants to come in, sit with you, share life with you. But He will not push Himself on you.

Some of us can hear the knock. We know He is there. But we are not opening the door — maybe because we are afraid of what He will ask for, maybe because we are comfortable with the distance, maybe because knowing about Jesus feels safer than actually knowing Him.

But knowing about someone and knowing someone are two entirely different things. And the only way to really know Jesus is to open the door.

The handle is on your side. He is still knocking.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I hear the knock today. I have been hearing it for a while — and I have been finding reasons to leave the door closed. Today I open it. Not because I have everything sorted out on my side first. Not because I am ready in the way I have been waiting to be ready. I open it now, as I am, from where I am. Come in. Sit with me. I would rather have You in the mess than have the mess without You.

## Daily Practices

- Read Revelation 3:14–22 — the full letter to the church at Laodicea. Note that Jesus is speaking to a church — people who were already religious, already attending, already going through the motions. Ask: is Jesus on the inside of my life or still knocking from the outside?
- Journal: "What specifically has been keeping the door closed — fear, comfort, the cost of what He might ask? What would opening the door fully look like today in practical terms?" Write the honest answer.
- Read John 15:4–5 — remain in me, and I will remain in you. The open door is the beginning of an abiding relationship, not a one-time event. Ask: am I abiding, or did I open the door once and slowly let it drift shut again?
