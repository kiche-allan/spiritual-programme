## Revelation

What would you ask God for if you were not afraid He might say no?

That question is worth sitting with — because the honest answer reveals something important about the actual size of the God we are praying to. Most of us pray prayers that are shaped not by the character of God but by the ceiling of our own expectation. We ask for what we think He can manage. We frame our requests around what seems reasonable, what seems likely, what seems within the range of normal outcomes. And in doing so we pray to a God who is roughly the size of our imagination — which means we pray to a very small God indeed.

Jeremiah prayed from prison. The Babylonian army was surrounding Jerusalem. By every human measure the situation was not merely difficult — it was finished. The city was going to fall. The people were going to be taken captive. There was no political solution, no military option, no realistic path to a different outcome. And in the middle of that completely hopeless situation, God told Jeremiah to go and buy a field. In the territory that was about to be overrun by the enemy. With money that made no economic sense to spend. As an act of prophetic faith in a God who was saying: I am not done, and the land will be inhabited again.

Jeremiah obeyed. And then he prayed. And the prayer begins with the words that anchor this day: Ah, Lord GOD — it is You who have made the heavens and the earth by Your great power and by Your outstretched arm. Nothing is too hard for You. He starts with the bigness of God before he brings the impossibility of the situation. He establishes the character and the power of the One he is praying to before he describes what he needs. And the theological sequence matters — because when you start with the outstretched arm that made the heavens and the earth, the thing that felt impossible suddenly has a different frame around it.

Jesus said His house would be called a house of prayer. Not a house of preaching, not a house of music, not a house of programming. I love all of those things. But nothing replaces and nothing rivals the specific power of God's people crying out to Him together — bringing the things they have been carrying alone to the One who has been waiting for them to ask. The burdens that are too heavy for human resolution. The situations that have no earthly solution. The family member who seems beyond reach. The business that is hanging by a thread. The health report that the doctors cannot explain. The marriage that everyone else has written off.

None of these have surprised God for one second. He has seen them from before the foundation of the world. And He is the God who told Jeremiah to buy a field in the middle of a siege — because He was already working on the restoration that the present devastation could not prevent.

Bring Him the thing you have been carrying alone. Pray the prayer you have been afraid to pray because you were protecting yourself from the disappointment of a no. He is not small enough to be managed by your disappointment. He is the God of the outstretched arm. And nothing — nothing — is too hard for Him.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I confess that I have been praying small prayers because I have been protecting myself from the disappointment of a no. I have kept my requests within the range of what seems possible to me — which means I have been praying to a God roughly the size of my own imagination. Forgive me for that. Today I bring You the thing I have been carrying alone — the situation that by every human measure is beyond resolution, the prayer I have been afraid to pray. You made the heavens and the earth with Your outstretched arm. My situation has not surprised You for one second. Nothing is too hard for You. I believe that. Help me to pray like I believe it.

## Daily Practices

- Read Jeremiah 32:1–27 in full — the complete account of the field purchase and Jeremiah's prayer. Note the sequence: obedience first, then prayer. Jeremiah bought the field before he prayed about how impossible the situation was. Ask: is there a specific act of obedient faith that God is asking me to take before I fully understand how the outcome will work?
- Read Luke 18:1–8 — the parable of the persistent widow. Note that Jesus tells this parable specifically to teach His disciples that they should always pray and not give up. Ask: what is the prayer I have given up on that Jesus is calling me to bring back to the Father?
- Journal: "What is the specific impossible thing I have stopped asking God for because I was protecting myself from disappointment? What would it look like to pray that prayer again today — not with the desperation of someone who has nothing left, but with the confidence of someone who has read Jeremiah 32:17?" Write the prayer. Actually write it.
- Spend fifteen minutes in prayer specifically for the thing that by every human measure is beyond resolution. Do not manage the prayer or keep it within reasonable bounds. Pray like Jeremiah prayed — starting with who God is before you describe what you need. Let the bigness of God set the frame for the request.
