## Revelation

What did you put on this morning?

You stood in front of a closet and chose something, and you wouldn't think of leaving the house without it. Paul uses that picture in Colossians 3. A few verses earlier he says to take off the old life like dirty clothes: anger, wrath, malice, slander. Then he tells you what to wear instead: compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, patience. He says that you "put on" a new wardrobe, and that you choose it every day.

Then he adds the last piece: "above all these put on love."

I like how he describes it. Love is what binds everything together in perfect harmony. Think of a belt. You can have a nice shirt, nice trousers, even nice shoes, but if there's nothing holding it all together, you're going to have a problem by lunchtime. Without love, kindness turns into politeness and patience turns into gritting your teeth. You can do all the right things, and without love they become a performance.

Notice also that love is something you put on. It's a decision before it's a feeling. We tend to wait until we feel loving, and the feeling doesn't show up when the person in front of us is difficult. Jesus didn't wait until we were lovable. "While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."

You'll see where this goes. Yesterday we talked about forgiving. Forgiveness clears the ground, and love builds on it. The Spirit gives you what you don't naturally have.

So today, before you walk into your home, your church, or your workplace, picture yourself getting dressed on purpose. Who is hardest for you to love this week? The coworker, the relative, the neighbor with the barking dog? Pray, "Lord, I put on love toward this person today, because I can't produce it on my own." Then do one small, concrete thing: a kind word, a helping hand, or a text. Love is a verb, and it looks good on you.

## Morning Prayer

Father, thank You that You loved me before I was lovable. I can't produce this kind of love on my own, so today I put it on by faith. Hold together every other thing You are growing in me. Show me the one person who is hardest for me to love, and give me one small, real way to love them today.

## Daily Practices

- Read Colossians 3:12–14 slowly. List every piece of the "wardrobe" Paul names. Which one do you most often leave at home?
- Journal: "Who is hardest for me to love this week, and what is one concrete thing I can do for them?" Write it down and schedule it.
- Do that one thing today, quietly and without announcing it.
