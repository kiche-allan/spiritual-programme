## Revelation

You know one of the top reasons people give for not attending church? It is full of hypocrites. And honestly — they are right. Every church is full of people who do not live up to what they believe. Who say one thing and do another. Who preach grace and practise judgement. Who worship on Sunday and wound on Monday. Including you. Including me.

But here is the problem with that excuse — it reveals misplaced faith. It reveals that the person making the argument had placed their faith not in Jesus but in the people who claim to follow Him. And when the people failed — as people always eventually do — the faith built on them collapsed along with their credibility.

If your faith is in a pastor, you will be disappointed. Pastors will say something you took wrong, or maybe you took it right and they got it wrong. Either way they will let you down — not because they are exceptional failures but because they are ordinary human beings carrying the weight of an extraordinary calling. The most gifted pastor in the world is still a person — with blind spots, with wounds, with inconsistencies between their public theology and their private practice. Building your faith on them is building on sand that can shift.

If your faith is in a denomination, you will eventually be disillusioned. Institutions drift. Systems accumulate the corruption of the people who run them. Organisations that began with genuine spiritual fire can become bureaucracies that protect their brand more than they pursue their calling. The history of the church is full of movements that started in revival and ended in religion.

But Deuteronomy 32:4 gives us the description of the One on whom faith can actually be placed without eventual disappointment: He is the Rock. His works are perfect. All His ways are just. A faithful God who does no wrong. Every clause in that description is an absolute — not He is usually reliable, not His ways are generally just, not He is mostly faithful. The language is the language of the unqualified, the unconditional, the permanent. The Rock does not shift. The perfection does not have an exception clause. The faithfulness does not have an off day.

On Christ the solid rock I stand. All other ground is sinking sand. The hymn is not poetry — it is architecture. It is the description of the only foundation that does not eventually sink under the weight of what life places on it. Pastors sink. Denominations sink. Christian influencers sink. Movements sink. Organisations sink. The Rock does not.

The practical application is not to distrust every human being in the church. It is to audit where your faith is actually placed — to ask honestly whether the reason a specific failure shook you so badly is because you had placed more of your faith in the person who failed than in the God they were pointing to. If the fall of a leader causes you to question your faith in God, the faith you had in God was partly faith in the leader. The Rock is the only place your faith is safe.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I audit my faith today. Not the faith I claim to have — but the faith I actually practise, revealed by what shakes me and what does not. Where I have placed my confidence in people, in institutions, in leaders, in movements — and found it shaken when they failed — show me the misplacement. And draw my faith back to the only solid ground available: You, the Rock whose works are perfect, whose ways are just, who does no wrong.

Let me honour the people You have placed in my life without making them the foundation of my faith. Let me belong to a church without building on the church. Let me follow leaders without following them to the place where their failure becomes my crisis. On Christ the solid rock I stand. Let that be the actual architecture of my faith — not just the lyric I sing.

## Daily Practices

- Read Matthew 7:24–27 — the parable of the two builders. Note that both builders heard the words of Jesus. The difference was not information — it was foundation. Ask: in what specific area of my life am I currently building on sand — on a person, an institution, a feeling, a circumstance — rather than on the Rock?
- Read Psalm 62:1–8 in full — my soul finds rest in God alone; my salvation comes from him. He alone is my rock and my salvation. Note David's repeated use of alone — not primarily, not mostly, but alone. Ask: what would it mean for God to be my rock alone in the specific area where I have been most shaken by human failure?
- Journal: "Who or what has my faith actually been placed in — revealed by what has shaken me most? Where has a person's failure, a leader's fall, an institution's failure, or a movement's drift caused me to question God rather than simply question the person? What does it mean to rebuild that faith on the Rock rather than on the person who pointed to the Rock?" Write the honest audit.
- Read 1 Corinthians 3:1–15 — the complete passage on foundations and builders. Note verse 13: each person's work will be shown for what it is. The foundation is tested by fire — and only what is built on the right foundation survives the test. Ask: what in my spiritual life would survive the fire of testing and what would not?
