## Revelation

One of the most spiritually costly things that happens to single people in the church is the subtle, sometimes explicit, sometimes entirely unspoken message that the full expression of their calling is on hold until the marriage arrives. The assumption operates in different ways in different communities — sometimes it is the leadership structures that reserve the most significant roles for married people, sometimes it is the personal theology of the single person who has decided that the real chapter begins at the altar and that everything before it is the prologue. However it operates, the effect is the same: a generation of gifted, called, Spirit-filled young people spending their most formative years in a holding pattern, waiting for the relationship that will signal the beginning of the life they were made to live.

The biblical witness contradicts this at every turn. Jeremiah was called before he was born — and he was young and single when the call was activated. David was anointed before he was married — and the years between the anointing and the throne were the years in which the character was being formed that the throne would require. Daniel served with extraordinary faithfulness and influence in the court of Babylon before marriage is ever mentioned in his story. Esther saved a nation as a young woman whose marital status was the instrument of the saving rather than the precondition for the significance. Paul — by his own testimony — was single and regarded his singleness as a specific advantage for the ministry that consumed his life. And Jesus Himself, as we have already noted, lived the most purposeful and most fruitful life available without ever being married.

Romans 11:29 is the theological foundation for the calling that does not wait: for God's gifts and His call are irrevocable. Irrevocable. Not contingent on marital status. Not suspended during the season of singleness. Not fully activated only after the conditions that human convention considers prerequisite have been met. The gifts were given. The call was issued. And the irrevocability means that the waiting season is not a pause in the calling — it is a chapter of the calling, one in which the specific gifts and the specific calling can be expressed with a freedom and an undivided energy that the married season will not equally provide.

1 Timothy 4:12 gives us the direct instruction to the young person who has been told — explicitly or implicitly — that their age or their season disqualifies them from significant contribution: don't let anyone look down on you because you are young, but set an example for the believers in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith and in purity. The example is not set after the young person has arrived at the stage of life that commands respect. It is set now — in the speech and conduct and love and faith and purity of the current season. The example is the calling, and the calling is available now.

Mark 1:17–18 gives us the most important detail about the timing of the call to follow Jesus: at once they left their nets and followed Him. The disciples did not finish what they were doing first. They did not wait until their circumstances were more conducive to the following. They left at once — mid-task, mid-season, without the conditions that might have seemed prerequisite for the following being in place. The call of Jesus does not wait for the right conditions. It interrupts the current conditions and invites the immediate response of the person who hears it.

Your ministry does not begin at the altar. It began at conversion — at the moment the gifts were given and the call was issued and the irrevocable commitment of God to work through your specific life was activated. The altar is a chapter of the ministry, not its beginning. And the chapter before the altar is not the prologue. It is full text — as significant, as purposeful, as genuinely part of the story as any chapter that follows.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the irrevocability of the call — the gifts that were given and the calling that was issued and that do not wait for the conditions that human convention considers prerequisite. I repent of treating the current season as the prologue to the real chapter. This is the real chapter. The calling is active now. The gifts are available now. The example that 1 Timothy 4:12 calls me to set is the example of the current season — in speech, in conduct, in love, in faith, in purity — and I am not too young and not in the wrong season to set it.

Let me leave the nets today. At once. Whatever I have been in the middle of that I have been using as the reason to defer the following — let me leave it and follow. The call does not wait for the conditions to be right. I am following now.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Timothy 4:12–16 in full — the complete instruction to Timothy about not allowing anyone to look down on his youth and about the specific practices that make the example credible: reading Scripture publicly, preaching, teaching, not neglecting the gift, being diligent in these matters. Ask: what is the specific gift I have been neglecting in the current season because I have been treating the season as a waiting room rather than a ministry context?
- Read Esther 4:14 — and who knows but that you have come to your royal position for such a time as this. Note that Esther's most significant act of ministry happened before her marital situation was what she might have wanted it to be. Ask: what is the such a time as this of my current season — the specific moment for which I have been specifically prepared and which requires my specific response right now?
- Journal: "What is the specific calling or gift I have been deferring to a future season — waiting for the marriage or the stability or the right conditions before I pursue it fully? What would it look like to activate that calling now, in the current season, with the specific freedom and energy that the current season provides?" Write the honest exploration and then write the first concrete step toward activation.
- Identify one specific way you will express your calling this week — one act of service, one use of a gift, one contribution to the body of Christ that reflects the calling you have been carrying rather than deferring. Let it be specific rather than aspirational. And let it be the beginning of the at once rather than the continuation of the waiting.
