## Revelation

The week closes with the three stories held together in a single frame, and what you see when you hold them together is more powerful than any one of them alone. The Samaritan on the road, Job in the ash heap, Esther in the palace — three completely different circumstances, three completely different kinds of person, three completely different expressions of purpose. And yet every principle Myles Munroe identified runs through all three with the consistency of a thread through a tapestry.

Purpose preceded all three of them. The Samaritan's compassion was embedded in him before he took the road to Jericho. Job's capacity for an encounter with God in the depths was inside him before the sabeans stole his oxen. Esther's access, her courage, her relationship with Mordecai — all of it was in place before Haman was appointed. None of them chose their purpose in the moment of its expression. They stepped into what was already true about them. Purpose is discovered not invented, and in all three cases the discovery happened not in comfort but in the moment of demand — the bleeding man on the road, the whirlwind, the sealed decree. Their passions pointed to their purposes. The Samaritan could not pass by. Job could not stop wrestling with God, which itself was evidence of a relationship with God too deep to be severed by suffering. Esther could not remain silent. And in all three cases, the purpose was entirely in service of others. The man on the road. The watching generations who would read Job's testimony. An entire people scheduled for extinction.

Psalm 139:16 says that all the days ordained for you were written in God's book before one of them came to be. This is not determinism that eliminates your choices — it is sovereignty that precedes your arrival and encompasses everything that follows. The question is not whether your life has a purpose. The question is whether you will live from it. Philippians 1:6 seals the confidence: He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion. The work was begun before you were born. It will be completed on the other side of your death. You are living in the middle of a story whose Author has already written the ending — and the ending is the full expression of everything He placed in you before you arrived.

You were not an accident. You were not a coincidence. You were not a product of circumstance stumbling through a random universe looking for meaning. You were known before you were formed, purposed before you were born, and equipped before you arrived with everything necessary for the specific role you were created to fill. The question that remains — the only question that remains — is what you will do with what you have been given.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week in the posture that all three of these stories have been building toward — not the posture of someone who is still searching for their purpose as if it might not exist, but the posture of someone who is learning to uncover what was already placed inside them before they were born.

I receive today the full testimony of this week. The Samaritan showed me that purpose is discovered in what moves me. Job showed me that purpose survives the fire and is sometimes only revealed by it. Esther showed me that purpose is always for such a time as this — always connected to a specific moment, a specific need, a specific people who require exactly what God placed in me. And Myles Munroe gave me the framework to hold it all together.

I am not an accident. My days were written in Your book before one of them came to be. The good work You began in me before my birth You will carry to completion. I choose today to live from the centre of that truth — not from the opinions of others, not from the pressure of circumstances, not from the fear of failure, but from the unshakeable conviction that I was created on purpose, for a purpose, to serve a purpose that will outlast me.

Let that be enough. Let that be everything.

## Daily Practices

- Review your journal from the whole week. Across all seven days and all three stories, what single sentence captures what God said to you about your purpose? Write it down. This is your testimony from this week — keep it somewhere you will return to.
- Write your own answer to Myles Munroe's five principles as they apply specifically to your life: What did God place in you before you were born? What is your purpose pointing toward based on what moves you without your permission? Who is your purpose for? What problem are you specifically equipped to solve? And where have you been pursuing success without purpose?
- Share something from this week with someone in your life. The word of your testimony is a weapon. The themes of this week — purpose, compassion, suffering, courage — are things people around you are carrying questions about right now. Be generous with what God has given you.
- Read Psalm 139 in full as your closing meditation and your sending declaration. Let it be the last word of the week. You are known. You are purposed. You are sent. Go and live accordingly.
