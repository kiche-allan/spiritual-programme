## Revelation

Iron sharpens iron through friction. This is not a comfortable metaphor. The sharpening of a blade requires contact, resistance, and the deliberate application of pressure. The edge that emerges from the sharpening is not the edge that went in — something has been removed, something has been refined, and the process required the blade to be held firm while another hard surface worked against it. Proverbs 27:17 uses this image deliberately. The person who sharpens you is not the person who agrees with everything you say. They are the person who loves you enough to press against you — who sees the dull places and is willing to create the friction that produces the edge.

Most believers avoid this. We have developed a sophisticated vocabulary for maintaining the appearance of community while ensuring that no one ever gets close enough to see what actually needs sharpening. We share the victories and manage the defeats. We present the version of ourselves that is already sharp. And the result is that we remain dull — not because God has not placed sharpening people in our lives, but because we have kept a safe enough distance that the friction never happens. Accountability requires proximity. And proximity requires vulnerability. And vulnerability requires trust. And trust requires time and consistency and the willingness to be known before you are ready to be known.

Paul's relationship with Timothy is the New Testament's clearest portrait of what discipleship — the deepest form of community — actually looks like. It was not a programme. It was a life. Timothy travelled with Paul, watched him work, heard him pray, saw him suffer, received his letters, was entrusted with his most important assignments. 2 Timothy 2:2 gives us the principle in its most compressed form: the things you have received, entrust to reliable people who will entrust them to others. Discipleship is not the transmission of information. It is the passing on of a life. And it requires the kind of proximity that information transfer does not — because what is being transmitted is not a set of doctrines but a way of being in the world.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I want to be the kind of person who can be known. Not known in the curated, managed way that presents only what is ready to be seen, but known in the full, uncomfortable, vulnerable way that actually allows another person to speak into the dull places. I confess that I have sometimes preferred the safety of superficial community over the cost of genuine accountability. I have managed my image within the body of Christ rather than submitting my reality to it.

Show me today who You have placed in my life to sharpen me — and show me who I am called to sharpen. Let me not avoid the friction that the sharpening requires. Where I have been keeping my distance from the people who could speak most truthfully into my life, give me the courage to close that distance. And where I have been holding back from speaking honestly and gently into another person's life, give me the love and the courage that genuine care requires.

Let me be iron that sharpens and is willing to be sharpened.

## Daily Practices

- Read 2 Timothy 1:1–7 and 3:10–17 — Paul's affectionate, honest letters to Timothy. Note the depth of the relationship they describe. Timothy knew Paul's teaching, conduct, purpose, faith, patience, love, endurance, and sufferings. That is proximity. Ask: is there anyone in your life who knows you at that depth? If not, what is preventing it?
- Journal: "Who is currently sharpening me — pressing against me in love in ways that produce growth? And who am I currently sharpening? If the answer to either question is no one, what does that tell me about the quality of my community?" Be honest.
- Read Galatians 6:1–5 in full. Note that the instruction to restore gently comes with a warning — watch yourself, or you also may be tempted. Accountability is not a position of superiority. It is mutual vulnerability. Ask: what would it look like for me to practise this kind of gentle, watchful restoration with someone in my community right now?
- Take one practical step toward deeper accountability today. Name a person you trust, and ask them specifically to hold you accountable in one area of your life that you know needs sharpening. Not a general request for prayer — a specific, named accountability that creates the friction that produces the edge.
