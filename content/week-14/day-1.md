## Revelation

There is a kind of devotional writing that speaks about trials in the abstract — generalised, softened, applicable to everyone and therefore specific to no one. This is not that kind of devotion. This week begins by naming what is actually being carried, because Scripture itself does not speak in vague generalities about suffering. It speaks about health crises, about families torn apart, about job loss, about financial desperation, about marriages under strain, about rejection that cuts to the bone. The Bible is not embarrassed by specificity, and neither should we be.

If you are reading this and your body is failing you — a diagnosis you did not expect, a pain that will not resolve, a test result you are waiting on — Psalm 34:18 says the Lord is close to the broken-hearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit. Not close to those who have already recovered. Close to those who are currently crushed. The nearness of God is not contingent on your circumstances improving first. It is available now, in the crushing, before anything has changed.

If your family has fractured — through conflict, through distance, through a relationship that has not been the same in years — you are not alone in this either. 2 Corinthians 4:8–9 gives us four pairs that describe exactly this kind of compounding pressure: hard pressed but not crushed, perplexed but not in despair, persecuted but not abandoned, struck down but not destroyed. Paul is not describing a single clean crisis. He is describing the layered, compounding nature of real suffering — pressure on every side, confusion without clear answers, the feeling of being struck down again just as you were getting up. And in every pairing, the second half is not the absence of the first. It is the presence of God inside the first.

If you have lost a job, or are facing financial need that keeps you awake at night, Psalm 55:22 gives the clearest instruction available: cast your cares on the Lord and He will sustain you. The word cast is not a gentle suggestion. It is the language of throwing something with force — taking the full weight of what you are carrying and physically hurling it onto Someone who is strong enough to bear it. This is not denial of the crisis. It is the deliberate, repeated, sometimes desperate act of refusing to carry alone what was never meant to be carried alone.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with the specific crisis I am carrying — not a vague sense of difficulty, but the actual weight of what is pressing on me right now. Whether it is my health, my family, my work, or my need, I bring it to You by name rather than hiding it behind generalities.

I receive today that You are close to the broken-hearted — not after the breaking has resolved, but in the middle of it. I receive that I can be hard pressed without being crushed, perplexed without being in despair, struck down without being destroyed — because You are the difference between those two halves of every pairing. And I cast my care on You today — not once, but as many times as it returns, because You have promised to sustain what I throw onto You.

Let me not carry today what You have already offered to carry for me.

## Daily Practices

- Name your specific crisis in writing today — not generally, but specifically. What exactly is happening in your health, your family, your work, or your need? Do not minimise it and do not exaggerate it. Write it as it actually is.
- Read 2 Corinthians 4:7–18 in full. Note that Paul does not deny the reality of the pressure — he names it specifically in verse 8–9 before moving to the eternal perspective in verses 16–18. The comfort does not come from pretending the crisis is smaller than it is.
- Journal: "What have I been carrying that I have not yet fully cast onto God? Have I been managing it privately, presenting a composed face while quietly being crushed underneath?" Be honest.
- Practise the casting today — not as a one-time prayer but as a physical, deliberate act. Write your specific cares on paper if it helps, and as you pray, imagine physically throwing the weight of each one toward God. Repeat this whenever the weight returns.
