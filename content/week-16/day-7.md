## Revelation

Five encounters have shaped this week. Monday and Tuesday established what it means to know God and to be known by the God who makes Himself known — the relationship that is eternal life itself, the divine initiative that has always preceded the human response. Wednesday brought us to the Person who is the peace — not the principle or the promise but the Prince, who meets us in the storm rather than waiting for the storm to end. Thursday sat us at the table where bread was broken and wine was poured and feet were washed — the covenant sealed in blood, the remembrance that does not let the church forget what it cost. Friday left us at the well, listening to a woman tell us that she left her water jar behind — the surest sign that a genuine encounter with Jesus had replaced what she came for with something she had never expected to find. And Saturday turned our faces to the wall with Hezekiah, weeping, praying without composure, and discovering that the God who heard before Isaiah had left the building is the same God who hears today.

The thread running through all five encounters is the same thread. It is not a doctrine, though all five encounters are doctrinally rich. It is not a discipline, though all five encounters involve the practice of prayer and attention and honesty. The thread is a Person — the same Person who walked in the garden in the cool of the day, who called Abraham His friend, who met a woman at a well at noon in Samaria, who washed feet and broke bread in an upper room in Jerusalem, who burst through the wind of the storm to meet the disciples on the water, who heard the prayer of a weeping king before the prophet had left the middle court.

The Person is always the same. The encounters are all different. But what they produce in everyone who has them is the same — a life that has been changed not by information received but by proximity experienced. The disciples who walked with Jesus for three years were not primarily the people who had heard the most teaching. They were the people who had been most with Him — who had sat at His table, who had watched Him sleep in the storm, who had seen Him stop at a well in the middle of nowhere to talk to the wrong person. Their lives were changed by the accumulation of encounters with a Person who was present rather than distant, specific rather than general, genuinely interested in the people He was with rather than merely available to them.

Psalm 27:4 gives us the distilled desire of a person who has understood what the whole week has been building toward: one thing I ask from the Lord, this only do I seek — that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, to gaze on the beauty of the Lord and to seek Him in His temple. Not many things. One thing. The dwelling, the gazing, the seeking — the continuous, unhurried, undistracted proximity to the One who is the source of everything the week has explored. The peace is found in proximity. The bread and cup are received in proximity. The living water wells up in proximity. The prayers are heard in proximity. Everything the week has offered flows from the one thing — dwelling, gazing, seeking, being with the Person who is the point.

Revelation 3:20 closes the week with the image it has been building toward all along. Here I am. I stand at the door and knock. The God who walked in the garden, who called to Moses from the burning bush, who had to go through Samaria, who sat with His disciples in the upper room, who heard Hezekiah before Isaiah left the building — this same God is standing at the door of your specific life right now, knocking, waiting to be invited further in. Not to inspect. Not to correct. To eat with you, and you with Him. The meal at the table. The friendship of the vine and the branches. The proximity that changes everything.

Open the door a little wider today than it was at the beginning of this week.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week with the thread that has run through all five days — not a doctrine but a Person, not information but encounter, not religion but relationship. You are the God who walked in the garden and is still walking. Who called Abraham friend and is still calling. Who met a woman at a well at noon and is still meeting. Who broke bread with His friends on the most important night in history and is still at the table. Who heard a weeping king before the prophet left the room and is still hearing.

I receive today the one thing David asked for — to dwell in Your house all the days of my life, to gaze on Your beauty, to seek You in Your temple. Let this be the organising desire of my life — not the many things I am pursuing simultaneously, but the one thing that, when it is present, makes everything else either possible or unnecessary.

I hear You knocking. I open the door. Come in and eat with me.

## Daily Practices

- Read the five anchor passages from each day of this week in sequence — Philippians 3:10, Hebrews 1:1–2, Isaiah 9:6, Luke 22:19–20, John 4:13–14, Isaiah 38:2–3, and John 15:15. Read them slowly as a single meditation on the Person who has been present in each encounter. Notice what the cumulative effect is of reading them all together.
- Journal: "Which of the five encounters this week spoke most directly to where I am right now — and why? What did it say to me specifically that I want to carry forward?" Write the specific word rather than the general impression.
- Read Psalm 27 in full — the psalm of the one thing. Note how the psalm moves through genuine fear — the enemies, the armies, the foes — and returns again and again to the one thing: the Lord is my light and my salvation, the Lord is the stronghold of my life. The one thing is the answer to every specific fear the psalm names.
- Write a letter to God today — not a prayer in the conventional sense, but an actual letter. Tell Him what this week has meant. Tell Him where you are in the relationship. Tell Him what you want more of. Let the letter be the beginning of the ongoing conversation that the friendship requires.
