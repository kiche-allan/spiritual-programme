## Revelation

Before there was a world, there was community. The Trinity — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — existed in perfect, eternal, self-giving relationship before a single atom was spoken into being. When God said in Genesis 1:26, let us make mankind in our image, the us was not a grammatical quirk. It was a window into the nature of the God who was doing the making. You were made by a community, in the image of a community, for community. The isolation that modern culture presents as freedom — the curated, self-sufficient, digitally mediated existence that needs no one and is accountable to no one — is not freedom. It is a slow amputation of something essential to what you are.

The early church in Acts 2 is the most vivid portrait in Scripture of what Christian community looks like when it is functioning as designed. They devoted themselves — the Greek word is proskartereo, which means to persist in, to attend constantly to, to hold fast — to the apostles' teaching, to fellowship, to the breaking of bread, to prayer. Four things, in constant, persistent, communal practice. And the result was not merely a pleasant social environment. Everyone was filled with awe. Signs and wonders were performed. People were added daily. The community was not the nice extra on top of the real Christian life. The community was the environment in which the real Christian life became possible.

Hebrews 10:24–25 adds the most urgent dimension: not giving up meeting together, as some are in the habit of doing — and all the more as you see the Day approaching. The writer is connecting two things that might seem unrelated: the habit of gathering together and the imminent return of Christ. The approaching Day is not an argument for withdrawal and private spiritual preparation. It is an argument for more community, not less. The person who is genuinely watching for the return of the King lives more deeply invested in the body of Christ, not less — because the Day is coming when the body will be complete, and everything done in community now is preparation for the eternal community that is coming then.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I confess that I have sometimes treated the Christian life as a private project — something to be pursued alone, in my own way, on my own terms, with You as my only accountability. I have sometimes used the language of personal faith to justify the avoidance of the vulnerability and cost that genuine community requires. Forgive me. You made me for belonging, and I have sometimes preferred the safety of isolation to the risk of being truly known.

Show me today what You designed community to be — not the superficial gathering of people who sit in the same room once a week, but the devoted, persistent, self-giving fellowship of people who are genuinely for one another. Show me where You are calling me to invest more deeply in the body of Christ around me. Show me who needs me to spur them on today, and who I need to allow to spur me.

Let me not be someone who gives up meeting together. Let the approaching Day make me more present to my community, not less.

## Daily Practices

- Read Acts 2:42–47 in full. Note the four things they devoted themselves to and the outcomes that followed. Ask honestly: which of these four — teaching, fellowship, breaking of bread, prayer — is least present in your current experience of Christian community? What would it look like to devote yourself more fully to that one?
- Journal: "What is my honest relationship with Christian community right now? Am I genuinely invested in a specific body of believers, or have I been treating church as optional or supplementary?" Name the specific people God has placed around you who constitute your community.
- Read 1 Corinthians 12:12–27 — the body of Christ metaphor. Note that Paul says the parts of the body that seem weaker are indispensable. Ask: where in the body of Christ am I functioning, and where have I been absent?
- Identify one specific act of community you can practise today — not in theory but in action. A phone call to a believer you have been meaning to connect with. A message of genuine encouragement. A commitment to show up somewhere you have been avoiding. Do it today.
