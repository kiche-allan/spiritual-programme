## Revelation

There is a version of the Christian life that is entirely correct and entirely empty. It has the right theology, the right church attendance, the right vocabulary, the right moral behaviour — and yet the Person at the centre of all of it remains, in some deep and unacknowledged sense, a stranger. The doctrines are known. The story is known. The commands are known. But the Person — the living, present, specific, speaking, pursuing Person of Jesus Christ — is known about rather than known. And the gap between these two things is the gap between religion and relationship, between information and encounter, between a faith that can be explained and a faith that has been experienced.

John 17:3 is one of the most compressed and most important definitions in the New Testament: this is eternal life — that they know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom you have sent. Eternal life is not defined here as going to heaven when you die, though that is included. It is not defined as the forgiveness of sins, though that is foundational. It is defined as knowing God. The knowing is the life. The relationship is the inheritance. What you are being saved into is not primarily a destination but a Person — and the quality and depth of that knowing is what gives the Christian life its substance.

The Greek word Jesus uses — ginosko — is the word for intimate, experiential, relational knowledge. Not the knowledge of a textbook but the knowledge of a friendship. Not the knowledge that describes someone accurately but the knowledge that knows them from the inside — their character, their voice, their ways, their heart. Paul uses the same word in Philippians 3:10, and he uses it with an ache: I want to know Christ. This is Paul — who had a direct encounter with the risen Christ on the Damascus road, who had been caught up to the third heaven, who had written more of the New Testament than anyone else — and he is still pressing toward more knowing. Still aching for a depth of relationship with Christ that has not yet been fully reached.

This is the invitation at the beginning of this week. Not to know more about God — you may already know a great deal about Him. But to know God Himself, more deeply, more honestly, more intimately than you currently do. Jeremiah 9:24 says this is the one legitimate boast available to a human being — not wisdom, not strength, not wealth, but the understanding to know God. The knowing itself is the treasure.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I begin this week with the ache that Paul expressed in Philippians 3 — the ache for more knowing. Not more information about You, though I want that too. More of You Yourself. The experiential, intimate, relational knowing that John 17:3 says is eternal life. I want that to be the defining characteristic of my life — not what I know about You, but the depth to which I actually know You.

Show me today where the gap is between my knowledge about You and my knowledge of You. Where I have been satisfied with information when You were offering encounter. Where I have been managing a religious life when You were offering a genuine friendship.

Draw me closer. I want to know You.

## Daily Practices

- Read Philippians 3:7–14 in full. Note that Paul counts everything he previously considered gain — his religious pedigree, his blameless law-keeping, his Pharisaic credentials — as loss compared to the surpassing worth of knowing Christ. Ask: what have I been counting as spiritual gain that might actually be a substitute for the knowing itself?
- Journal: "What is the honest state of my knowing of God right now? If I am being completely truthful, is it primarily a knowing about or a knowing of? What would it take to move from one to the other?"
- Read Jeremiah 9:23–24 in full. Note what God says He delights in — kindness, justice, and righteousness on earth. Ask: do I know the God who delights in these things, or do I know facts about a God who delights in them? Is there a difference in how I live?
- Spend five minutes today in silence before God — no words, no list, no agenda. Simply be present before the One who is already present before you. Notice what that silence feels like and what, if anything, surfaces in it.
