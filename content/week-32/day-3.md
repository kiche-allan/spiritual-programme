## Revelation

When someone you love is grieving, do you know what to say?

Most of us freeze. We walk into the funeral home and we don't know what to do with our hands or our words. So we say something awkward, like "He's in a better place" or "Everything happens for a reason," or we say nothing at all and stay away.

Look at what Paul does here. He has just finished describing the most dramatic event in human history. The Lord Himself descending from heaven with a shout. The voice of the archangel. The trumpet of God. The dead in Christ rising first, and those of us who are alive being caught up together with them to meet the Lord in the air.

And then he gives an assignment. He doesn't say, "Now make a chart about it." He doesn't say, "Now argue about it." He says, "Encourage one another with these words."

The return of Jesus was never meant to be just a doctrine we debate. It was given to us to comfort hurting people. It's meant to be spoken out loud at a hospital bedside, at a graveside, and at a kitchen table when the chair across from you is empty. Paul wrote this passage because the Thessalonians were grieving, and he didn't want them grieving "as others do who have no hope."

Notice he says "these words." Not your words. His words. You don't have to be eloquent. You don't have to have all the answers about why it happened. You just need to point people back to the promise. Jesus is coming. The grave doesn't get the last word. We will be together again, and we will always be with the Lord.

Those are words worth repeating.

So who in your life needs to hear them today? Maybe it's a widow who has been alone for a while now, and everybody else has moved on. Maybe it's a friend who just lost a parent. Call them. Write them a note. Read them 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17. Then just sit with them. God will use His Word to do what your words never could.

## Morning Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You that the grave does not get the last word. Thank You that You are coming back and that we will be together again. Give me the courage to walk toward people who are grieving instead of away from them. Put Your words in my mouth, and when I have no words, let me simply stay and sit with them.

In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18 aloud. Notice that Paul gives the promise first and the assignment second.
- Journal: "Who in my life is grieving right now, and has been forgotten by everyone else?" Write their name and one thing you will do for them this week.
- Call or write to that person today. Share 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17, and then listen more than you talk.
