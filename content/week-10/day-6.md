## Revelation

The call to holy living is the most repeated moral command in all of Scripture — and the most misunderstood. For most people, holiness is primarily a list of things you do not do: the prohibitions, the abstentions, the careful navigation of what is off-limits. This version of holiness is exhausting, joyless, and ultimately ineffective, because it is trying to produce from the outside what can only grow from the inside. Biblical holiness has an entirely different starting point. It begins not with a list but with a Person — and not with your effort to become like that Person but with your encounter with who that Person is.

Titus 2:11–13 gives us the most important insight into the relationship between grace and holiness. Grace is not the thing that makes holiness optional. Grace is the thing that makes holiness possible — and more than that, it is the thing that teaches us to say no to ungodliness. The grace of God has appeared, and it teaches. It is an active instructor. It does not simply forgive your sin and leave you unchanged. It works in you the capacity to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in this present age — while you wait. The waiting is not passive. The life you live while you wait is itself an expression of your expectation. A person who genuinely believes that Jesus is coming lives differently from a person who does not. Not because they are performing religion but because the orientation of their life has fundamentally changed.

Romans 12:1–2 reveals what holy living actually looks like at its core: offering your body as a living sacrifice and being transformed by the renewing of your mind. This is not a list. It is a posture. The body offered — your whole physical, daily, ordinary existence given to God as an act of worship. The mind renewed — not by willpower but by the ongoing work of the Holy Spirit in a person who has chosen to be shaped by God's Word rather than by the world's patterns. And the outcome is not a person who has successfully avoided a sufficient number of prohibited activities. It is a person who is visibly, unmistakably, increasingly different in a way that the world cannot explain apart from God.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with the whole of my ordinary life — the habits, the patterns, the choices I make when no one is watching, the ways I spend my time and my attention and my energy. I offer all of it to You as a living sacrifice. Not the spectacular acts of devotion alone, but the mundane ones. The morning. The screen. The conversation. The choice.

Let the grace that has appeared in Christ Jesus be the teacher of my daily life. Not just the forgiver of my failures but the active instructor of my choices. Teach me to say no — not through gritted teeth and willpower but through the genuine, growing sense that what You are offering me is so much better than what I am being asked to give up that the comparison is almost embarrassing.

And transform my mind. Where the patterns of the world have shaped my thinking more than Your Word has, renew them. Not by my effort alone but by Your Spirit working in a person who is genuinely, daily, presenting themselves before You with open hands.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Peter 1:13–25 in full — Peter's extended treatment of the call to holiness. Note the motivation he gives: you were redeemed with the precious blood of Christ. Holiness is not the condition of redemption — it is the response to it. Let the order matter to you today.
- Identify one specific area of your life where the pattern of the world has been shaping you more than the Word of God. Name it — do not keep it in the abstract. Then bring it to Romans 12:2 and ask: what would the renewing of my mind look like in this specific area?
- Read Titus 2:11–14 as a description of your own life rather than a theological principle. The grace has appeared. It is teaching you. You are waiting for the blessed hope. Let each phrase be a mirror — where does your current life reflect this portrait, and where does it not?
- Journal: "If someone who did not know me observed my daily life for one week — my habits, my entertainment, my speech, my use of time — would they see a person living in holy expectation of the coming King? What would they see that confirmed it? What would they see that contradicted it?"
