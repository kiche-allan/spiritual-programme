## Revelation

Does God really expect you to thank Him for everything?

Read the verse carefully. Paul doesn't say "give thanks for all circumstances." He says, "give thanks in all circumstances; for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus for you." One small word makes a big difference.

You don't have to thank God for the funeral. You don't have to thank Him for the betrayal, the diagnosis or the retrenchment letter. God isn't asking you to call evil good or to pretend a bad thing is a blessing. That would be dishonest, and it would be cruel to people who are grieving.

But in the funeral, you can thank Him that He is close to the brokenhearted. In the betrayal, you can thank Him that He never leaves. In the long wait, you can thank Him that He is not finished. Thanksgiving doesn't deny the pain. It looks past the pain to the One who is still on the throne.

Notice also that it's "the will of God." This isn't a suggestion for the naturally cheerful. It sits right between "rejoice always" and "pray without ceasing," a three-part rhythm for every believer. And remember who wrote it. Paul wasn't sitting in comfort. He had been beaten, shipwrecked, jailed and misunderstood. He knew what he was talking about.

Romans 8:28 gives us the reason it's possible: "all things work together for good, for those who love God." Not all things are good. But in His hands, all things are being worked towards good. That is what lets us give thanks while the work is still in progress.

Anxiety and thanksgiving can't live in the same sentence for long. Philippians 4:6 says, "do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God." Notice again: in everything.

So think of the hardest situation you're in right now. Don't thank God for the pain. Ask yourself: "What can I thank Him for in the middle of this?" Write down three things. Start with the fact that He is with you. Then say them to Him, even if your voice shakes.

## Morning Prayer

Father, You know the hard place I am in. I won't pretend it is good, and I won't hide my pain from You. But in the middle of it, I thank You that You are here, that You have not left, and that You are still working. Teach me to give thanks in all circumstances, and hold me while I learn.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Thessalonians 5:12–24. Notice that the thanksgiving command sits among very practical instructions for living together.
- Journal: "What is the hardest thing in my life right now? What can I thank God for in the middle of it, not for it?" List three things.
- If someone you know is in a hard season, send them one encouraging message today that points them to God's nearness.
