## Revelation

John 20:21 contains the most important sentence in the Great Commission. It is not Matthew 28:19 — go and make disciples. It is this: as the Father has sent me, I am sending you. The sending of the disciples is not merely analogous to the sending of the Son — it is patterned on it. The same kind of sending. The same quality of commission. The same indwelling Spirit who enabled everything Jesus did now breathed into the disciples to enable everything they would do. This is not a delegation of tasks. It is a participation in mission. You are not executing a programme that Jesus designed and handed off. You are continuing the work that He began and never stopped being part of.

The eleven weeks of this programme have been building to this Sunday. Week by week, the formation has been going deeper — fresh starts and new beginnings, the fear of God, confession and repentance, the resurrection, identity and purpose, spiritual disciplines, trust, providence, prayer, submission, holy living. All of it inward. All of it foundational. All of it necessary. But formation was never the destination. It was always the preparation. The person who has been formed by prayer knows how to sustain a witness through difficulty. The person who has been formed in identity knows who they are when the world tells them otherwise. The person who has been formed in purpose knows why they are going. The person who has been formed in community knows they are not going alone. Formation was always for mission. The inward work was always in service of the outward sending.

2 Corinthians 5:20 gives us the most breathtaking description of what the sent life actually is: we are Christ's ambassadors, as though God were making his appeal through us. An ambassador does not speak in their own name or carry their own message. They represent the one who sent them, speak the words of the one who commissioned them, carry the authority of the kingdom they come from. And Paul says this is what you are. Not what you might become after sufficient training. What you already are — by virtue of the Spirit you have received, the identity you have been given, the commission you have been handed. God is making His appeal to the world through you. The question Isaiah answered — whom shall I send, who will go — is being asked again. And the answer that changed the world is available to you today: here I am. Send me.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week in the posture of sending. Not the anxious, unprepared, self-reliant sending of someone who is not sure they have what it takes — but the confident, Spirit-filled, community-supported, armour-wearing, victory-standing sending of someone who has spent eleven weeks being formed for exactly this.

As the Father sent You, You are sending me. I receive that today — not as a burden but as the highest privilege available to a human being. To be the means through which the God who made the world makes His appeal to the world. To carry in my ordinary, daily, imperfect life the extraordinary message that reconciliation is possible, that the war is over, that the King is coming, and that anyone who comes to Him will not be turned away.

Here I am. Send me. To my Jerusalem first — to the household, the workplace, the neighbourhood where my witness is most needed and most available. And then further, wherever You lead. I am formed. I am sent. I am not alone. Let us go.

## Daily Practices

- Read Isaiah 6:1–8 in full — the complete account of Isaiah's commissioning. Note the sequence: he saw God's glory, he was undone by his own unworthiness, he was cleansed, and then — only then — he heard the question and answered it. The sending follows the formation. Ask: what has God been forming in you across these eleven weeks that has prepared you for the sending you are now receiving?
- Review your journal from the whole week. Write one sentence that captures what God said to you about community, one about spiritual warfare, and one about the Commission. These three sentences together describe the sent life you are being called into.
- Write out the names from your Jerusalem map again — the people who do not yet know Jesus who are in your immediate relational world. Commit to praying for them daily for the next thirty days. Not a vague commitment — write the names in your journal, set a reminder, and keep the commitment.
- Read 2 Corinthians 5:17–21 as your sending declaration. You are a new creation. The old has gone. You have been given the ministry of reconciliation. You are Christ's ambassador. God is making His appeal through you. Read it aloud. Then go and live it.
