## Revelation

The contemporary church has largely replaced discipleship with church attendance — and the replacement has been so gradual and so complete that most people within the church do not notice the difference. They attend services, participate in programmes, give to the offering, serve in rotas, and call this following Jesus. But when Jesus called people to follow Him, He was not describing any of these things. He was describing something that looked like the relationship between a rabbi and his talmidim in first-century Judaism — a relationship in which the student left what they were doing, went where the teacher was going, learned by watching the teacher live rather than only by hearing the teacher speak, and was being formed into the kind of person the teacher was, not merely informed about the things the teacher knew.

Luke 14:27–28 gives us the version of the call to discipleship that the church's marketing department would never have approved: whoever does not carry their cross and follow me cannot be my disciple. Suppose one of you wants to build a tower — won't you first sit down and estimate the cost? Jesus is explicitly inviting His listeners to count the cost before committing. Not to discourage them from committing — but to ensure that the commitment they make is the genuine, eyes-open, cost-counted commitment of someone who has understood what they are agreeing to, rather than the enthusiastic but uninformed response of someone who has heard the benefits without hearing the requirements. The cross that discipleship requires is not the dramatic cross of martyrdom that most people will never face. It is the daily cross of Luke 9:23 — the daily denial of the self-life in favour of the Christ-life, the daily carrying of the specific weight that following Jesus in the specific circumstances of the specific life produces.

Matthew 28:19–20 gives us the commission that the costliness of Luke 14 is in service of: go and make disciples. Not go and make converts. Not go and increase church attendance. Make disciples — people who are being formed into the image of Christ through the sustained, intentional, costly process of following Him. The teaching them to obey everything I have commanded is the content of discipleship — not the transmission of information about what Jesus said, but the formation of people who are learning to do what Jesus did, in the way that Jesus did it, from the same motivation that drove Jesus. This is the commission. And it is a commission that requires the commissioning person to be a disciple themselves — not merely a teacher of doctrine but a person who is genuinely following Jesus and can therefore show others what the following looks like from the inside.

John 8:31–32 gives us the definition of discipleship that Jesus Himself offers — and it is both more demanding and more liberating than most versions: if you hold to my teaching, you are really my disciples. Then you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free. The really is significant. It implies that there is a discipleship that is nominal — a relationship with Jesus that is real enough to be called belief but not sustained enough to be called holding. The holding is the key word — the sustained, consistent, daily continuing in the teaching that produces the knowing, and the knowing that produces the freedom. The freedom of John 8:32 is not the freedom of a person who has been told something liberating. It is the freedom of a person who has been formed by sustained engagement with the truth — who has held to the teaching long enough and consistently enough that the truth has become the operative reality of their life rather than a proposition they have affirmed.

The transformation that discipleship produces is not available through any other mechanism. Church attendance can inform. Programmes can organise. Serving can engage. But the specific formation of the character of Christ in the life of the believer — the patience, the humility, the love, the courage, the wisdom that comes from following Someone rather than merely admiring them — can only be produced by the actual following. The disciples who turned the world upside down were not the people who had heard the best sermons about Jesus. They were the people who had been with Jesus — who had watched Him pray, watched Him heal, watched Him eat with sinners, watched Him rebuke the Pharisees, watched Him weep at the tomb, watched Him endure the cross, watched Him rise. The watching and the following and the doing produced what the hearing alone could not.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the call to discipleship in its actual form — not the version that fits comfortably into a busy schedule and asks nothing that disrupts the existing arrangement of my life, but the version that Jesus described to the crowds on the road. The cross-carrying version. The cost-counting version. The holding-to-the-teaching version. The version that produces the freedom of John 8:32 because it has sustained the engagement long enough for the truth to become the operative reality rather than the affirmed proposition.

Let me be a disciple today — not merely a person who attends, gives, serves, and knows the right things. A person who is genuinely following — going where You are going, watching how You live, being formed by the proximity into the kind of person You are making me rather than merely the kind of person I am managing to be through my own effort.

The cost is real. I have counted it. Let me carry the cross today and follow.

## Daily Practices

- Read Luke 14:25–33 in full — the complete passage on the cost of discipleship. Note the three images Jesus uses: the cross, the tower, and the king going to war. Each requires prior calculation. Ask: have I genuinely counted the cost of the discipleship I have committed to — or have I committed to a version of following Jesus that does not yet include the cross He described?
- Read Mark 3:13–14 — the calling of the Twelve. Note the specific purpose: He appointed twelve that they might be with him and that he might send them out to preach. Being with Him comes before the sending. The formation precedes the mission. The proximity produces what the mission requires. Ask: is my current relationship with Jesus primarily one of being with Him, or have I moved to the sending without sufficient time in the being with?
- Journal: "What is the specific cross I am being called to carry in my current season — the specific cost that genuine discipleship is asking of me right now? Have I counted that cost honestly, or have I been avoiding the counting because the cost is higher than I want to pay?" Write the honest reckoning.
- Identify one specific way your discipleship has been functioning as attendance rather than following — one area where you have been informing yourself about Jesus rather than actually following Him into the specific thing He is doing. Take one concrete step today toward the following rather than the attending.
