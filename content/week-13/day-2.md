## Revelation

The most revealing word in Luke 9:23 is the one that is easiest to pass over: daily. Take up your cross daily. Not at the moment of dramatic persecution. Not in the season of intense spiritual warfare. Daily — on the unremarkable Tuesday when nothing significant is happening, when the inbox is full and the schedule is ordinary and the day looks exactly like yesterday and the day before. Jesus did not say take up your cross in the crises. He said take it up every single day. The cross is not a crisis response. It is a daily posture.

This reframes everything about what spiritual maturity looks like. We have a tendency to measure our spiritual life by its high points — the powerful worship experience, the answered prayer, the moment of breakthrough, the season of intense growth. But the texture of genuine discipleship is not primarily high points. It is the accumulated, unremarkable, daily decisions of a person who has taken up their cross again this morning and is following again today. The high points are the rewards and the confirmations. The undramatic days are the actual substance of the spiritual life.

Daniel is the clearest portrait of this in the Old Testament. He prayed three times a day toward Jerusalem for decades. Not three times a day when the lions were coming. Three times a day whether anything significant was happening or not. The discipline that made him capable of the extraordinary response to the lions' den was built in the ordinary responses of thousands of unremarkable days when no one was watching and nothing dramatic was at stake. When the crisis arrived, he did not have to decide to pray. He already was praying. The crisis simply revealed a character that had been formed long before the crisis required it.

Colossians 2:6–7 gives us the agricultural image that captures this best: rooted and built up. Roots do not grow in dramatic moments. They grow slowly, invisibly, in the unremarkable process of daily nourishment and daily anchoring. The tree that stands through the storm is not the tree that had the most dramatic growing season. It is the tree whose roots went deepest during the ordinary seasons — when the weather was mild enough that the roots could grow without the distraction of survival. The ordinary day is the root-growing day. Give it the same intentionality you give the dramatic ones.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You on what may be an ordinary day — not a crisis, not a high point, just a Tuesday. And I receive it as exactly what it is: a root-growing day. A day for the unremarkable faithfulness that forms the character that will sustain me when the unremarkable days end and the extraordinary ones begin.

I take up my cross today. Not dramatically — quietly, specifically, deliberately. I deny the part of me that wants to coast because nothing urgent is demanding my best. I commit to giving the ordinary day the same attentiveness I would give a crisis. Because the person I am on the ordinary days is the person I will be on the extraordinary ones.

Let me be faithful today in what is small, in what is unseen, in what will not be celebrated or noticed. Let me trust that labour in the Lord is never in vain — even when it is invisible, even when it produces no immediately visible fruit, even when today looks exactly like yesterday. The roots are growing.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Kings 19:1–8 — Elijah under the broom tree, burned out after the extraordinary victory on Carmel. Note that God's response to his exhaustion is not another dramatic spiritual experience. It is bread and water and sleep. The angel touches him and says: the journey is too much for you. Ask: what does God's care for Elijah's ordinary physical needs say about the importance of the ordinary?
- Read Daniel 6:10 and sit with the phrase just as he had done before. Daniel's extraordinary response to the lions' den was simply the continuation of what he had always done. Ask: what are the spiritual practices I am maintaining consistently right now — regardless of whether anything dramatic is at stake — that will form the character I need for the crises I cannot yet see?
- Journal: "What does my ordinary Tuesday look like spiritually? Not the Sunday, not the retreat, not the crisis moment — the regular Tuesday. What does that day reveal about the actual state of my faith?" Be honest rather than aspirational.
- Practise the discipline of the ordinary today: pray at a fixed time even if you do not feel like it, read Scripture even if nothing seems to land, serve someone in a small way that no one will notice. Let the ordinary day be a full expression of your faith rather than a pause between the significant ones.
