## Revelation

Do you ever feel like a work in progress? Like there are areas of your life that are still rough, still unfinished, still not what you know they are supposed to be — and the gap between where you are and where you sense God is taking you feels wider than you have the strength or the time to close?

Good news: you are a work in progress. And even better news: you are not the one responsible for finishing the job.

Paul writes to the Philippians with a confidence that is worth examining carefully — not the vague optimism of someone who hopes things will improve, but the specific, grounded, theologically precise confidence of someone who has understood something fundamental about how God works. He who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion. He started it. He will finish it. Your salvation was not your idea — you did not wake up one morning and decide to pursue God out of the pure goodness of your own heart. He reached down, He initiated, He drew you. And the One who initiated the work has not handed the project over to you to complete on your own strength.

That ought to take enormous pressure off.

The Psalm captures the same truth with the tenderness of a prayer: do not abandon the works of your hands. The works of His hands — that is what you are. Not a problem to be solved or a project to be managed or a case file to be reviewed. The work of His hands — crafted, shaped, held. And the God who does not abandon His creative work in the physical world does not abandon His redemptive work in the spiritual one. He is not going to walk away from what He started in you. The unfinished places, the areas that still need growth, the struggles you keep bringing back to Him with a weariness that comes from bringing them so many times before — He is still working on all of it.

1 Thessalonians 5:24 gives us the theological foundation underneath the promise: the One who calls you is faithful, and He will do it. The faithfulness is the key. The completion of the work is not conditional on your consistency or your performance or your ability to maintain the standard. It is conditional on His faithfulness — which does not waver, does not diminish, does not get tired of the project or impatient with the pace of the progress. He called you, He began the work, and He is faithful to complete what He has committed Himself to completing.

You may not be who you want to be yet. But you are not who you used to be either. And you are not yet who He is going to make you. The journey is not finished. The work is not complete. But it is in the most faithful hands available — the hands that made the heavens and the earth, the hands that were stretched out on the cross, the hands that are even now working in the unfinished places of your life with a patience and a precision that exceeds anything you could produce by trying harder.

He started it. He is still working. He will complete it. Count on it.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the truth that I am a work in progress — and that the progress is Your responsibility, not mine alone. I have been carrying the weight of my own sanctification as if the completion of the work depends entirely on my effort and my consistency. It does not. You who began a good work in me are faithful to carry it to completion. I bring You today the unfinished places — the areas that still need growth, the patterns that are still being broken, the character that is still being formed. Do not abandon the works of Your hands. I trust the faithfulness of the One who calls me. You started this. You will finish it.

## Daily Practices

- Read Philippians 1:1–11 in full — the complete opening of the letter including verse 6 in context. Note that Paul is writing to real people with real struggles and real unfinished areas. Ask: what is the specific unfinished area of my life where I most need to receive today the promise that He will carry the work to completion?
- Read Romans 8:29–30 — those He foreknew He also predestined, called, justified, glorified. Note the sequence — it ends in glorification, which is future but stated in the past tense as if it is already accomplished. Ask: what does it mean for my confidence today that the God who began the work has already seen its completion?
- Journal: "What is the specific area of my life where I have been carrying the weight of completion on my own — trying harder, performing better, managing the gap between who I am and who I am supposed to be through my own effort? What does Philippians 1:6 say specifically to that area today?" Write the honest exploration.
- Read Hebrews 12:2 — Jesus is the author and perfecter of faith. He is both the One who writes the story and the One who brings it to its perfect ending. Ask: what would change about how I approach my unfinished areas today if I genuinely believed that the author of my faith is also its perfecter?
