## Revelation

The rabbis had a saying that the eye and the hand are the two handmaidens of sin. What you see, and then what you reach out and grab hold of — that is how sin happens. It begins in the eye and it ends in the hand. And Jesus, who understood the mechanics of temptation better than any theologian who has ever lived, built His instruction around this exact sequence. If your right eye causes you to stumble, gouge it out. If your right hand causes you to stumble, cut it off.

He was not being dramatic. He was being honest — more honest than most of us are willing to be about what the battle actually costs and what winning it actually requires.

The problem with most of us is that we approach temptation passively. We allow ourselves to be exposed to the things we know stir up desires that do not honour God. We linger at the edge of the fire and then act surprised when we get burned. We justify a little compromise here — just this one scene, just this one conversation, just this one website, just this one relationship that we know has already begun moving in a direction it should not go. And we tell ourselves that we are strong enough to manage it, disciplined enough to keep it contained, spiritual enough to get close without catching. And before long we are in deeper than we ever intended to be, and the thing we told ourselves we were managing is now managing us.

Dietrich Bonhoeffer wrote that temptation and lust, when fully developed, robs the mind of clear discernment and decision-making. He was right. The evidence is everywhere. Gifted leaders whose ministries collapsed because they refused to cut off what needed to be cut off. Marriages that ended because someone decided the fire was manageable until it was not. Futures that were derailed because the compromise that started small was never dealt with ruthlessly while it was still small enough to deal with.

The word ruthless is the right word. Jesus is not calling you to be vaguely more careful about what you expose yourself to. He is calling you to be ruthless — to identify with absolute honesty the specific thing that gives sin its access to your life and to remove it without sentimentality, without negotiation, without the five-point plan to gradually reduce your exposure over the next six months. Cut it off. Gouge it out. Get out of that relationship. Delete the app. Put the guardrails in place before the next temptation arrives rather than in the middle of it.

This is not about fear. It is about love — the specific love of a God who thinks too highly of what He has put in you to watch you stay comfortable in a compromise that is slowly costing you everything He designed you for. He is not trying to take something from you. He is trying to protect something in you that the thing you are holding onto is destroying.

Let Him show you what needs to go. And trust that what He has for you on the other side of the ruthlessness is worth far more than what you are being asked to release.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with honesty about the specific areas where I have been passive about temptation — where I have allowed exposure to things I know stir up desires that do not honour You, and told myself I could manage what I have no business entertaining. Give me the courage to be ruthless today where I have been lenient. Show me the eye and the hand — the specific thing I am seeing and the specific thing I am reaching for — that is giving sin its access to my life. I do not want to cut off something good. I want to cut off something that is cutting me off from what You have for me. Give me the clarity to see it and the courage to deal with it.

## Daily Practices

- Read Matthew 5:27–30 in full — the complete passage on the eye and the hand. Note that Jesus places this instruction in the middle of the Sermon on the Mount, in the context of the higher righteousness of the kingdom. Ask: what specific thing in my life right now is the eye or the hand that Jesus is asking me to deal with ruthlessly?
- Read James 1:13–15 — temptation conceives desire, desire gives birth to sin, sin gives birth to death. Note the progression: it is a sequence, not an event. Ask: where in the sequence am I currently? Have I identified the thing before it moves to the next stage?
- Journal: "What am I currently exposing myself to that I know is stirring up desires that do not honour God — and what is the specific ruthless action that Jesus is calling me to take about it today? Not next week. Today." Write the honest answer and the specific action.
- Read 1 Corinthians 6:18 — flee sexual immorality. Note the word flee — not manage, not moderate, not gradually reduce. Flee. Ask: is there something I have been managing that I was actually called to flee?
