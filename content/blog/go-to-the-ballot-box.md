Where are you putting your hope for our country?

Let me start with something simple. Go and vote. As a child of God, you have a responsibility to vote your values, and those values come from the Bible. Kenya will hold its next general election on 10 August 2027, according to the Independent Electoral and Boundaries Commission. Continuous voter registration has been running since September 2025, so check with the IEBC whether it is still open. If you haven't registered, do it while you can. If you have, check your details and make sure you are on the register for the polling station you will actually use. Then, on the day, get up early and go.

I say that because the numbers should bother us. In 2022, turnout was about 65 per cent, which means roughly one in three registered voters stayed at home. Some of those were believers who told themselves that politics is dirty and that Christians should stay out of it. But the Bible never tells God's people to withdraw from the places where they live. Through the prophet Jeremiah, God told the exiles in Babylon, "Seek the welfare of the city where I have sent you into exile, and pray to the Lord on its behalf, for in its welfare you will find your welfare." And Proverbs reminds us that "Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a reproach to any people." What happens in a country affects the church, the family and the workplace. A believer who does not care about any of it is not being holy. He is being absent.

Voting as a Christian also means voting differently. Your vote is not for sale. The Law of Moses is clear: "You shall take no bribe, for a bribe blinds the clear-sighted and subverts the cause of those who are in the right." A few hundred shillings at a rally, a bag of sugar or a promise of a job is not a gift. It's a price, and the people who pay it expect to be owed. Your vote is also not an inheritance from your tribe or your village. It is a stewardship, and one day you will answer for it. How you vote is between you and the Lord. Let His Word shape the values you carry to the ballot box, and let your conscience, and not the loudest voice on the stage, make the final decision.

But here is something just as important. God is not a member of any party. He is not in a coalition, a community or a political movement. He is God.

Never think that your vote, or anyone's vote, will create a move of God. That's not how it works. Kenya has seen elections that raised enormous hopes, and it has also seen elections that ended in violence. Neither outcome could do what only God can do. The only thing that brings real change to a village, a county, a church or a nation is when God's people do God's business God's way, seeking His face.

Look at the verse everyone knows: "If My people who are called by My name humble themselves, and pray and seek My face and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven and will forgive their sin and heal their land." These words were spoken to Solomon after he dedicated the temple, and they were first addressed to Israel. We apply them with care, but the principle has never changed: this is how God deals with His people. Notice that He doesn't address the government, the opposition or the culture. He says, "If My people." Notice also the four things He asks for. Humble yourselves. Pray. Seek My face. Turn from your wicked ways. None of those happens at a polling station. All of them happen on our knees.

The healing of the land starts in the house of God. If the church is full of bitterness, tribalism, corruption and prayerlessness, we can't expect the nation to be any different. And if the church repents, it won't be long before the nation feels it.

So what do we do between now and August 2027? We register, and we help others register. We pray for those in authority, because Paul told Timothy to make "supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings" for "kings and all who are in high positions, that we may lead a peaceful and quiet life, godly and dignified in every way." We pray without ceasing, as the same apostle told the Thessalonians. We refuse to share rumours and hatred in our family and church WhatsApp groups. And we pray that, when the day comes, Kenya will vote in peace, count in peace and accept the result in peace.

There is much to grieve, in our country and in the world around us. I pray that God will visit us once more before Jesus comes. That will happen when His children pray, humble themselves and turn.

So go to the ballot box. Then go to your knees.
