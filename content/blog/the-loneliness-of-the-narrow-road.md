There is a specific kind of discouragement that does not look like temptation. It does not arrive as desire. It arrives as comparison — and comparison, when it comes to sexual purity, is rarely a neutral measuring tool. It is almost always a quiet argument that your obedience has been pointless.

It tends to happen the same way every time. You have been fighting a real battle — by grace, with real cost, often invisible to anyone around you — and then someone hands you a story designed, intentionally or not, to make your faithfulness look like foolishness. They tell you what others are doing. They tell you what they themselves are doing. They frame their compromise as freedom and, without saying it directly, frame your restraint as deprivation. And something in you that has been standing firm for months or years suddenly feels unsteady — not because the temptation got stronger, but because the meaning of your resistance got smaller in your own eyes.

This is worth addressing directly, because it is one of the most common and least discussed struggles among believers who are genuinely trying to live a holy sexual life.

## The Comparison Is Not Evidence

The first thing to recognise is that someone else's reported behaviour is not data about whether your conviction is correct. Numbers have never been God's measure of whether a thing is right. Matthew 7:13–14 tells us this directly: wide is the gate and broad is the road that leads to destruction, and many enter through it. But small is the gate and narrow the road that leads to life, and only a few find it. Jesus told us in advance that the majority pattern would not be the faithful one. If you are looking for the comfort of numbers to validate your obedience, He already told you that you would not have it — and that the absence of company was never meant to be read as evidence that you had chosen wrongly.

There is also a simpler observation worth making: people often exaggerate their conquests, especially to someone they perceive as more disciplined or more restrained than themselves. The exaggeration inflates the teller and unsettles the listener — and that effect, whether intended or not, tells you something about the conversation itself, regardless of whether the underlying claims are even true. Even when the claims are entirely accurate, someone else's choice to indulge is not an argument against your choice to abstain. It is simply a different choice, with different and often delayed consequences.

## What Comparison Is Actually Doing

What is happening in this moment of discouragement is subtle and worth naming precisely. The temptation is not primarily toward the sin itself in this instant — it is toward a reinterpretation. The temptation is to start seeing your obedience as deprivation rather than gift, and someone else's disobedience as freedom rather than danger. This inversion is old. It is the same question the serpent posed in the garden — did God really say — now dressed up as did God really mean it, when everyone else seems to be enjoying what you are denying yourself.

Galatians 6:7–9 names the inversion directly: do not be deceived. Whoever sows to please their flesh, from the flesh will reap destruction; whoever sows to please the Spirit, from the Spirit will reap eternal life. Let us not become weary in doing good, for at the proper time we will reap a harvest if we do not give up. The harvest language matters here. What is being sown right now, in secret, under real pressure, with no audience and no applause, is a seed. Seeds do not look like anything for a long time. The absence of visible reward today does not mean nothing is happening underground.

## It Was Never Your Willpower

The believer who has managed, by grace, to hold the line in a season of real pressure already knows something important even if they have not articulated it: the holding was never primarily their own strength. 1 Corinthians 10:13 promises that God will not let His people be tempted beyond what they can bear, and will always provide a way out. This means that every single time the line held, that was not an accident of personality or a triumph of unaided willpower. That was God actively at work, sustaining a battle that would otherwise have been lost long ago. The temptation to feel foolish for the cost of obedience often forgets this — that the obedience itself was a gift, not merely an achievement to be second-guessed under social pressure.

## What the Obedience Is Actually Doing

Sexual purity, held under real pressure, is not absence. It is worship. It is the body, in the area where it is hardest to govern, saying to God: I trust You more than I trust my own urgency, and more than I trust the approval of people pursuing a different definition of freedom. 1 Thessalonians 4:3–5 describes sanctification as including the control of one's own body in a way that is holy and honourable — not in passionate lust like the pagans, who do not know God. That final phrase is significant. The pursuit of every opportunity without restraint is not, in the biblical framing, the behaviour of someone who has found freedom. It is the behaviour of someone operating without the knowledge of God shaping their desire. The believer who is restraining themselves, even imperfectly, even under pressure, even while wavering, possesses something the indulgent comparison cannot offer: a desire that is being shaped by knowing God, even when that shaping is costly.

The pressure itself is not a sign of failure or of missing out. It is a sign that the obedience is costing something real — which is precisely what makes it meaningful. Joseph's resistance to Potiphar's wife in Genesis 39 was not remarkable because the temptation was weak. It was remarkable because she was relentless, he was alone, and no one would have known if he had given in. The text does not hide that it was difficult for him. It simply shows that he ran anyway.

## What to Do With the Discouragement

The most dangerous thing a person can do with this kind of discouragement is carry it alone. Isolation metabolises discouragement badly — it tends to curdle into resentment, and resentment tends to curdle into the question of why not just do it too. The discouragement needs to be brought honestly to God, even angrily if that is where the heart currently is. He is not offended by the honest admission that obedience feels unfair in this particular season.

Psalm 73 is built entirely around a believer who is staring at the apparent prosperity of those who are not living faithfully, and who very nearly loses his footing over it. He says plainly: I envied the arrogant when I saw the prosperity of the wicked. The psalm does not resolve by explaining why the unfaithful seem to be doing fine. It resolves with a different kind of conclusion entirely: as for me, it is good to be near God. The nearness itself is the reward being pointed to — not vindication, not an explanation, not even necessarily a visible outcome in this life. Nearness.

The person sowing to the flesh today is not building something that lasts. They are spending something that cannot be recovered, and the true cost of that spending is rarely apparent until much later — sometimes far later than expected. The person walking the narrower road is building something the New Testament repeatedly describes as rare and valuable precisely because so few choose it: a life that says no to the loudest demand of the body, out of love for God, even when that road feels lonely. It is narrow because few walk it. Not because it leads nowhere.

## A Final Word

If you are the person who recognised your own story somewhere in this post, you are not behind, and you are not the fool in this particular narrative — whatever the comparison in your head is currently insisting. The line you have held by grace was never wasted, even on the days it felt unbearable and unacknowledged. Bring the discouragement to God honestly. And if it is heavy, bring it also to someone further along the road than you are — a pastor, an older believer, someone who has walked further and can remind you, patiently and repeatedly, that this is worth it.

## A Prayer

Father, I bring to You the discouragement that comparison produces — the moments when faithfulness feels foolish because someone else's compromise has been dressed up as freedom. Remind me that the road being narrow was never evidence that I chose wrongly. It was Your own warning, given in advance, that the majority pattern would not be the faithful one.

Thank You that the obedience I am holding by grace has never actually been my own strength. You have been the One sustaining it, providing the way out before I asked for it, in every moment the pressure felt unbearable. Let me trust that what is being sown in secret is not being wasted, even when the harvest is not yet visible.

Keep me near You. Not vindicated. Not proven right in front of anyone. Just near. That is enough.

In Jesus' name, Amen.
