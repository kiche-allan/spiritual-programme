## Revelation

Monday established the human side of knowing God — the ache, the longing, the pressing toward more. Tuesday must establish the divine side, because the knowing of God is only possible because God has first made Himself known. The initiative in the relationship has always been His. Before you ever sought Him, He was seeking you. Before you turned toward Him, He had already turned toward you. Before the ache for knowing was in you, the desire to be known was in Him.

Hebrews 1:1–2 gives us the full sweep of this divine initiative across history: at many times and in various ways, God spoke. The speaking began immediately — in the garden, in the dreams of patriarchs, in the burning bush, in the still small voice on Horeb, through the mouths of prophets across centuries. God has never been silent. He has never hidden Himself so completely that no one could find Him. The entire history of Scripture is the documented record of a God who initiates, who speaks, who reveals, who pursues — at many times, in various ways, through every available channel.

And then — the climax of all of it — the Son. In these last days He has spoken by His Son. The incarnation is the most complete act of divine self-revelation in the history of the universe. John 1:14 gives us the astonishing declaration: the Word became flesh and made His dwelling among us. The Greek word for dwelling is skenoo — to pitch a tent, to tabernacle. The God who had dwelt in the tabernacle of Israel, behind the veil, accessible only through the priests and only on prescribed terms — that same God pitched His tent in human flesh and walked among His people in a way that was touchable, visible, audible, and fully knowable to every person who encountered Him.

Exodus 34:6–7 records the most direct self-description God ever gave — the moment when Moses asked to see His glory, and God passed in front of him proclaiming His own name and character: the Lord, the compassionate and gracious God, slow to anger, abounding in love and faithfulness, maintaining love to thousands, forgiving wickedness, rebellion and sin. This is God describing Himself. Not what others have observed about Him — what He says about His own character. Compassionate. Gracious. Slow to anger. Abounding in love. Faithful. Forgiving. This is what you are getting to know when you know God. Not a distant sovereign whose ways are entirely mysterious. A Person who has described Himself in terms that make Him genuinely knowable — and who went as far as becoming flesh to ensure that the knowing was available to everyone.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the truth that the knowing I am aching for is something You have been pursuing from Your side for longer than I have been pursuing it from mine. You spoke at many times and in various ways. You sent prophets and dreams and burning bushes and still small voices. And then, when none of that was enough, You sent Your Son — the full, complete, embodied, touchable, audible self-revelation of everything You are.

Let me receive the revelation You have already given. Let me not treat the incarnation as familiar when it is the most extraordinary event in history — that You became flesh, pitched Your tent among us, walked where we walk, wept where we weep, and made Yourself fully knowable so that the knowing I am aching for would genuinely be available to me.

Show me Your glory today, the way You showed it to Moses. Let the character You proclaimed over him — compassionate, gracious, slow to anger, abounding in love and faithfulness — become the foundation of how I know You rather than a description I have heard without yet fully receiving.

## Daily Practices

- Read Hebrews 1:1–4 in full. Note the specific description of the Son in verse 3 — the radiance of God's glory and the exact representation of His being. Ask: what does it mean that Jesus is the exact representation of God's being? How does this change how you read the Gospels?
- Read John 1:1–18 slowly. Note especially verse 18 — no one has ever seen God, but the one and only Son has made him known. The word translated made him known is exegesato — the root of our word exegesis, to explain, to unfold, to bring out the full meaning. Jesus exegetes the Father. He unfolds what God is like in a form human beings can understand.
- Journal: "What has God been doing to make Himself known to me specifically — in my own history, in my own life? Where can I see the divine initiative — the times when God was clearly moving toward me before I was moving toward Him?" Write the specific moments.
- Read Exodus 34:4–8 — the full account of God's self-proclamation to Moses. Sit with each word of the description in verses 6–7. For each quality named, ask: do I experience God this way? If not, which quality is hardest for me to receive personally?
