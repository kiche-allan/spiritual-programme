## Revelation

Jeremiah did not want the calling. That is the detail that most readings of Jeremiah 1 pass over too quickly — the fact that the most comprehensive statement of divine intentionality in the prophetic literature was spoken to a person who immediately responded to it with: I do not know how to speak; I am too young. The declaration before I formed you in the womb I knew you was not spoken to a person who felt adequate, prepared, or obvious as the recipient of a divine appointment. It was spoken to a person who felt entirely unqualified — young, inarticulate, and overwhelmed by the gap between what God was saying about him and what he felt about himself.

This is the context that makes Jeremiah 1:5 most relevant to the person who needs it most. Not the person who already feels purposeful and designed and significant. The person who has grown up without the affirmation that confirms significance. The person who was not told they were wanted, was not made to feel remarkable, has spent years in the ordinary without any clear signal that the ordinary was building toward something. The person who looks at the people around them who seem to have been formed for obvious purposes and wonders what their purpose is — whether there is one — whether the silence on the subject means the answer is no.

Before I formed you in the womb I knew you. The knowing precedes the forming. God's knowledge of Jeremiah was not acquired through observation of the person Jeremiah became. It was prior to the forming — which means it was not based on Jeremiah's performance, his character, his obedience, or his adequacy for the task. It was the knowing of a Creator who had already decided what He was making before the making began. The purpose was in the mind of God before it was in the life of Jeremiah. The appointment was declared before the prophet existed to be appointed.

Before you were born I set you apart. The setting apart — the Hebrew qadash, to sanctify, to consecrate, to dedicate to a specific purpose — happened before birth. Not at conversion. Not at the moment of spiritual maturity. Not after Jeremiah had demonstrated sufficient faithfulness to merit the consecration. Before he was born. Which means the consecration is not contingent on anything Jeremiah does. It is the prior decision of a God who knew what He was making and what He was making it for before the making began.

Psalm 139:13–14 gives us the intimate detail of how the knowing and the forming actually worked: you knit me together in my mother's womb. The image of knitting is the image of deliberate, patient, detail-attentive work — the specific crafting of each part by a God who was not mass-producing but individually forming. Every person is a specific act of divine attention. The inmost being — the seat of personality, the character, the specific combination of gifts and sensitivities and capacities that makes each person irreducibly themselves — was created by God, knitted together by God, formed in the place where only God could see. Fearfully and wonderfully made. The fearfully is the word that speaks of the awe-inspiring nature of the work — the kind of work that, when you see it properly, produces awe rather than indifference. You are that kind of work.

Ephesians 2:10 gives us the destination of the forming: we are God's handiwork — the Greek word is poiema, from which we get the word poem — created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do. The good works were prepared in advance. Not improvised after the fact. Not developed in response to how the person turned out. Prepared in advance — which means the life you are living has works in it that were prepared before you were born to do them. The purpose is not something you create. It is something you discover — something that was already there, already prepared, already waiting for the person who was being knitted in the womb to arrive at the moment of doing it.

You are not an accident. You are a poem. You are knitted. You are known before forming. You are set apart before birth. You are created for works that were prepared before you existed to do them. The doubt that says otherwise is not humility. It is the failure to receive what God has declared about the person He made.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the declaration that precedes every doubt about my purpose and my worth — before You formed me in the womb, You knew me. Before I was born, You set me apart. Before I existed to be adequate or inadequate, the purpose was already in Your mind and the works were already being prepared. Let me receive today the identity that was established before I had any opportunity to earn or lose it. I am Your handiwork. I am fearfully and wonderfully made. Let me know that full well — not as a theological position but as the lived, settled, unhesitating confidence of a person who has genuinely received what their Creator has declared about them.

## Daily Practices

- Read Jeremiah 1:1–10 in full — the complete call of Jeremiah. Note his objection and God's response to it: do not say I am too young — I will be with you. Ask: what is the specific version of I am too young that I have been using as a reason not to receive the purpose God has declared over my life?
- Read Psalm 139 in full — all twenty-four verses. Let it be a personal address rather than a theological study. Note every specific dimension of the knowing: the sitting and rising, the thoughts, the going out and lying down, the words, the path, the inmost being, the days written in the book. Ask: which dimension of God's knowing of me is hardest to receive and why?
- Journal: "What is the specific lie I have believed about whether my life has a purpose behind it — the specific doubt that the silence of circumstance or the absence of affirmation has produced? What does Jeremiah 1:5 say directly to that specific lie?" Write the honest engagement and then write God's declaration over the doubt.
- Read Ephesians 2:8–10 — grace, faith, and the handiwork in their full context. Note that the good works were prepared in advance for us to walk in — the walking is the human participation in what was already prepared. Ask: what are the specific good works that feel most prepared for me — the things I do that feel least like effort and most like what I was made for?
