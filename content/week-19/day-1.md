## Revelation

The church has become comfortable with God. This sounds like progress — like the fruit of a gospel that has successfully communicated grace, acceptance, and the nearness of a Father who welcomes His children. In many ways it is. But there is a kind of comfort that has crossed a line — that has moved from the settled confidence of a child who knows they are loved into the casual familiarity of a person who has forgotten who they are standing before. And when that line is crossed, something essential is lost — not the love, not the grace, not the access — but the awe. And without the awe, worship becomes entertainment, prayer becomes conversation with an equal, and the God who is worshipped bears increasingly little resemblance to the God who is actually there.

Isaiah 6 is the corrective. In the year that King Uzziah died, Isaiah saw the Lord — high and exalted, seated on a throne, the train of His robe filling the temple. The seraphim covered their faces with two wings and their feet with two wings and used the remaining two to fly. They did not look directly at God. The beings who live in the immediate presence of God, who have never sinned and have nothing to be ashamed of, cover their faces before Him — not from guilt but from the sheer weight of a holiness that exceeds what any created being can sustain without covering. And they cry to one another in the language of the superlative: holy, holy, holy. In Hebrew, repetition is the grammar of emphasis. Something said twice is important. Something said three times is the most important thing that can be said. Holy, holy, holy is the only triple declaration in the entire Bible. It is the highest possible statement of the most important attribute of God.

The word holy — qadosh in Hebrew — means set apart, other, different in kind from everything that is not God. When applied to God, it is not primarily a moral category — though it includes perfect moral purity. It is an ontological category. It describes the sheer otherness of God — the fact that He exists in a category entirely His own, that there is nothing in creation that can be compared to Him without the comparison being immediately misleading, that the distance between the greatest created thing and God is infinitely greater than the distance between nothing and the greatest created thing. He is not a bigger, better, more powerful version of what we are. He is categorically other. And the appropriate response to that otherness, when it is genuinely encountered, is not casual familiarity. It is the response of Isaiah: woe to me — I am ruined — for I am a man of unclean lips, and my eyes have seen the King, the Lord Almighty.

Isaiah's response is not neurotic self-deprecation. It is accurate self-knowledge in the presence of perfect holiness. The same light that reveals the glory of God simultaneously reveals the true condition of the person standing in it. There is no middle ground in the presence of holiness. You either cover your face with the seraphim or you misunderstand what you are looking at. And yet — and this is the grace that Isaiah 6 could not have anticipated before the cross — the same God whose holiness undoes the prophet also sends the seraph with the coal to touch the lips and declare: your guilt is taken away and your sin atoned for. The holiness that exposes is also the holiness that cleanses. The God before whom no flesh can stand is also the God who makes standing possible through the blood of His Son.

Revelation 4:8 tells us that the four living creatures — the beings closest to the throne of God — have never stopped crying holy, holy, holy. Day and night, without ceasing, across the entire span of heaven's existence. This is not obligation. This is not a liturgical duty assigned to them by a God who needs the reassurance of their praise. This is the spontaneous, unceasing, inexhaustible response of beings who see clearly what they are looking at. The holiness of God is not a doctrine to be filed in the theology section of the brain. It is a reality so vast, so weighty, so entirely consuming that the only adequate response, for beings who see it directly, is unceasing worship.

Hebrews 12:28–29 gives us the practical implication for the believer who has received the kingdom that cannot be shaken: worship God acceptably with reverence and awe, for our God is a consuming fire. The fire is not a threat to the person who has been cleansed by the blood of Christ. It is the description of a reality — the same fire that consumes everything that is not of God purifies everything that is. The consuming fire is what makes genuine holiness possible in the believer — not a comfortable negotiation with sin, but the burning away of what does not belong, in the presence of the One who is holy and who is making His people holy.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today with the recovered awareness of who You actually are — not the God I have sometimes reduced to a manageable, comfortable, domesticated presence, but the God before whom the seraphim cover their faces, whose holiness fills the earth with glory, who is a consuming fire that nothing impure can survive and nothing genuine can resist. I do not come in terror, because the blood of Your Son has made me clean. But I come in reverence and awe — the appropriate posture of a creature before its Creator, of a sinner before the Holy One who has made holiness available to them at infinite cost.

Let the fear of the Lord be the beginning of my wisdom today. Not the fear that drives me away but the awe that draws me near — the awe of someone who has seen enough of who You are to know that nothing else is worth worshipping, and that the worship of the Holy One is the most significant activity available to a human life.

Holy, holy, holy — Lord God Almighty. Let that be the truth I build on today.

## Daily Practices

- Read Isaiah 6:1–8 in full — slowly, as though for the first time. Note every specific detail of the vision: the throne, the train of the robe, the seraphim, the shaking of the doorposts, the smoke. Note Isaiah's response and the seraph's response to the response. Ask: when did I last feel the weight of the holiness of God in a way that undid me before it commissioned me?
- Read Revelation 4 in full. Let the imagery be as vivid as the text intends it to be — the rainbow, the sea of glass, the living creatures, the unceasing worship. This is not allegory. This is the present reality of what is happening in heaven right now. Ask: how does this picture change the way I understand the worship I bring?
- Journal: "Where have I allowed familiarity with God to cross into a casualness that has reduced my awe? What would it look like to recover the fear of the Lord — not as terror but as the appropriate reverence of a person who has actually reckoned with who they are standing before?"
- Read Proverbs 9:10 — the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom. Ask: in which specific area of my life do I most need the wisdom that only the fear of the Lord produces? Bring that area before God today with the reverence that genuine wisdom requires.
