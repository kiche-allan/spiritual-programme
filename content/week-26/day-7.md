## Revelation

The week has addressed the season of singleness with the specific, pastoral, honest attention that the season deserves. The purpose behind the forming, the dignity of the waiting season, the identity that precedes and outlasts every relational status, the discipline of guarding the heart, the calling that does not wait for the altar, the prayer that holds the desire honestly and releases it openly. All of it has been addressed to the specific experience of the person in the waiting season, with the specific challenges and the specific graces that the season uniquely provides.

But the closing day is not addressed to the waiting season specifically. It is addressed to the whole life — the life that is whole and flourishing and fully alive regardless of its relational status. Because the most important truth about the season of singleness is not that it is a season to be endured or even a season to be redeemed. It is that it is a season in which the full life that Jesus came to give — the life to the full of John 10:10 — is completely available.

John 10:10 is the declaration of the intended life — and it is not conditional on marital status. I have come that they may have life, and have it to the full. The full life is not the married life, though marriage can be a dimension of the full life. It is the life that is fully inhabited — fully present, fully purposeful, fully receiving what the season provides and fully expressing what the season makes possible. The thief comes to steal and kill and destroy — and one of the things the thief most effectively steals from the single person is the present. The thief replaces the full presence in the current chapter with the anxious preoccupation with the chapter that has not yet arrived. And the person who has allowed this theft spends the most formative season of their life in a state of perpetual future-orientation that leaves the present impoverished and the future, when it arrives, built on the foundation of a person who never learned to be fully present.

Colossians 2:10 gives us the theological ground of the whole life: in Christ you have been brought to fullness. The Greek word is pleroo — to fill, to make complete, to bring to the full measure of what was intended. In Christ, you are already brought to fullness. Not on your way to fullness pending the arrival of certain conditions. Already there — in Him, by virtue of union with Him, in the present tense of the verse. The completeness is not the completeness that marriage adds. It is the completeness that Christ provides. Marriage is a beautiful covenant and a profound gift — but it is not the completion of the person who receives it. The person in Christ is already complete. The marriage adds the specific joys and challenges and depths of the covenant relationship — but it adds to a person who was already whole, not to a person who was incomplete.

2 Peter 1:3 gives us the provision that makes the whole and holy life possible in every season: His divine power has given us everything we need for a godly life through our knowledge of Him who called us by His own glory and goodness. Everything we need. Not everything we need once the marriage arrives. Not everything we need for the chapter we prefer. Everything we need for the godly life — in the current chapter, in the current season, with the current challenges and the current graces. The provision is complete for the life that is currently being lived.

The whole and holy life is the life that receives this provision — that does not wait for a different season to begin living fully, does not defer the full expression of the calling until the conditions are more favourable, does not treat the present chapter as less than what it is. It is the life of the person who has received the purpose behind their forming, inhabited the dignity of the waiting season, lived from the identity that precedes every relational status, guarded the spring from which everything flows, activated the calling that does not wait for the altar, brought the desire honestly to God in the open hand — and who, having done all of these things, is simply, fully, wholly alive in the season they are in.

That is the life the week has been building toward. Not the life that begins when marriage arrives. The life that is fully alive right now — created on purpose, dignified in the waiting, secure in identity, guarded in heart, activated in calling, honest in desire, and complete in Christ. This is the whole and holy life. It is available today. In this season. In this chapter. Fully.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I close this week with the declaration that the full life — the life to the full of John 10:10 — is available to me today, in this season, in this chapter, without the conditions that I have sometimes treated as prerequisites. I am complete in Christ. Everything I need for a godly life has already been given. The provision is sufficient for the life I am currently living, not only for the life I am anticipating.

Let me be fully alive today. Not performing the fullness. Not projecting it for the benefit of the people who are watching. Genuinely, quietly, unhurriedly fully alive — in the purpose behind my forming, the dignity of my season, the security of my identity, the guarded condition of my heart, the activated state of my calling, and the open-handed holding of my desires.

The full life is not ahead of me. It is here. In Christ. Right now. Let me live it.

## Daily Practices

- Read John 10:7–15 in full — the complete good shepherd passage in which the life to the full appears. Note the contrast between the thief and the shepherd: the thief steals, kills, destroys; the shepherd gives life abundantly. Ask: in what specific ways has the thief stolen from my present season — what abundance has been taken by the anxiety of the future-orientation or the grief of the comparison? And what would it look like to receive the abundant life specifically in those areas?
- Read Colossians 2:6–15 — the complete passage on fullness in Christ. Note verse 6: just as you received Christ Jesus as Lord, continue to live your lives in him, rooted and built up in him. The continuing to live in Him is the daily, present-tense inhabiting of the completeness that was received at conversion. Ask: am I continuing to live in Him — rooted and built up — or have I been living at a distance from the completeness that He provides?
- Review the entire week — created on purpose, singleness as a season not a sentence, identity before relationship, guarding the heart, called before married, trusting God with the desire, and the whole and holy life. Write one sentence for each day capturing what it produced in you. Seven sentences. This is your testimony of the week and of the season.
- Read 2 Peter 1:3–8 in full — the complete provision passage and the qualities that the provision is given to produce: faith, goodness, knowledge, self-control, perseverance, godliness, mutual affection, love. Ask: which of these qualities is most present in my life right now as the fruit of the current season? And which is the one the current season is most specifically forming? Let both answers be the closing prayer of the week.
