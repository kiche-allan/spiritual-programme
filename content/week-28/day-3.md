## Revelation

How do you make major decisions? When the crossroads arrives — the career choice, the relationship question, the city to move to, the door to walk through or close — where do you go first? The temptation for most people, including most believers, is to begin with the horizontal: the friend with the good track record of advice, the family member whose wisdom you trust, the online community that has relevant experience, the consensus of the people whose opinions you value.

None of these are wrong. Wisdom gathered from other people is legitimate and often essential. Proverbs is full of the value of many counsellors. But there is a specific order that the believer is called to — and the Word of God belongs at the beginning of the decision-making process, not at the end of it as the confirmation of a conclusion already reached by other means.

Psalm 119:105 gives us the image that defines the relationship between the Word and the path: a lamp for my feet, a light on my path. The lamp image is worth sitting with carefully because it specifies something about the nature of the guidance the Word provides. A lamp for the feet does not illuminate the entire road — it does not give you a satellite view of the whole journey, the complete map from where you are to where you are going, the comprehensive picture of every turn and every obstacle between here and the destination. It gives you enough light for the next step. And then the next. And then the next.

This is the specific way the Word functions as guidance — not as a comprehensive plan delivered in advance but as the sufficient light for the present step. The person who refuses to move until they have the complete map will never move. The person who opens the Word and takes the next lit step will eventually arrive at the destination one faithful step at a time.

The reason the Word comes before the friend, before the family, before the online consensus, is not that those sources have nothing to offer. It is that the Word alone carries the specific authority to speak into your specific life from the specific knowledge of the One who designed you, called you, and knows the end from the beginning. Your friend gives you their best assessment from their limited vantage point. God speaks from omniscience into the particular.

Matthew 4:4 gives us Jesus's own statement of the primacy of the Word: man shall not live on bread alone but on every word that comes from the mouth of God. The word that comes from the mouth of God is not merely information or inspiration. It is sustenance — the specific nourishment without which the life that God designed cannot be properly lived. The person who makes decisions without the Word is the person making decisions on insufficient nutrition — competent perhaps, but missing the specific input that the decision actually requires.

Open the Word before you open the group chat. Let the lamp illuminate the next step before you ask anyone else which direction to go. And then — with the Word as your compass — gather the wisdom of the counsellors who can help you walk in the direction the light has already indicated.

## Morning Prayer

Father, before I open any other input today — before the group chat, before the timeline, before the opinions of the people whose voices I value — I open Your Word. I receive it today as the lamp for my feet and the light for my path. I do not need the complete map. I need enough light for the next step. Give me that light from Your Word today.

Speak into the specific decisions I am carrying. The crossroads I am standing at. The door I am trying to read. I am not starting with the horizontal today. I am starting with You — with the Word that is a lamp, the truth that sanctifies, the bread that sustains the life You designed. Speak, Lord. I am reading.

## Daily Practices

- Read Psalm 119:97–112 in full — the complete section surrounding verse 105. Note how many times the psalmist describes the Word as the source of wisdom, understanding, and guidance. Ask: what is my current relationship with the Word — is it the first place I go or the last confirmation I seek?
- Identify the specific major decision you are currently carrying and spend fifteen minutes in the Word specifically looking for light on it — not proof-texting, not looking for a verse that confirms what you already want to do, but genuinely opening the Scripture and asking God to speak into the decision from His word. Write what you find.
- Journal: "When I face a major decision, what is my actual process — where do I go first, second, third? How would my decision-making process change if the Word was genuinely the lamp I consulted before every other light? What has a previous decision cost me because I consulted everything else before the Word?" Write the honest audit.
- Read 2 Timothy 3:16–17 — all Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work. Note the word thoroughly — the Word does not partially equip. It thoroughly equips. Ask: what decision am I trying to make with partial equipment that the Word could thoroughly equip me for?
