## Revelation

Notice what the Samaritan does that the story did not require him to do. He bandages the wounds — the story required that. He puts the man on his own donkey — the story required that. He brings him to an inn — the story required that. But then he stays the night to care for him, and when he leaves in the morning he pays the innkeeper for the room, and then he commits to an open-ended financial responsibility: when I return, I will reimburse you for any extra expense. He does not know what that cost will be. He does not cap it. He simply guarantees it. This is purpose in action — not the minimum necessary, not the calculated response, but the generous overflow of a person who has found the intersection of their capacity and someone else's need and decided to stay there.

Myles Munroe said your purpose is never entirely about you. This is one of the most important correctives to the way purpose is typically discussed in motivational culture, which tends to frame it in terms of personal fulfilment, self-actualisation, and individual achievement. Biblical purpose has an entirely different centre of gravity. It is always outward-facing. It is always in service of something beyond yourself. Your gifts, your passions, your capacities — they are not given for your private enjoyment. They are given for the world's benefit. The Samaritan's compassion was not self-directed. It was deployed entirely in the service of a stranger who had no way to repay it and no connection to him whatsoever.

Matthew 20:26–28 gives us Jesus's own definition of greatness in the kingdom: the greatest is the servant of all. This is not humility as self-deprecation. This is the recognition that the highest expression of a human life is to pour itself out for the flourishing of others. Jesus frames this not as a spiritual principle but as the literal shape of His own existence — the Son of Man did not come to be served but to serve, and to give His life as a ransom for many. Your purpose, when you find it, will always carry this same directional quality. It will always be pointing away from yourself and toward the person on the road who needs what only you were made to give.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I confess that I have sometimes pursued purpose as a private project — something to be discovered for my own clarity, my own peace, my own sense of meaning. Today Your Word reorients me. Purpose is not a destination I arrive at for my own satisfaction. It is a direction I walk in, and the direction is always toward someone else's need.

Show me today where my specific capacities — the things You placed in me before I was born — are needed by someone in my actual life. Not in the abstract, not in some future season when I have figured everything out, but today. There is a person on a road somewhere in my world this week. Give me the Samaritan's instinct to see them and the Samaritan's generosity to stay longer than is strictly necessary.

And where I have been calculating the cost of giving before I have even begun to give, free me from that. The Samaritan did not know what the total bill would be. He committed anyway. Let me commit with the same open-handedness, trusting that the One who embedded my purpose in me will resource the expression of it.

## Daily Practices

- Reread Luke 10:33–37 specifically looking for every act of the Samaritan that went beyond the minimum. Count them. Then ask: where in my own expression of purpose am I stopping at the minimum when I have capacity for more?
- Journal: "If my purpose is never entirely about me, what problem in the world does my specific set of gifts and passions equip me to solve? What does the world around me need that I am uniquely placed to provide?"
- Read Acts 9:36–42 — the story of Dorcas, a woman whose purpose was expressed in making garments for widows. It is one of the most ordinary-sounding purposes in Scripture, and it was so significant that Peter raised her from the dead. Let it challenge any idea that purpose must be dramatic to matter.
- Identify the most generous act of service you are currently capable of performing for someone in your community. Not the comfortable act — the generous one. Do it this week.
