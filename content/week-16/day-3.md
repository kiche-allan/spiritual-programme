## Revelation

Isaiah 9:6 is one of the most well-known prophecies in the entire Bible — read every Christmas, sung in Handel's Messiah, printed on cards and banners across the church year. And yet the name that ends the list is the one most often received as background music rather than as the specific, urgent, personally relevant title it actually is. Wonderful Counsellor. Mighty God. Everlasting Father. And then — Prince of Peace.

Not the Principle of Peace. Not the Promoter of Peace. The Prince — the one who rules over it, who embodies it, who brings it with Him wherever He goes, whose very presence is the substance of what the peace is made of. When the Prince of Peace arrives in a situation, He does not bring peace as a secondary gift. He is the peace. His presence and the peace are not two separate things. They are the same thing.

This changes the nature of the prayer for peace. Most people pray for peace as though it is a state of affairs to be achieved — a set of circumstances in which the anxiety-producing things have been resolved and the trouble has passed. This is the world's version of peace — the peace that the world gives, which Jesus specifically distinguishes from His own in John 14:27. The world's peace is circumstantial. It requires the storm to stop before it can be experienced. Christ's peace is personal — it is the presence of a Person who is greater than the storm, who met the disciples on the water in the middle of the storm rather than waiting on the shore until it was over.

My heart a storm, the song begins. And then — the Prince of Peace came bursting through the wind. Not tip-toeing in once the conditions were calm. Bursting — through the very thing that was causing the distress. The peace does not wait for the storm to be suitable for its arrival. It arrives in the storm, through the storm, while the storm is still fully present and fully active.

Philippians 4:7 gives us the description of what this peace actually does when it arrives: it guards your hearts and your minds. The Greek word for guard is phroureo — a military term for a garrison standing watch, protecting a city from external attack. The peace of God is not a feeling of calm. It is a garrison. A stationed force that stands between your heart and the fear that is trying to gain entry. It guards — actively, continuously, militarily — even when the circumstances that are producing the fear remain unchanged outside the walls.

The violent sky held its breath. Not because the storm ended. Because the Prince arrived — and the storm, like the sea in Mark 4, recognised the authority of the One who had entered it and paused in the presence of Someone greater than itself.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I receive today the full weight of the title Isaiah 9:6 gives Your Son: Prince of Peace. Not the principle of peace. Not the distant source of peace. The Prince — the One who rules over it, who carries it with Him, whose presence in any situation is itself the peace the situation needs.

I bring You today the specific storms I am in — the ones inside my heart more than the ones around me, the violent sky of my circumstances, the night that has not yet held its breath. I do not ask You to resolve the storm before You arrive. I ask You to arrive as You always have — through the wind, into the middle of it, meeting me there rather than waiting for conditions to be more suitable.

Let my eyes find Yours today. In the middle of the storm, before it ends, while it is still fully present. Let the peace that guards my heart and mind be the garrison that stands watch while the circumstances remain unresolved outside the walls.

Prince of Peace — come.

## Daily Practices

- Read Isaiah 9:1–7 in full — the full context of the prophecy. Note that it is spoken into darkness — the people walking in darkness have seen a great light. The Prince of Peace is promised into a situation of genuine darkness and oppression, not into already-peaceful circumstances. Ask: what does this say about when the Prince of Peace is most relevant to your situation?
- Read Mark 4:35–41 — Jesus calming the storm. Note that He was asleep in the boat — present in the storm, not absent from it — before He spoke to it. Ask: what does the fact that He was already in the boat before the storm intensified say about His presence in your current situation?
- Journal: "What is the storm I am most in right now — the one inside me more than the one around me? What would it look like for my eyes to find His in the middle of it, rather than waiting for the storm to resolve before I look up?"
- Sing or listen to Prince of Peace by Hillsong United today as an act of prayer rather than entertainment. Let the lyrics be the honest expression of where you are and what you are asking for.
