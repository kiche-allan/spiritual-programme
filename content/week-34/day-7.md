## Revelation

What is the last prayer in the Bible?

It isn't a prayer for money, healing or a breakthrough. It's three words: "Come, Lord Jesus!" After sixty-six books of history, law, poetry, prophecy and letters, the Bible ends with a longing. Jesus says, "Surely I am coming soon," and John answers, "Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!"

Look at the week we've walked through. We began with a clean heart, because the Spirit is grieved by what we hide. We went to make things right with the people we've hurt, because worship can't sit on top of a broken relationship. We listened to the alarm and refused to grow deaf to it. We checked the oil in our own lamps, remembered that we are children of the day, and asked what faithful waiting looks like. All of that is preparation for one moment.

But preparation isn't meant to make us anxious. It's meant to make us hopeful. Paul calls Christ's return "our blessed hope." Not a dreaded appointment, but a blessed hope: "the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ." If you belong to Him, the day you've been getting ready for is the day you've been longing for.

Think about what that day means. John saw it: "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more, neither shall there be mourning, nor crying, nor pain anymore, for the former things have passed away." No more funerals. No more hospital corridors. No more alarms on a monitor. No more sitting beside a bed, wondering what the next number will be. For anyone who has waited and wept, those words aren't poetry. They are a promise.

So this Sunday, as you gather with God's people, pray the prayer that closes the Bible. Not as a way to escape your problems, but because you love Him and want to see Him. The early believers had a word for it: Maranatha, "Our Lord, come." Say it today, from your heart.

And then go back to Monday faithful, forgiven, reconciled and awake, with the lamp full and the door ready. Come, Lord Jesus.

## Morning Prayer

Lord Jesus, I thank You for this week of looking at my own heart. Cleanse me, keep me awake, and keep me faithful. But more than that, I thank You that You are coming. I long to see Your face, and I long for the day when every tear is wiped away. Amen. Come, Lord Jesus.

In Jesus' name, Amen.

## Daily Practices

- Read Revelation 21:1–5 and 22:12–21 aloud. Notice how the Bible ends with a promise and a prayer.
- Journal: "What am I most looking forward to on the day He comes?" Write it down, then thank Him for it.
- Pray "Maranatha, come, Lord Jesus" at the start and the end of today. Encourage one person with the promise of Revelation 21:4.
