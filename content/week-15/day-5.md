## Revelation

The faith established on Thursday — the steady walk forward even without seeing the whole path — produces, over time, a particular kind of visible evidence. Galatians 5:22–23 calls this evidence fruit, and the word choice is deliberate and instructive. Fruit is not manufactured through willpower or produced through effort in the way a product is manufactured on a production line. Fruit grows. It is the natural, organic result of a plant that is healthy, properly rooted, and receiving what it needs to thrive. The nine qualities Paul lists — love, joy, peace, forbearance, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control — are not a checklist of behaviours to perform through gritted-teeth discipline. They are the natural outgrowth of a life that is genuinely connected to the source.

John 15:4–5 identifies that source explicitly: remain in me, as I also remain in you. No branch can bear fruit by itself; it must remain in the vine. This single image reframes the entire pursuit of Christian character. The branch does not strain to produce grapes through effort. The branch simply remains attached to the vine, and the vine's life flows through it, and the grapes appear as the natural consequence of that connection. Apart from me, Jesus says in the following verse, you can do nothing. This is not an exaggeration. It is a precise statement about the relationship between effort and fruit. Disconnected effort, however sincere, cannot produce what only abiding in the vine can produce.

This does not mean the cultivation of fruit requires no participation from the believer. A branch can be pruned, watered, exposed to light, or neglected — all of which affect how much fruit it bears, even though the branch itself does not manufacture the fruit through its own effort. The practical disciplines of prayer, Scripture, worship, and community are the equivalent of remaining well-positioned to receive what the vine is providing. They do not produce the fruit directly. They position the believer to receive the life that produces it.

Matthew 7:16 gives the practical test: by their fruit you will recognise them. The fruit of the Spirit is not primarily a private, internal experience — though it certainly includes internal transformation. It is visible. It is recognisable. The love, joy, peace, patience, and self-control that grow from a life rooted in Christ become evident to the people around that life, not because the person is performing virtue for an audience, but because what is genuinely growing inside eventually becomes visible outside.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today not asking for the strength to manufacture love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control through my own effort. I ask instead to remain in You — to stay connected to the vine in such a way that this fruit grows naturally, the way fruit always grows, as the consequence of health and connection rather than the product of strain.

Show me today what it looks like to remain — in prayer, in Your Word, in worship, in community with other believers. Let these be the practices that position me to receive what only You can produce in me, rather than substitutes for the connection itself.

Let the fruit growing in me today be visible to the people around me — not because I am performing virtue, but because what is genuinely growing in connection with You has naturally become evident.

## Daily Practices

- Read John 15:1–8 in full — the entire vine and branches passage. Note the specific mention of pruning in verse 2. Ask: is there anything in my life right now that feels like pruning — a removal or a difficulty — that might actually be God's way of helping me bear more fruit rather than less?
- Read through the nine qualities listed in Galatians 5:22–23 slowly, one at a time. For each one, ask honestly: is this currently growing in me, or is it largely absent? Do not judge yourself for the honest answer — simply notice it.
- Journal: "What does 'remaining in the vine' practically look like for me this week? What specific habits of prayer, Scripture, worship, or community would help me stay more consistently connected to the source?"
- Choose one quality from the fruit of the Spirit that you most want to see grow in you. Rather than trying to produce it directly through effort today, spend ten minutes simply abiding — in prayer, in worship, in stillness before God — and trust the growth to the vine rather than to your own striving.
