## Revelation

The waiting season is one of the most spiritually formative and most spiritually dangerous seasons available to a believer. Formative because it is in the waiting — not the receiving — that the deepest character is built, the deepest roots grow, and the deepest dependence on God is established. Dangerous because the waiting season, if it is not navigated with intentionality, can quietly erode the faith it was meant to deepen — through discouragement, through the subtle conclusion that God has forgotten, through the drift that happens when the urgency of crisis has passed and the discipline of the comfortable season has not yet been established.

Four practices sustain faith through the waiting season — not as a formula that makes the waiting shorter, but as the specific postures that keep faith alive and growing while the wait continues.

The first practice is looking for the beautiful things in the quiet seasons. God is still at work in both the quiet and the unseen moments — and the person who has not developed the eye to see His work in the ordinary will miss most of what He is doing. The sunrise that was there every morning while the prayer was going unanswered. The friendship that deepened in the season when the expected breakthrough had not yet arrived. The character that was forming in the invisible, underground way that roots form — slowly, without spectacle, in the direction of what is most needed for what is coming. The beautiful things are there. They require a trained eye to find them — the eye that has decided to look for evidence of God's activity rather than waiting for the activity to be dramatic enough to be unmissable.

The second practice is remembering that God's timing can be trusted. His delays are never without purpose. Abraham waited twenty-five years for Isaac. The delay was not a failure of the promise. It was the formation of the man who would become the father of faith — whose long wait produced the very trust that was credited to him as righteousness. Joseph waited thirteen years. David waited approximately fifteen years between his anointing and his coronation. In every case, the delay was the preparation — the shaping of the person who would need to be a specific kind of person to inhabit the specific promise that was coming. God's delays are not gaps in His attention. They are the active workings of a wisdom that sees the end from the beginning and calibrates the timing to what the person needs to be ready.

The third practice is staying faithful in the small, everyday things God has placed before you. Luke 16:10 — whoever can be trusted with very little can also be trusted with much — is the principle that governs the waiting season. The small, unglamorous, unrewarded faithfulness of the present is the preparation for the expanded responsibility of the future. The person who is faithful in obscurity is the person God can trust with visibility. The person who tends what is small with the same diligence they would bring to what is significant is the person whose character has been formed for what is significant when it arrives.

The fourth practice is doing what the Holy Spirit is leading you to do — even imperfectly. Even if you are wrong, by His grace He will course correct and honour the faith in Him. This is one of the most liberating truths available in a waiting season: God is not looking for the perfectly executed obedience of a person who waited until they were completely certain before they moved. He is looking for the faith-motivated step of a person who moved in the direction the Spirit seemed to be pointing, trusting that the same grace that called them will correct and redirect them if the specific step was misread. The faith that never acts for fear of getting it wrong is not mature faith. It is paralysis dressed as caution.
