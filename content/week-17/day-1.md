## Revelation

There is a particular kind of disorientation that arrives when multiple crises compound simultaneously — when the personal difficulty is happening at the same time as the national instability, when the body is failing at the same time as the finances are strained, when the family is fractured at the same time as the spiritual warfare is most active. In those seasons, the world feels genuinely out of control — not as a metaphor but as a lived, daily, visceral experience of a reality that no one seems to be governing, of a ship that has lost its captain, of a situation that is beyond the jurisdiction of any authority capable of addressing it.

Psalm 93:1 does not address this feeling by explaining it away. It answers it with a declaration: the Lord reigns. Present tense. Active. The reign is not historical — God once reigned, in a previous era when things were more orderly. It is not future — God will reign again when the chaos has been resolved. It is now. The Lord reigns in the middle of the shaking. He is robed in majesty and armed with strength precisely when everything visible suggests that neither majesty nor strength are present. And then the most important line: your throne was established long ago; you are from all eternity. The throne is not a recent development that the current crisis might threaten. It was established before the crisis existed. Before the world that is currently shaking was made, the throne was already there.

Isaiah 6:1 gives us the most important detail about when this vision came: in the year that King Uzziah died. Uzziah had been king for fifty-two years — the longest and most stable reign in Judah's recent history. His death represented the collapse of the political stability that an entire generation had grown up within. It was the year when the human throne that had provided order and security was suddenly gone. And it was precisely in that year — not before it, not after it had been resolved, but in it — that Isaiah saw the Lord, high and exalted, seated on a throne. The human throne had emptied. The divine throne was fully occupied. And the train of His robe filled the temple — there was no shortage of His presence, no diminishment of His glory, no evidence that the death of the earthly king had in any way affected the heavenly One.

Revelation 4 gives us the eternal perspective on every earthly crisis. John is writing from Patmos — exiled, the church under persecution, the Roman Empire at the height of its hostility toward believers. And the first thing the Spirit shows him is a throne in heaven with someone sitting on it. Not an empty throne. Not a throne being contested. A throne with Someone on it — surrounded by living creatures crying holy, holy, holy, twenty-four elders casting their crowns, the sea of glass before it, the rainbow above it. Whatever is happening on earth, this is what is happening in heaven. The Someone on the throne has not moved. He has not been distracted by the persecution or alarmed by the empire. He is seated — the posture of completed authority, of a ruler who is not scrambling but reigning.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I come to You today in the middle of everything that feels out of control — the personal crises, the unanswered questions, the situations that no human hand seems capable of governing. And I receive today the truth that has not changed regardless of how the circumstances feel: You are still enthroned. Your throne was established before any of this began. You are from all eternity, and the shaking of my world has not moved You one inch from the throne You have occupied since before time began.

Let the vision of Isaiah 6 be mine today — let me see, in the year of whatever is dying around me, the Lord high and exalted, seated, the train of His robe filling the temple. Let the Revelation 4 perspective reframe everything I am looking at — whatever is happening on earth, Someone is on the throne in heaven, and He is not scrambling.

You reign today. That is enough.

## Daily Practices

- Read Psalm 93 in full — it is only five verses, but read each one slowly. Note the specific images of instability — the seas lifting their voice, the pounding of the waves — and the specific contrast: mightier than the thunder of the great waters, mightier than the breakers of the sea, the Lord on high is mighty. Ask: what are the pounding waves in my current situation, and what does it mean that He is mightier than all of them?
- Read Isaiah 6:1–8 in full. Note that Isaiah's encounter with the enthroned God produces two things in sequence: first, the devastating recognition of his own unworthiness — woe to me, I am ruined — and then the cleansing, and then the commissioning. The vision of God enthroned does not make Isaiah comfortable. It makes him usable. Ask: what would a genuine vision of God enthroned do to my current sense of my own situation?
- Journal: "What specifically feels out of control in my life right now? For each thing I name, I will say aloud: the Lord reigns over this. Not as a denial of the difficulty but as a declaration of who is above it." Write the list and speak the declaration over each item.
- Read Revelation 4 in full. Let the imagery be vivid — the colours, the creatures, the elders, the thunder, the sea of glass. This is not a distant theological construct. This is the present, ongoing reality of what is happening in heaven right now, simultaneously with everything happening on earth. Let it reorient you.
