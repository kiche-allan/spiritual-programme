## Revelation

What do you say when someone gives you something you can never repay?

Paul reaches that point at the end of 2 Corinthians 9, after talking about giving and generosity. Suddenly he stops and says, "Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift!" The word means beyond description. Paul, one of the finest writers who ever lived, runs out of words.

What is the gift? Jesus Himself. The Son of God left heaven, took on flesh, carried our sin to the cross, rose from the dead, and offers us forgiveness and eternal life. Not as wages, but as a gift. You can't earn it, and you can't repay it. All you can do is receive it and say thanks.

Everything else we've thanked God for this week, the answered prayer, the rain, the job, the people, is a gift that rests on this one. If you had nothing else, you would still have reason to give thanks for as long as you live.

The psalmist asks a question I love: "What shall I return to the Lord for all his goodness to me?" He answers in the next verse: "I will lift up the cup of salvation and call on the name of the Lord." He'll respond with praise, with vows kept, with thanks given in front of God's people.

Paul says it like this to the Colossians: "just as you received Christ Jesus as Lord, continue to live your lives in him, rooted and built up in him, strengthened in the faith as you were taught, and overflowing with thankfulness." Overflowing. Not a drip. The kind of gratitude that spills over into how you give, how you serve, how you forgive and how you speak.

Notice also that thanksgiving belongs in community. As you gather with God's people today, don't come only to receive. Come to give thanks. Sing like you mean it. Give your thanksgiving offering as a response, not an obligation. Tell somebody what God did for you this week.

Look back over the week: the one who came back, the "yet" of Habakkuk, the love that saved us from wrath, a gate opened with thanks, thanks in the hard places, a list of benefits, and now the gift beyond words. Together they make a way of life.

So today, before you leave the service or close your Bible, finish with one sentence: "Thank You, Lord, for Jesus." Say it slowly. Mean every word. Then carry it into Monday.

## Morning Prayer

Father, thank You for Jesus, the gift beyond words. I can't repay You, so I receive Him again with open hands. Let my gratitude overflow into how I give, how I serve and how I speak. As I gather with Your people today, teach me to give thanks together, and carry this into the week ahead.

## Daily Practices

- Read 2 Corinthians 9:6–15 and Colossians 2:6–7. Notice how thanksgiving and generosity belong together.
- Journal: "What is one thing I will do this coming week to show my thanks, in giving, serving, or speaking?" Write it down and commit to it.
- Tell one person at church today what God did for you this week. Finish with: "Thank You, Lord, for Jesus."
