## Revelation

The question is one of the most common objections to Christian faith, and it deserves a serious answer rather than an embarrassed deflection: how can a God who is love send people to hell? The apparent contradiction sits at the intersection of two things the Bible insists on simultaneously — the absolute love of God and the absolute justice of God — and most people have been given only half of the picture.

The first thing to say is what John 3:17 says: God did not send His Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through Him. Hell was not God's plan A. It was not designed for human beings. Matthew 25:41 says the eternal fire was prepared for the devil and his angels — not for people. The entire trajectory of the gospel is God doing everything possible to ensure that no human being needs to go there. The incarnation. The cross. The resurrection. The sending of the Spirit. The existence of the church. The continued preaching of the gospel. All of it is God's relentless, costly, personal intervention to make hell unnecessary for every human being who will receive what He is offering. 2 Peter 3:9 — He is patient with you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance. This is not the picture of a God who is looking for reasons to send people to hell. It is the picture of a God who is doing everything possible to prevent it.

The second thing to say is this: hell is not primarily something God does to people. It is the final confirmation of something people have chosen for themselves. C.S. Lewis put it most memorably: in the end, there are only two kinds of people — those who say to God, your will be done, and those to whom God says, your will be done. Hell is the eternal establishment of a choice that was made in time — the choice to live without God, to refuse the rescue He offered, to insist on the self-sovereignty that does not want Him. Romans 1:18–20 says that God's eternal power and divine nature are clearly seen in creation, so that people are without excuse. The rejection of God is not a simple ignorance. It is a deliberate turning away from a light that was visible.

The love and the justice of God are not in tension. They are the same thing viewed from different angles. A God who loved human beings enough to die for them but then shrugged at the rejection of that death would not be a God worth worshipping. The same love that drove the cross drives the judgment — the love that takes human choice with ultimate seriousness, that refuses to override the freedom He gave, that honours the decision even when the decision is devastating. Hell is the proof that God loves human beings enough to let them be what they choose to be forever.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I sit today with the hardest question about You — and I bring it to You honestly rather than pretending it is not there. How can a loving God send people to hell? I receive Your answer this morning: You do not send them. You do everything possible to ensure they do not go. You gave Your Son. You sent Your Spirit. You are patient, not wanting any to perish.

And I receive the other half of the answer: that human freedom is real, that choice has weight, that You take the decisions of the people You made with the ultimate seriousness of a God who refuses to override what He gave. The same love that drove the cross drives the judgment.

Let this understanding make me more grateful, not more comfortable. I am not in hell because Someone loved me enough to pursue me before I had the sense to turn toward Him. Let me live with that gratitude pressing outward toward every person I know who has not yet turned.

## Daily Practices

- Read Romans 1:18–32 in full — Paul's description of the progressive consequences of rejecting the knowledge of God that is available in creation. Note that the judgment described is largely God giving people over to what they have chosen rather than imposing something alien. Ask: what does this say about the nature of hell?
- Read C.S. Lewis's chapter on hell in The Problem of Pain if you have access to it. His argument that the doors of hell are locked from the inside is one of the most important theological contributions to this question in the twentieth century. If you do not have the book, journal on the idea itself — what does it mean that hell is ultimately a choice rather than an imposition?
- Journal: "If hell is the final confirmation of a choice made in time, what does that say about the urgency of the choices being made right now — by me and by the people I love who do not yet know Jesus?" Write what surfaces honestly.
- Pray specifically for one person in your life who has not yet turned to Christ. Not a general prayer — a specific, named, informed intercession for a specific person. The God who is not willing that any should perish is already at work in their life. Ask to participate in that work.
