## Revelation

Have you ever wondered how God teaches you to love people?

Not the people who make it easy — loving them requires very little of you. The people who are warm and affirming and uncomplicated in their affection for you do not stretch you toward anything you were not already capable of. They comfort you. They confirm you. They are a gift. But they are not the curriculum.

There is a word in 1 Thessalonians 4:9 that appears exactly once in the entire New Testament — theodidaktoi. Taught by God. Paul says the Thessalonian believers have been theodidaktoi — directly, personally, divinely instructed in the art of loving one another. Not taught by a sermon, not taught by a book, not taught by watching someone else do it well. Taught by God. And the question worth pressing is: how does God teach this? Through what classroom does theodidaktoi happen?

Through the people you find most challenging.

This is God's preferred classroom for the lesson of love — not the comfortable relationships that require nothing from you, but the difficult ones that require everything. The person who looks at you sideways. The one you are not quite on the same level with socially or culturally or temperamentally. The one who drives a different kind of car, comes from a different part of the world, holds different opinions about things you care deeply about, and generally manages to make you feel the friction of difference every time your paths cross. That person — specifically that person — is the instrument God has chosen for your theological education in love.

This is why Proverbs 27:17 says iron sharpens iron and one person sharpens another — because the sharpening is not pleasant. It produces heat and friction and the loss of material from both surfaces. Sharpening is not the same as polishing. Polishing only makes the surface smoother. Sharpening changes the edge. And God is far more interested in the edge of your character than in the smoothness of your social experience.

God keeps creating new scenarios in your life for this specific purpose — to get you to the place where you look more like Jesus in the exact dimensions where you currently look least like Him. And the scenarios He chooses are not random. The spouse who has an edge you did not notice during the courtship. The neighbour who plays music at exactly the wrong time. The colleague who takes credit for work you did. The person in your small group who asks the questions that derail the discussion every single week. The family member at Christmas who finds the exact nerve that no one else can locate. None of these are accidents. They are enrolled in the same programme — God's curriculum for producing in you the specific quality of love that the easy relationships could never have grown.

James 1:2–3 tells us to consider it pure joy when we face trials of many kinds, because the testing of faith produces perseverance. The trials James has in mind are not only the dramatic ones — the health crisis, the financial collapse, the sudden loss. They include the sustained, low-grade trial of the person who just will not stop being themselves in a way that costs you something. That person is testing your faith in the God who commanded you to love without exception — and the test, faithfully endured, produces something in you that the comfortable life never could.

So the next time someone frustrates you — the next time the friction rises and the distance feels like the only reasonable option — ask yourself: what is God teaching me through this person? Because He is a good teacher. He does not design a curriculum that does not produce results. And He has never wasted a lesson.

## Morning Prayer

Father, I confess that I have been treating the difficult people in my life as problems to manage rather than as teachers You have specifically enrolled in my formation. Open my eyes today to the theodidaktoi — the lessons You are teaching me directly through the specific friction of the specific relationships I would have avoided if I had designed my own curriculum. Give me the humility to be a student of what You are teaching rather than a critic of the method. The people who sharpen me are not my enemies. They are Your instruments. Let me receive them as such today.

## Daily Practices

- Read 1 Thessalonians 4:1–12 in full — the complete passage on living to please God. Note that the instruction about love for one another comes in the middle of a passage about holiness and sexual purity — the connection is deliberate. Ask: how does the pursuit of holiness in my personal life relate to the quality of love I am able to give to the people around me?
- Read Romans 5:3–5 — we also glory in our sufferings, because we know that suffering produces perseverance, perseverance character, and character hope. Note the chain: the difficult thing produces the valuable thing. Ask: what specifically is the difficult relationship in my life currently producing in my character — and what would it produce if I cooperated with it rather than resisted it?
- Journal: "Who is the specific person God is currently using as theodidaktoi in my life — the one whose presence or behaviour is teaching me something about myself and about love that the comfortable relationships could not teach? What specifically is the lesson? What would it look like to lean into the classroom rather than skip the class?" Write the honest reflection.
- Read Matthew 5:43–48 — love your enemies and pray for those who persecute you. Note that Jesus lifts the standard all the way to the person who is actively working against you — not merely the person who is mildly irritating. Ask: if the standard is love for enemies, how does that reframe the person who is merely difficult?
